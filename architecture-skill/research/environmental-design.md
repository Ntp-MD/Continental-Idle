# Research file 09 — Environmental design (climate → orientation → plan chain)

Scope: the part of environmental design that is a **spatial planning** problem — daylight reach, solar gain by orientation, shading, ventilation depth, acoustic separation, envelope compactness and the mechanical/duct consequences of all of them. Everything here must change room placement, room depth, aspect, façade allocation or circulation. Green technology that does not move a tile is out of scope by construction.

Host grid this file is written against: `1 tile = 0.5 m`, one 2D grid per floor, tile states ONLY `walkable | blocked | door`; a wall is one blocked tile; windows are not a tile state. There is no section, no heights, no sun-path, no climate data and no material. Every claim below is therefore converted into a **plan-geometry proxy** and the assumption that makes it computable is declared.

Derived grid constants used throughout:
- 1 tile = 0.5 m; 2 tiles = 1.0 m; 4 tiles = 1 m²; 6 tiles = 3.0 m.
- `façade tile` = a blocked boundary tile with a walkable tile on its inner face (façade exposure).
- `glazed façade tile` (metadata, not a tile state) = a façade tile the agent tags as carrying glazing; assumed 0.5 m wide × 1.5 m high = **0.75 m² of glass per glazed tile**.
- `vent tile` = a glazed façade tile additionally tagged openable; assumed openable area 0.5 m × 1.1 m = **0.55 m² per vent tile**.
- `aspect` of a room = the set of distinct compass directions of its façade tiles. `single-aspect` = 1 direction; `double-aspect` = 2 opposed directions; `corner` = 2 adjacent.
- Room depth in tiles `d` = Manhattan distance from the interior tile to its nearest glazed façade tile.

Declared section assumptions (the grid carries none of these; every rule that needs them re-states them, and a rule run without them is void):
- floor-to-floor 3.0 m = 6 tiles; clear height 2.7 m = 5.4 tiles.
- window head 2.1 m = 4.2 tiles; sill 0.9 m = 1.8 tiles; clear vision band 0.9–2.0 m.
- `head height` H = 2.1 m. Daylight depth multipliers become: 1.0 H = **4 tiles**; 1.5 H = **6 tiles**; 2.0 H = **8 tiles**; 2.5 H = **11 tiles**.
- Whole-floor depth caps (these are *maximum* values, so **tile conversion rounds DOWN** per the host's rounding rule — a maximum never exceeds the physical cap; a *minimum* rounds UP; see EN-03): 60 ft = 18.29 m = 36.58 tiles → **≤ 36 tiles**; cross-ventilation depth 45 ft = 13.72 m = 27.43 tiles → **≤ 27 tiles**; 4–5 × 2.7 m = 10.8–13.5 m = 21.6–27 tiles → **≤ 21–27 tiles**; single-sided ventilation depth 2 × 2.7 m = 5.4 m = 10.8 tiles → **≤ 10 tiles**.

## Rules

### EN-01 Declare the climate driver before you draw, and write down which chain step it inverts
- Rule: State the site's governing load as one of heating-dominated / cooling-dominated / humidity-dominated / mixed-temperate, and state the sun-path hemisphere. Every later rule in this file has a sign that flips with this declaration; no environmental rule may be applied while the declaration is blank.
- Evidence: The chain the deliverable specifies (Climate → Orientation → Envelope → Openings → Room placement → Circulation → Mechanical systems) is the standard passive-design ordering: government tropical-housing guidance prescribes a north–south long axis with an azimuth tolerance of 0–30° for tropical climates and compact (low surface-to-volume) forms for hot-dry and cold regions — i.e. the *same* lever (plan form) with opposite settings per climate. Natural ventilation guidance is likewise climate-bound: WBDG states cross ventilation is ineffective in humid climates and best in hot-dry or warm-temperate ones.
- Source: NZEB (The Energy and Resources Institute), *Form & Orientation* https://nzeb.in/knowledge-centre/passive-design/form-orientation/ — T1/T3; WBDG, *Natural Ventilation* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/natural-ventilation — T1.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: universal
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Emit a `climate_profile` record with fields `governing_load`, `hemisphere`, `winter_sun_low_summer_sun_high = true/false` (a declared assumption, since no sun angles are modelled) and `ventilation_viable = true/false`. Validation: reject any floor whose `façade_solar` allocation table (EN-02, EN-12) is populated while `climate_profile` is unset; report the count of EN-rules fired with a defaulted assumption.
- Exceptions / failure mode: The agent optimises for a climate it has not declared — typically maximising glazing "for daylight" on a cooling-dominated site, which adds a load, not a benefit. Symptom: the plan improves on all daylight proxies and worsens on every gain/acoustic proxy.

### EN-02 Set the long axis to face the usable sun, within a stated azimuth tolerance
- Rule: Orient the primary daylit/solar-hungry façade toward the equator, with a declared tolerance (≤15° off due south in the northern hemisphere is the federal guidance figure; ≤30° appears in UK daylight/sunlight practice and in tropical guidance). Tolerance is not slack: record the actual bearing of each façade run and the deviation, and re-derive which rooms lose when it is exceeded.
- Evidence: WBDG: "Deviation from due south should not exceed 15° in either direction for best solar access and ease of control", and "South-facing exposure maximizes benefit" (https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/daylighting). Anstey Horne's summary of the BRE daylight/sunlight guidance gives "Building façades should be oriented within ±30° of due south" for solar access. Peer-reviewed glare work finds the intermediate orientations worst: Karimoshaver & Derakhshan (2026) report south-east and south-west façades producing the highest discomfort glare (DGP > 0.35) while north-east / north-west give the better light/glare balance.
- Source: WBDG, *Daylighting* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/daylighting — T1; Anstey Horne, *Daylight and Sunlight: BRE Guidelines* https://www.ansteyhorne.co.uk/news/daylight-and-sunlight-bre-guidelines — T3; M. Karimoshaver & F. Derakhshan, "Window Orientation, Glare, and Visual Comfort in Offices", *Journal of Daylighting* 13:167 https://solarlits.com/jd/13-167 — T2.
- Class: STANDARD
- Scope: universal; binds hardest where solar gain is a design asset (cold, heating-dominated)
- Confidence: High that a stated tolerance exists and differs by guidance; Medium on which value to use (15° vs 30° — see Weak or contested)
- Grid translation: Tag every façade run with a bearing; compute `dev_from_equator` and `façade_tiles_by_direction` (N/E/S/W, 8-point allowed). Validation: (a) `Σ equator_band_tiles / total_façade_tiles ≥ 0.30` on heating-dominated floors, `≥ 0.25` on cooling-dominated ones (declared target, not a code figure); (b) any run with `dev > tolerance` is reported as a solar-access debt with the list of rooms starved by it; (c) count `corner` rooms sitting in the 15–30° glare band and log them as glare-risk rooms (EN-13).
- Exceptions / failure mode: Site geometry, views, heritage street grid or a planning-obstructed boundary override the ideal bearing. Then the tolerance is knowingly breached and the *façade allocation* (EN-05) is re-ranked to compensate — never silently left as "orientation doesn't matter".

### EN-03 Cap the floor-plate depth at the daylight limit or the ventilation limit, whichever binds first
- Rule: Fix the maximum floor depth in tiles from the two governing reach rules — daylight reach (WBDG: a floor depth of no more than 60 ft has been shown viable for daylighting) and cross-ventilation reach (WBDG: maximum width for cross ventilation estimated at 45 ft; CIBSE AM10 as summarised by NCC practice: 4–5 × ceiling height). If the plate exceeds both, the project is artificially lit and mechanically ventilated by definition, and that must be declared, not discovered.
- Evidence: WBDG Daylighting: "A floor depth of no more than 60 ft., 0 in. from south to north has been shown to be viable for daylighting"; WBDG Natural Ventilation: plan "maximum width … estimated at 45 ft" for cross ventilation. CCC Engineering's reading of NCC Part F6 with CIBSE AM10: single-sided ventilation depth "about 2 times the ceiling height" (≈5.4 m at 2.7 m) and cross ventilation "4–5 times the ceiling height" (≈11–13.5 m). WindowMaster's guidance gives single-sided ≈10 m. Steel Construction Institute's thermal-mass note (BRE Digest 454): deep plans "hinder air flow" and mass only helps where air can reach the exposed soffit.
- Source: WBDG, *Daylighting* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/daylighting — T1; WBDG, *Natural Ventilation* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/natural-ventilation — T1; CCC Engineering, *Natural Ventilation NCC 2025* https://cccengineering.com.au/design-memos/natural-ventilation-ncc-compliance/ — T3; WindowMaster, *Natural Ventilation Design Guidelines* https://www.windowmaster.com/expertise/natural-ventilation-and-mixed-mode-ventilation/natural-ventilation-design-guidelines/ — T3; Steel Construction Info, *Thermal mass for cooling* https://steelconstruction.info/topics/sustainability/thermal-mass/ — T3.
- Class: ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT
- Scope: all types; the cap is the design's *whole* depth, not a room's
- Confidence: High for the existence of the two reach limits; Medium for the specific metres (they are measured at different ceiling heights and for different climates)
- Grid translation: Compute `plate_depth_N_S` and `plate_depth_E_W` in tiles (bounding box of the floor's walkable set), and `plate_area_tiles`. Validation with the declared section — **these are maximum caps, so any metre-to-tile conversion of them rounds DOWN; a *minimum* in a rule would round UP; the file's rounding direction is stated here rather than silently truncated**: `plate_depth ≤ 36 tiles` (18.0 m, from WBDG's 60 ft = 18.29 m = 36.58 tiles rounded down — the earlier "37 tiles" over-shoot the cap by 0.5 m), `≤ 27 tiles` if any part of the floor claims natural ventilation (13.5 m, WBDG's 45 ft = 13.72 m = 27.43 tiles rounded down), `≤ 21 tiles` for the stricter 4 × reading (10.5 m; 4 × 2.7 m = 10.8 m = 21.6 tiles rounded down — the earlier "22 tiles" over-shoots the cap). Emit `lighting_regime ∈ {daylight-led, mixed, fully-artificial}` and `ventilation_regime ∈ {passive, hybrid, mechanical}` derived from those numbers; the mechanical claim then triggers EN-21 (riser and duct reservation) as a hard dependency.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Atrium, gallery, arena, plant floor and basements are legitimately deep — but then no room beyond the reach line may be tagged habitable/long-occupancy. Symptom: a 50-tile-deep plate whose central third is offices and bedrooms, silently credited with daylight because a façade tile exists somewhere on the boundary.

### EN-04 Apply one daylight depth multiplier per room type, publish it, and measure the daylit tile share
- Rule: Cap each room's depth from a *single named* daylight multiplier of the window head height, chosen per room type and stated in the output. The published multipliers disagree by a factor of 2.5, so choosing between them is a design decision the agent must make explicitly and defend, not an inherited default.
- Evidence: Four independent published rules exist for the same quantity. (i) IECC/ASHRAE 90.1 lineage, the code definition of the *sidelight daylight zone*: depth "up to 1.0 times the height from the floor to the top of the fenestration", extended laterally by up to 1.0 × head height and "up to 2 feet (610 mm)" beyond the opening; Title-24 acceptance text restates width = window + 0.5 × head height, depth = min(1.0 × head height, obstruction height). (ii) BRANZ passive-design guidance: "useful illumination to a depth of approximately 1.5 times the window head height". (iii) WBDG: "The depth of daylight penetration is about two and one-half times the distance between the top of a window and the sill." (iv) EN 17037 abandons depth multipliers entirely in favour of target illuminance: ≥300 lx median over ≥50 % of the reference plane, ≥100 lx over ≥95 %, for ≥50 % of daylight hours (≈2190 h), plane at 0.85 m.
- Source: UpCodes, *Sidelight Daylight Zone* https://up.codes/s/sidelight-daylight-zone — T1; Energy Code Ace, *NA7.6.1 Automatic Daylighting Control Acceptance* https://energycodeace.com/site/custom/public/reference-ace-2016/Documents/1324na761automaticdaylightingcontrolacceptance.htm — T1; BRANZ Level, *Passive design for daylight in a home* https://www.level.org.nz/passive-design/daylighting/ — T1; WBDG, *Daylighting* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/daylighting — T1; BS EN 17037:2018 targets as reported in the open-access practice study https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10773465/ — T2 (standard record https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/836e5b91-1eb0-4643-a2ba-7ca5a5988e64/en-17037-2018 — T1).
- Class: STANDARD
- Scope: every room claiming daylight; universal across types
- Confidence: High that these are the published values; Medium for any single one as a predictor of a real room's light
- Grid translation: Choose `d_daylight` per type and state it: default `d = 6 tiles` (1.5 × H) for long-occupancy rooms, `d = 4 tiles` (1.0 × H) for code-literal compliance checks, `d = 11 tiles` for double-aspect rooms. Then per room: `daylit_share = |{interior tiles : dist(tile, nearest glazed façade tile) ≤ d}| / |interior tiles|`. Validation: `daylit_share ≥ 0.6` for a room tagged habitable/work/ward/classroom; `≥ 0.8` if the room is billed as "daylit"; below threshold the room is re-tagged (corridor/store/plant) or the design is edited. Report the multiplier used next to every percentage, since the same plan scores 0.35 at 1.0 × H and 0.90 at 2.5 × H.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Light-coloured finishes, deep reveals, a opposite-building obstruction and internal partitions all break the proxy — the grid cannot see them. Symptom: the agent reports 95 % daylit shares for a plan with a 2-tile-deep reveal and no vision strip; the check is only meaningful when the same multiplier is used across design revisions.

### EN-05 Allocate façade tiles by room value, and publish the façade budget before the walls
- Rule: Treat façade length as a scarce, budgeted resource. Rank room types by their need for light and view, allocate glazed tiles against that ranking, and report every unallocated or misallocated tile. Do not let façade fall wherever the geometry happens to leave it.
- Evidence: Federal guidance makes the view/task link explicit: daylight design targets "a minimum of 50 footcandles on an imaginary desk-height horizontal work surface" and requires glare control so that direct sun is not admitted "on task surfaces or into occupants' eyes" (WBDG). Peer-reviewed office research measures orientation-specific discomfort glare and finds "full-height windows that occupy 20–40% of the facade provide the most daylight", with vertical illuminance above ~600 lux triggering complaints (Karimoshaver & Derakhshan 2026). Daylight/view *distance* matters for occupants, not just lux: Woo et al. (2021) report workers nearer to windows scoring better on physical and emotional wellbeing, with distance from the façade a predictor.
- Source: WBDG, *Daylighting* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/daylighting — T1; Karimoshaver & Derakhshan, *Journal of Daylighting* 13:167 https://solarlits.com/jd/13-167 — T2; S. Woo et al., "Access to Daylight and Views Improves Physical and Emotional Wellbeing…", *Frontiers in Sustainable Cities* 3:690055 (2021) https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-cities/articles/10.3389/frsc.2021.690055/full — T2.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: universal; acute in hotels (every room is long-occupancy), hospitals, schools, offices
- Confidence: High for the allocation method; Medium for the specific lux/illuminance thresholds (measured in particular rooms/climates)
- Grid translation: `façade_budget`: for each direction band, `glazed_tiles` vs `façade_tiles` (utilisation ratio), and `unlit_boundary_tiles` (façade tiles deliberately not glazed, e.g. plant, lift halls). Emit an allocation table: room type × aspect × tiles at risk beyond `d`. Validation: (a) 100 % of top-ranked rooms have `façade_directions ≥ 1` and `daylit_share ≥ 0.6`; (b) no room of a bottom-ranked type (store, plant, riser, back-of-house) occupies an equator-facing tile band while a top-ranked room is interior; (c) `glazing_ratio = glazed_tiles/façade_tiles` lies inside the band set in EN-12.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A podium, a neighbouring obstruction or an access road makes one façade worthless — then the budget must be direction-weighted, not uniform. Symptom: a beautiful uniform glazing ratio on all four sides and every guest bed against a service yard.

### EN-06 Give each habitable room a minimum glazed run computed from its floor area
- Rule: Every habitable room must carry a glazed run whose area is at least 10 % of the room's floor area (with 15 % as the higher, forward-looking target), and the glazing must sit in the clear-vision band 0.9–2.0 m above the floor. Where the room's façade is too short to deliver that area, the room is too deep for its own façade and must be reshaped, not just re-tagged.
- Evidence: BRANZ Level: glazing "no less than 10% of the floor area", head height at "half the room width" for glazing on one wall or "one-quarter the width" for glazing on opposite walls, daylight reaching "approximately 1.5 times the window head height", performance target "no less than 30 lux at floor level for 75 % of the standard year", and the clear-vision zone "between 900 mm and 2000 mm above floor level". VELUX's survey of codes records "present (10% glass area to floor area) and future Danish demands for glass area to floor area (15%)".
- Source: BRANZ Level, *Passive design for daylight* https://www.level.org.nz/passive-design/daylighting/ — T1; VELUX DEIC, *Daylight requirements in building codes* https://www.velux.com/healthy-buildings/research-and-knowledge/deic-basic-book/daylight/daylight-requirements-in-building-codes — T3.
- Class: STANDARD
- Scope: habitable rooms: residential, hotel bedrooms, hospital wards, classrooms, offices
- Confidence: High (multiple independent code-derived statements); Medium on transferability (BRANZ is NZ housing; Denmark is a different sky) — class kept: the admitted gap is the *project* constant, the assumed 1.5 m glass height per glazed tile, not the glazed-run-to-floor-area duty, which is stated independently by each body read
- Grid translation: With 0.75 m² of glass per glazed façade tile: `required_glazed_tiles = 0.0333 × room_tiles` (i.e. **1 glazed tile per 30 interior tiles**; a 12 m² room = 48 tiles needs 2 glazed tiles = a 1.0 m run). Validation per room: `glazed_tiles ≥ 0.0333·N` AND `glazed_run_tiles ≤ 0.5 × room_width_tiles` for single-aspect (BRANZ's head-height rule re-expressed: the run must be long enough that the head sits at half the room width or better), else flag `façade_undersupplied`. Report `glazing_to_floor_ratio` per room with the assumed 1.5 m glass height.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Corridor-lit hotel bedrooms, internal suites, and rooms lit from an atrium or a monitored roof aperture legitimately fail a *sidelight* check — record them as `toplight` or `borrowed` and check them against EN-24's toplight geometry instead of silently passing them.

### EN-07 Push the programme that does not need light into the band that cannot get it
- Rule: Place circulation, storage, plant, risers, lift halls, staff back-of-house and wet services in the plan's interior daylight-shadow band and on the low-value façades. This is the single largest lever available: it converts a depth problem into a free re-allocation rather than a shape problem.
- Evidence: The federal daylighting guidance treats the interior of a deep floor as the place where daylighting *fails and controls must take over* — "circuiting focuses on areas where there is adequate daylight illumination to allow for light dimming or switching", i.e. the zone beyond the daylight zone is defined by the absence of daylight, and is where the code (IECC C402.4.2, via UpCodes) requires separate control treatment. NCC/AS1668 practice formalises the same logic for air: spaces beyond the ventilation reach get mechanical supply, and borrowed ventilation is allowed only at a higher openable-area allowance (≥10 % of floor area versus 5 % directly).
- Source: WBDG, *Daylighting* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/daylighting — T1; UpCodes, *Sidelight Daylight Zone / C402.4.2* https://up.codes/s/sidelight-daylight-zone — T1; CCC Engineering, *Natural Ventilation NCC* https://cccengineering.com.au/design-memos/natural-ventilation-ncc-compliance/ — T3.
- Class: BUILDING-TYPE CONVENTION
- Scope: all multi-room types; universal in practice
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Compute the `shadow_band` = interior tiles with `dist > d_daylight` from every glazed façade tile. Validation: `shadow_band` occupancy by room type — require `habitable_tiles_in_shadow_band / shadow_band_tiles ≤ 0.15`, and require `Σ(circulation+store+plant tiles) ≥ 0.6 × shadow_band_tiles`. Report the swap cost: which long-occupancy rooms would move inward and how many door runs/thresholds (privacy depth) that adds.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Some interior programmes have their own environmental demands the proxy cannot see: plant needs a service route and outside air for combustion/free-cooling; kitchens need an extract path. Symptom: the agent solves daylight by pushing a plant room to a façade and a kitchen into the core, then discovers no duct route (EN-21).

### EN-08 Prefer through-units: openings on two opposed façades buy light, air and comfort together
- Rule: Where the plan can afford it, give long-occupancy rooms openings on two *opposed* façades. Two-aspect geometry is the only single plan move that simultaneously raises daylight reach, enables cross ventilation and cuts the mechanical requirement, so rank it above adding glazing area to one face.
- Evidence: Ventilation: NCC/CIBSE-derived practice requires "openings on opposite walls" for cross ventilation and extends the usable depth from ~2 × ceiling height (single-sided) to 4–5 × ceiling height. Daylight: BRANZ's head-height rule is halved (from ½ the room width to ¼) when glazing is on opposite walls, i.e. two faces deliver the same light at a shallower head height. Alexander's Pattern 159 ("Light on Two Sides of Every Room") records the behavioural preference qualitatively — people gravitate to rooms lit from two sides and leave single-sided rooms — and gives no dimensions, so it supports the ranking but not any number.
- Source: CCC Engineering, *Natural Ventilation NCC 2025* https://cccengineering.com.au/design-memos/natural-ventilation-ncc-compliance/ — T3; BRANZ Level, *Passive design for daylight* https://www.level.org.nz/passive-design/daylighting/ — T1; Alexander, Ishikawa & Silverstein, *A Pattern Language*, Pattern 159 https://patternlanguage.cc/Patterns/Light-on-Two-Sides-of-Every-Room-(159) — T3.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: all types; strongest where the free-running strategy depends on it
- Confidence: High for the ventilation and daylight mechanics; Low for the preference claim (observation-based)
- Grid translation: `opposed_aspect = true` iff the room's façade tiles touch two boundary edges of opposite bearing. Validation: `opposed_aspect_share` over long-occupancy rooms (target set per programme, e.g. ≥ 0.5 for residential/hospital wards, ≥ 0.3 for hotel floors, ≥ 0.6 for offices); for every `opposed_aspect` room, additionally require `vent_path_tiles` — a walkable line from one vent tile to the other with no more than one door — else the through-unit is geometric but not ventilable.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Perimeter blocks and courtyard typologies produce "opposed" aspects that look onto a narrow self-shaded void — the two faces are opposed but neither is bright (see EN-11, EN-14). Symptom: a high double-aspect share computed on a plan where the second aspect is a 6-tile-wide light well.

### EN-09 Keep the shallow axis the free-flow axis in warm climates
- Rule: In cooling- or ventilation-dominated designs, the plan's *narrow* dimension must run between the two façades carrying openable vents, and the long dimension is the one that may grow. Where the narrow axis faces the wrong way, rotating the building is a cheaper fix than any plant.
- Evidence: WBDG natural ventilation gives the reach as a maximum *width* for cross ventilation ("estimated at 45 ft"), i.e. the flow dimension is the constrained one, and quantifies the wind flow as `Qwind = K × A × V` with `K` between 0.4 and 0.8 depending on the incidence angle of the wind on the opening — so a façade not square to the prevailing wind loses up to 40 % of its nominal flow. NZ tropical guidance prescribes the north–south long axis (east–west shallow axis) with a 0–30° azimuth tolerance, precisely so the shallow axis catches the trade wind and the long faces avoid the low-angle east/west sun.
- Source: WBDG, *Natural Ventilation* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/natural-ventilation — T1; NZEB, *Form & Orientation* https://nzeb.in/knowledge-centre/passive-design/form-orientation/ — T1/T3; BRANZ Level, *Location and orientation for passive heating and cooling* https://www.level.org.nz/passive-design/location-orientation-and-layout/ — T1 (advises a "shallow east-west floor plan" for airflow).
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: warm-humid, warm-dry and temperate; not applicable to deep mechanical plates
- Confidence: High for the geometry rule; Medium for the numeric K range (single-source restatement of the classic air-flow relation)
- Grid translation: Compute `narrow_axis_tiles`, `narrow_axis_bearing` and check that the two façade runs at its ends are both tagged `vent_band`. Validation: `narrow_axis_tiles ≤ 27` (13.5 m) and `≤ 22` (11 m) under the 4× reading; `vent_bearings = 2` distinct opposed bands; report `flow_capacity_proxy = Σ vent_area × K(incidence)` with `K` a declared assumption.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Dominant cooling loads, high pollution/noise on both façades, or a security constraint on opening all make the flow path undesirable; then the strategy is declared mechanical (EN-21, EN-25) and the shallow-axis rule relaxes — but only with that record.

### EN-10 Assign façades by acoustic exposure before assigning them by view
- Rule: Rank the four façades by external noise exposure and give the noisy ones to corridors, stairs, services, plant and commercial frontage; give the quiet ones to rooms where people sleep, recover or concentrate. Exposure ranking is a plan decision with an equipment consequence, not a site observation.
- Evidence: Room-level noise targets are quantified and type-specific: WHO gives "<30 dB(A) at night in bedrooms" and "<35 dB(A) in classrooms" (WHO Regional Europe noise fact sheet), with the 1999 *Guidelines for Community Noise* giving 35 dB for living rooms and 30 dB day/night for hospital areas, and annoyance beginning above ~55 dB outdoors. Measured reality in hospitals is far worse than the target: a peer-reviewed review reports shared hospital rooms averaging 49.9 dB with peaks over 100 dB versus single rooms averaging 44.7 dB with no >100 dB peaks, and 2–3× more noise events per night in shared rooms.
- Source: WHO Regional Office for Europe, *Noise* fact sheet https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/noise — T1; WHO, *Guidelines for Community Noise* (1999) executive summary https://www.ruidos.org/Noise/WHO_Noise_guidelines_summary.html — T1 text via T3 mirror; "The silent threat: investigating sleep disturbances in hospitalized patients" https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11107945/ — T2.
- Class: STANDARD
- Scope: hotels, hospitals, residential, schools; secondary in offices, retail, industrial
- Confidence: High for the target levels; Medium for the measured ward values (varies by unit, staffing model and case mix)
- Grid translation: Tag each façade band with a declared `exposure_LAeq` rank (1 quiet … 4 loud) from the site brief. Validation: (a) every `sleep | ward | classroom | private_office` room has `min_exposure_rank ≤ 2`; (b) `circulation|plant|service` rooms absorb the rank-4 band (`loud_band_habitable_share = 0`); (c) report the `exposure_inversion_count` — long-occupancy rooms on the loudest façade, each needing a documented mitigation (deep buffer, ventilation penalty, or accepted facade).
- Exceptions / failure mode: A noisy façade is sometimes the *entry* façade and the view/arrival side. Resolve by putting the lobby/commercial and circulation there (EN-07) rather than guest rooms — which is exactly what the façade budget is for.

### EN-11 Shade with geometry before you shade with glass
- Rule: Reduce solar gain first with plan and section geometry — self-shading wings, recesses, covered circulation, and overhangs sized from the sun-profile relation — before reducing it with glazing specification, which the grid cannot model at all. Each geometry move must be recorded as a façade-length consequence, since shading a façade also dims it.
- Evidence: Federal guidance: south is "easiest to shade" with overhangs while east and west are "hardest due to low sun angles"; exterior shading reduces cooling energy by "5 % to 15 %", whereas interior blinds "control glare but not heat gain" (WBDG *Sun Control and Shading Devices*, after the Olgyay tradition). The sizing relation is standard: overhang projection `P = (G + H) / tan(αp)` with `G` the gap above the window, `H` the window height and `αp` the solar profile angle — a worked example gives a 0.79 m projection for a 1.50 m window with a 0.20 m gap at a 65° profile angle. **DESIGN-PRINCIPLE direction (HEURISTIC, Confidence Medium)**: external shading outperforms internal on peak indoor temperature — external rejects gain before it enters the room, internal blinds mostly stop glare. **Magnitude NOT established: the previously quoted "12–18 °C vs 8–13 °C peak reduction" figures trace to a shutter/awning vendor's blog summarising a study it attributes to the British Blind and Shutter Association by name but does not cite (no link, no reference list); audit 05 A4 found that pairing implausible as a peak *operative-temperature* reduction in a heavyweight conditioned room and more likely a free-running test-cell figure. The magnitudes have been removed; a measured simulation or field study must be retrieved before any temperature-delta number is restored here.**
- Source: WBDG, *Sun Control and Shading Devices* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/sun-control-and-shading-devices — T1; Passive Solar Architecture, *Overhang Calculator* https://www.passivesolararchitecture.com/passive-solar-overhang-calculator/ — T4 (formula and worked example only); GDCG, *External vs internal shading — what the evidence says* https://gdcg.co.uk/blog/external-vs-internal-shading-what-the-evidence-says/ — T4 (trade-association campaign blog; **used for direction only, magnitudes not accepted as evidence**).
- Class: ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT
- Scope: all types on cooling-dominated and equator-facing façades
- Confidence: High for south-vs-east/west shading asymmetry and the geometry formula; Medium for the *direction* external > internal (per WBDG T1 statement); the *magnitude* of the temperature delta is **not established** — retrieve a measured simulation or field study before restoring any number.
- Grid translation: In plan, self-shading is representable; in section it is not, so model only the plan part: for each façade run compute `self_shaded_tiles` = façade tiles whose outer normal lies on a recessed/protected side (a tile whose two neighbours along the boundary are one/both deeper into the plan, i.e. adjacent to a notch, wing or courtyard). Validation: report `shaded_share` per direction band; require `equator_band_shaded_share` between 0.2 and 0.6 (too little = gain problem, too much = daylight debt per EN-05); require `E_W_unshaded_glazed_tiles` against a declared cap (EN-13); and log the paired daylight cost of each shading move as a `daylit_share` delta.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Self-shading plans are also the plans that fail daylight — courtyards and notch-heavy perimeters shade the very tiles EN-06 needs. Symptom: an excellent solar-gain score alongside a falling `daylit_share`; the two must be reported on one table.

### EN-12 Cap the glazing-to-façade ratio per orientation band and treat glass as a two-way load
- Rule: Set a maximum glazing fraction for each orientation band (glass area ÷ opaque+glass façade area) and a whole-floor cap, because glazing trades a winter gain against a summer gain and a cooling load against a daylighting credit. Publish the target band, then verify the design against it with the façade tiles actually glazed.
- Evidence: Code layer: IECC C402.5.3 fixes maximum U-factor and SHGC by climate zone with orientation-specific exceptions and a separate skylight allowance (higher SHGC 0.60 permitted with daylight controls); C402.4.2 ties lighting controls to the sidelight daylight zone. Quantified trade-off: a simulated office study in subtropical southern Hunan varied WWR from 0 % to 100 % and found electricity demand "increases by about 100 kW·h for each 20% increase in WWR", concluding "an increase in WWR is not beneficial in terms of energy savings" once daylight and aesthetic considerations are weighed. Glare/comfort layer: the measured office study finds the daylight benefit saturates — "full-height windows that occupy 20–40% of the facade provide the most daylight" — with vertical illuminance above ~600 lux driving complaints.
- Source: UpCodes, *C402.5.3 Maximum U-factor and SHGC* https://up.codes/s/maximum-u-factor-and-shgc — T1; J. Li, B. Zheng, K. B. Bedra, Z. Li, X. Chen, "Evaluating the Effect of Window-to-Wall Ratios on Cooling-Energy…", *Int. J. Environ. Res. Public Health* 18 (2021) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8393238/ — T2; Karimoshaver & Derakhshan, *Journal of Daylighting* 13:167 https://solarlits.com/jd/13-167 — T2.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT
- Scope: all types with mechanical cooling or heating; the band values are type- and climate-specific
- Confidence: High that the ratio is bounded by orientation and that more glass costs energy; Medium for transferable numbers (the +100 kWh/20 % WWR figure is one climate, one building, one system assumption)
- Grid translation: With 0.75 m² glass per glazed tile and 1.35 m² of wall per façade tile at 2.7 m clear: `WWR ≈ 0.556 × glazed_tiles/façade_tiles`. Validation: compute WWR per direction band with the declared caps (e.g. equator band 0.25–0.35, E/W bands ≤0.20, pole band per brief) and a floor-level cap; flag any band above cap with its paired `gain_tiles = glazed_tiles × band_weight` and its `daylit_share` gain, so the trade is visible. Report `floor_WWR` for the mechanical sizing step (EN-25).
- Exceptions / failure mode: Retail frontage, atria, museums, greenhouses and prison exercise galleries legitimately break the band; exempt by tag and record the reason, since the equipment consequence (peak cooling plant) must still be reported.

### EN-13 Do not put long-occupancy rooms on unshaded east or west façades in cooling climates
- Rule: East and west bands carry low-angle sun that overhangs cannot catch, and they are the worst glare bands. Where those bands cannot be shaded geometrically, allocate them to short-occupancy or shielded uses and keep beds, wards, desks and classrooms off them.
- Evidence: WBDG's shading guidance is explicit that east/west are the hard cases because of the low sun and that interior blinds do not stop the heat. The glare survey (Karimoshaver & Derakhshan) reports the off-equator intermediate orientations (SE, SW — the morning/afternoon low-sun bands) at DGP > 0.35, i.e. intolerable for a substantial share of occupied hours, while NE/NW give a better balance; and finds desk orientation matters, with desks placed perpendicular to the window performing worse for discomfort glare — a layout instruction the grid *can* check.
- Source: WBDG, *Sun Control and Shading Devices* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/sun-control-and-shading-devices — T1; Karimoshaver & Derakhshan, *Journal of Daylighting* 13:167 https://solarlits.com/jd/13-167 — T2.
- Class: HEURISTIC
- Scope: cooling-dominated and equator-adjacent climates; inverted for heating-dominated cold sites where west-afternoon gain is wanted
- Confidence: Medium (glare findings are from a specific climate and facade sample; the geometry argument is general)
- Grid translation: For each direction band compute `low_sun_band = true` for E and W. Validation: `long_occupancy_tiles_in_low_sun_band / total_long_occupancy_tiles ≤ 0.25` unless those tiles have a shading or buffer record; and for open-plan office tiles emit a `desk_orientation_check` — workstation tiles must be laid parallel to the glazed run, not perpendicular to it (measurable as the ratio of workstation-tile axes to façade-tile axes).
- Exceptions / failure mode: Cold climates where afternoon gain is an asset; already-shaded west façades (adjacent taller mass the grid cannot see); tower/horizontal-spread plans where the E/W band is a small share anyway.

### EN-14 Use the perimeter-to-area ratio as the plan-level heat-loss and heat-gain proxy, and penalise jaggedness
- Rule: Report every candidate floor plate's perimeter-to-area ratio, and treat a rising ratio without a corresponding daylight or ventilation benefit as an envelope penalty (heat loss in cold, heat gain in warm, and more junctions in both). Compactness is a choice the agent must be able to quantify in tiles.
- Evidence: Building-science analysis of form defines compactness as `C = Volume / Surface area` and reports the effect size: for small houses, shape variation moved heating load by about 25 %, while for large buildings the same variation moved it by only about 10 % — i.e. the penalty is scale-dependent (Straube, BSD-061). Government tropical guidance prescribes compact forms (low surface-to-volume) for hot-dry and cold regions specifically. Thermal-bridge reasoning reinforces it: more envelope means more junctions, and junctions leak (see EN-15's caveat about what mass can and cannot do).
- Source: J. Straube, "BSD-061: The Function of Form — Building Shape and Energy", *Building Science Corporation* (2012) https://buildingscience.com/documents/insights/bsi-061-function-form-building-shape-and-energy — T3; NZEB, *Form & Orientation* https://nzeb.in/knowledge-centre/passive-design/form-orientation/ — T1/T3.
- Class: ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT
- Scope: universal, but the leverage is small on large plates (per Straube) — do not over-claim it there
- Confidence: High for the direction of the effect; Medium for magnitude (25 %/10 % figures are quoted from a single practice column and depend on the base fabric)
- Grid translation: `P/A (m⁻¹) = 4 × (W + H) / (W × H)` for a rectangular W×H-tile plate; general form `P/A = 0.5 × boundary_tiles / (0.25 × interior_tiles)`. Also `compactness_index φ = boundary_tiles² / interior_tiles` (φ = 4 for a square, 7.5 for a 5:1 rectangle, higher for L/T/courtyard plans). Validation: report `P/A`, `φ`, `notch_count` and `junction_tiles = boundary_tiles + Σ internal_carrier_tiles`; require `φ ≤ 6` unless the excess is itemised against measured `Δdaylit_share` and `Δvent_path` it buys. Scale caveat: skip the penalty above a declared plate area where the study shows leverage collapses.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A jagged plan *is* the right answer when the jaggedness is what brings bedrooms within daylight reach of a façade (EN-04) — that is a purchase, not an error, provided the `Δ` is recorded. Symptom to avoid: wings added for aesthetics that buy no tiles of daylight.

### EN-15 Never justify a plan move with thermal mass — the grid cannot carry the claim
- Rule: Treat "orient the building for thermal mass" and mass-based claims as heuristics that may not change room placement, depth or façade allocation, because mass acts within ~100 mm of a surface and the host models no surface, no material and no section. Record the claim, do not act on it.
- Evidence: The governing physical statement is a *depth* statement: BRE Digest 454 Part 1, as reproduced by the Steel Construction Institute's thermal-mass guidance, is that "temperature variations penetrate up to about 100 mm" into a slab, with an optimum participating thickness of 75–100 mm; the same note reports that in one modelled comparison the carbon difference between massive and lightweight construction was "less than a 1% variation", that mass only helps where air can reach the exposed soffit (deep plans "hinder air flow"), and that exposed soffits *increase* the volume that has to be conditioned.
- Source: Steel Construction Information Service, *Thermal mass for cooling in multi-storey commercial buildings* (citing BRE Digest 454) https://steelconstruction.info/topics/sustainability/thermal-mass/ — T3.
- Class: HEURISTIC
- Scope: universal (applies as a demotion rule on all types)
- Confidence: High that the claim is not actionable on this grid; Medium for the quoted magnitudes (single secondary reproduction)
- Grid translation: Emit `thermal_mass_claims = []` in every design record; if a revision cites mass as justification, output `unverifiable_claim` with the pointer to this rule. The only mass-adjacent check the grid *can* run is the co-benefit that survives it: exposed-soffit rooms must lie within the ventilation reach band (`dist ≤ 10 tiles` single-sided / `≤ 27 tiles` cross — **both are maximums, rounded DOWN per EN-03's stated direction**) so purge air can reach them — which is EN-08/EN-19, and already checked there.
- Exceptions / failure mode: None for plan geometry. The failure is rhetorical: mass appears as the reason a deep plan is acceptable when it is the reason the plan needs ducts.

### EN-16 Buy acoustic separation with distance, buffers and room order — thickness is not available
- Rule: On this grid a wall is one tile and a cavity cannot be represented, so separation must be planned as geometry: source–receiver distance, buffer rooms between unlike functions, and no shared-wall adjacency between noisy and sensitive rooms. Every separation requirement is expressed as a path, not as a build-up.
- Evidence: Two independent quantitative facts support the distance lever. (i) Attenuation with distance is the cheapest control: a point source in free field loses 6 dB per doubling of distance, and in real indoor reverberant fields the fall-off is less than 6 dB per doubling — so distance still buys decibels, just fewer than the ideal. (ii) The ISO 3382-3 open-plan office quantities are defined as *distances*: the distraction distance `r_D` where speech intelligibility (STI) drops below 0.5, and the comfort distance `r_C` where the A-weighted level falls below 45 dB, with the spatial decay rate `D2,S` measured in dB per doubling of distance. Regulatory minima then bound what the wall itself must do: England's Part E requires at least 45 dB `DnT,w + Ctr` for new-build separating walls/floors, 43 dB for conversions, and a minimum 40 dB `Rw` for internal room-dividing walls, with impact limits of 62 dB (`L'nT,w`, new floors) and 64 dB (conversions).
- Source: eNoiseControl, *Doubling of Distance Noise Reduction* https://www.enoisecontrol.com/acoustic-terminology/doubling-distance-noise-reduction-decibel/ — T4; Treble Technologies, *Open Plan Office* acoustic parameters (ISO 3382-3) https://docs.treble.tech/acoustic-parameters/open_plan_office — T4; ISO 3382-3:2022 scope record https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/es/#iso:std:iso:3382:-3:en — T1; Plans Made Easy, *Building Regulations Part E explained* https://plansmadeeasy.org/building-regulations-part-e/ — T3.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: hotels, hospitals, residential, schools, offices with speech privacy needs
- Confidence: High for the mechanism and the Part E numbers; Medium for `Rw`-to-`DnT,w` transfer (practice notes a 5–10 dB loss between laboratory `Rw` and on-site `Dw`)
- Grid translation: For each sensitive/noisy pair compute: `wall_planes` = number of blocked-tile lines crossed by the straight tile path; `path_tiles` = A* octile distance in tiles; `door_share` = door area ÷ (door + wall area) on the shared line. Distance rule: each doubling of `path_tiles × 0.5 m` is worth up to 6 dB — so state the target as `required_path_m = source_range_m × 2^((L_source − L_target)/6)` in free field and treat the result as an optimistic bound. Buffer rule: for every pair with a declared `ΔL > 15 dB` requirement, `buffer_room_required = true` and the intervening room must be a `circulation|service|storage` type. Report `door_share` per separating line with the composite-loss warning (below).
- Exceptions / failure mode: A door is the hole in the wall. With a 45 dB wall and a ~28 dB door, a 6 m × 2.7 m separating wall with a 2 m² door composites to roughly 36–37 dB — a 12 % opening area costs about 8 dB. The grid cannot model the leaves, so the check must be reported as `door_share > 0.10 on a separation line ⇒ target unachievable by wall geometry alone; add a second line (vestibule/double door) or move the source`. Symptom: a "45 dB party wall" with a full-height 2-tile door run and no vestibule.

### EN-17 Break sound paths in the plan: stagger, turn, and never align openings or doors
- Rule: Flanking is a plan property. Party walls must not run continuously through the whole floor with rooms mirrored on both sides; noisy openings (doors to corridors, stairs, risers, plant) must not face sensitive openings directly; and where a wall line changes use, put a corner or a room break in it rather than a straight junction.
- Evidence: The mechanism is documented in the acoustics literature and in the codes' structure: laboratory `Rw` of a leaf is not the delivered value because sound reaches the receiving room through the junctions around it, which is why practice quotes a 5–10 dB gap between the tested `Rw` and the measured on-site `Dw` (Designing Buildings; typical office sound insulation quoted as ~45 dB `Dw`). Peer-reviewed work on flanking transmission through massive–lightweight connections and through gypsum-board junctions confirms the junction, not the panel, governs (sources listed).
- Source: Designing Buildings, *Sound insulation in buildings* https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Sound_insulation — T3; "Flanking Sound Transmission in Massive-Lightweight Connections", *Archives of Acoustics* (PAN) https://www.journals.pan.pl/Content/138550 — T2; "Prediction of flanking sound transmission through gypsum board…", DAGA https://pub.dega-akustik.de/DAGA_1999-2008/data/articles/003324.pdf — T2.
- Class: HEURISTIC
- Scope: multi-cell types: hotels, hospitals, residential, schools, offices
- Confidence: Medium for the specific dB gap (practice-level, definition-dependent); High that junctions dominate
- Grid translation: Compute three plan diagnostics: (a) `continuous_party_lines` — carrier wall lines present on every floor with rooms of *contradicting* sensitivity on both sides; require `sensitivity_flip` at least once every `k` tiles (k = 8 tiles = 4 m declared); (b) `aligned_door_pairs` — door runs on opposite sides of a corridor within `±1` tile of each other where one side is sensitive; (c) `corner_rooms_with_two_exposed_walls_on_mixed_bands` (a corner room inherits two façades and thus two exposure ranks — see EN-10). Report each as a list of tile coordinates to fix.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Deliberately mirrored cell wings (hotels) are the efficient plan and will always trip diagnostic (a); the fix is not to un-mirror but to insert non-sensitive cells (bathroom/linen/service) at intervals, which is the same trick as a bathroom buffer in a hospital bay. Symptom: an agent unstacks an otherwise-good wing to satisfy an acoustic diagnostic — record the trade explicitly instead.

### EN-18 Set the room-by-room environmental target table, and derive the plan requirement from the difference
- Rule: Before placing rooms, publish a target table per room type: illuminance/UGR class, background noise class, openable-vent need, exposure rank and occupancy density. The plan requirement is the *gap* between a room's target and what its band can deliver; a room whose target is met by the façade it happens to get was not designed, it got lucky.
- Evidence: The target layer is genuinely standardised and type-differentiated. EN 12464-1 (as reproduced in professional lighting tables): offices (writing/typing/conferencing) 500 lx, UGR ≤ 19, Ra ≥ 80; classrooms 300 lx, UGR ≤ 19; hospital general ward 100 lx, UGR ≤ 19, Ra ≥ 80; hospital examination room 500 lx, UGR ≤ 19, Ra ≥ 90; retail sales area 300 lx, UGR ≤ 22; hotel room general 100 lx, UGR ≤ 22; industrial rough machining 300 lx, UGR ≤ 22. Comfort/air layer: EN 15251 gives daylight factor bands of >5 % (Cat I), >3 % (Cat II), >2 % (Cat III) and ventilation rates from 2.5 to 7 L/s per person, with PMV operative ranges 20–24 °C (winter) / 23–26 °C (summer). Background noise layer: practice NC targets are ~NC 30–35 for private offices, ~NC 35–40 for open plan and ~NC 25–35 for hospital patient rooms.
- Source: Techlumen, *EN 12464-1 / EN 12464-2 lighting standards — specification tables* https://www.techlumen.gr/en/guide/lighting-standards-guide/ — T3 (vendor reproduction of the standard); REHVA Journal, *Revision of EN 15251: Indoor Environmental Criteria* https://www.rehva.eu/rehva-journal/chapter/revision-of-en-15251-indoor-environmental-criteria — T1/T3; Commercial Acoustics, *Noise Criteria (NC) Rating Chart* https://commercial-acoustics.com/sound-advice/noise-criteria-nc-rating-chart/ — T4.
- Class: STANDARD
- Scope: all inhabited types; the numbers themselves are what differ by type
- Confidence: High for the structure and the EN 15251 bands; Medium for the EN 12464-1 figures as quoted (secondary reproduction) and for the NC bands (trade guidance, not standard text)
- Grid translation: Store `env_targets[room_type] = {lux, UGR, NC, DF_class, vent_Ls_person, exposure_rank_max, daylight_share_min}`. Then per floor produce a **gap register**: for each room instance compute delivered proxies (`daylit_share`, `glazed_tiles`, `vent_tiles_available`, `exposure_rank`, `wall_planes_to_source`) and emit `gap = target − delivered`. Validation: `Σ gap = 0` is the goal; any residual gap must be closed by a plan edit (move, deepen, buffer, re-band) or by an explicit declaration that mechanical/lighting systems carry it (which then forces the EN-20/EN-21/EN-25 consequences).
- Exceptions / failure mode: A single plan cannot serve both a 500 lx/UGR 19 office and a 100 lx/UGR 22 hotel bedroom with the same façade allocation; a mixed brief must be zoned by *floor* or by *band*, not averaged. Symptom: an averaged façade policy that meets no room's target.

### EN-19 Size openable area from the room's floor area, and check the whole-floor opening budget
- Rule: Where natural or hybrid ventilation is claimed, every ventilated room must carry openable area of at least 5 % of its floor area (10 % where air is borrowed through another space), and the façade as a whole must be able to deliver the openable fraction its shading/glazing design leaves available. This is an area budget measured in vent tiles, and it constrains how much of the façade may be glass-free or shaded.
- Evidence: NCC 2025 Part F6 Deemed-to-Satisfy as summarised in engineering practice: "openable windows, doors, or vents equal to at least 5% of the floor area" (F6D7), borrowed ventilation requiring "at least 10% of the floor area" (F6D8(b)), with an indoor-air quality limit of 850 ppm CO₂ over 8 h (Table F6V1). WBDG gives the driving-flow relation `Qwind = K × A × V` (K 0.4–0.8), i.e. flow is proportional to *opening area*, and notes that air movement at ~160 fpm lowers perceived temperature by as much as 5 °F — the reason a smaller opening can still comfortably ventilate a room on a breezy site.
- Source: CCC Engineering, *Natural Ventilation NCC 2025* https://cccengineering.com.au/design-memos/natural-ventilation-ncc-compliance/ — T3 (NCC clause values); WBDG, *Natural Ventilation* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/natural-ventilation — T1.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT
- Scope: naturally and hybrid ventilated buildings; hotels/offices/hospitals frequently fail this in practice
- Confidence: High for the 5 %/10 % figures as NCC DTS values; Medium for their transferability as a general design rule
- Grid translation: `required_vent_tiles = 0.0227 × room_tiles` (**1 vent tile per 44 interior tiles**; a 44-tile ≈ 11 m² room needs one 0.55 m² vent) or `0.0455 × room_tiles` for borrowed air. Cross-check against the shading/glazing design: `available_vent_tiles = glazed_tiles × openable_fraction` with `openable_fraction` declared (fixed ≤ 0.5, opening ≤ 0.9). Validation per room: `available_vent ≥ required_vent`; per façade band: `vent_band_share ≤ glazed_band_share` and the band's `shaded_share` (EN-11) must not remove the tiles the budget needs.
- Exceptions / failure mode: High-rise, noisy-façade, security-restricted and heavily shaded façades cannot open — then `ventilation_regime` must be re-declared (EN-03) and the mechanical consequence paid. Symptom: a passive-ventilation claim surviving on paper while every window on the loud façade is fixed.

### EN-20 Give each daylit tile band its own lighting control zone and claim the power credit
- Rule: Daylighting without switched control is a load, not a saving. Every band of floor within the code daylight zone of a glazed run is a separate control zone, and the lighting-power deduction only applies where those zones exist. Draw the zones as tiles, then let them drive luminaire layout, circuiting and eventually the ceiling/service design.
- Evidence: The code defines the zone geometrically: IECC C405.2.3.2 — the sidelight daylight zone extends from vertical fenestration "up to 1.0 times the height from the floor to the top of the fenestration" laterally and "up to 2 feet (610 mm)" longitudinally; rooftop monitors use 1.0 × the height to the *bottom* of the fenestration and 0.25 × laterally; and Title-24's acceptance text requires primary and secondary sidelit zones to be controlled separately from skylit zones, with combined light at the reference location between the reference illuminance and 150 % of it, and luminaires reduced to under 35 % power (≥65 % reduction) under full daylight. Federal guidance closes the loop on the need: "Daylight-responsive electric lighting controls are absolutely essential to any daylighting system", and daylighting can reduce "as much as one-third of total building energy costs".
- Source: UpCodes, *Sidelight Daylight Zone* https://up.codes/s/sidelight-daylight-zone — T1; Energy Code Ace, *NA7.6.1 Automatic Daylighting Control Acceptance* https://energycodeace.com/site/custom/public/reference-ace-2016/Documents/1324na761automaticdaylightingcontrolacceptance.htm — T1; WBDG, *Daylighting* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/daylighting — T1.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT
- Scope: all non-domestic types; irrelevant for single-storey housing
- Confidence: High (code text), with the caveat that these are US/California provisions
- Grid translation: With H = 2.1 m: `control_band = 4 tiles` deep (2.0 m) from each glazed run, widened laterally by 2 tiles at each end. Validation: (a) `Σ control_band_tiles` reported per façade run, and `control_zones = number of disjoint bands` (each needs a switch/dimmer); (b) `daylight_credit_area / floor_area` published so the mechanical-electrical consequence is visible; (c) bands closer than 2 tiles must merge (avoid claiming two zones for one room). Cross-check with EN-04: the 1.0 × H code band is *narrower* than the 1.5–2.5 × H design band, so report both and never let the wider one claim the credit.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Spaces that must stay dimmed (theatres, galleries, wards at night, data halls) are excluded from the credit; open-plan bands split by tall storage/partitions break the lateral continuity the code assumes.

### EN-21 Reserve the service band and the riser grid before placing rooms, because every environmental gap becomes ductwork
- Rule: Any environmental target the plan cannot meet with geometry (EN-18 residual) becomes equipment and distribution: perimeter units, ducted supply and extract, risers and a ceiling/floor service zone. Reserve their tiles first — the sequence is gap → equipment → route → then rooms — otherwise the design discovers the duct at the end and eats a room.
- Evidence: The link from plan form to plant is direct and code-visible: the ventilation rate procedure in ASHRAE 62.1 sizes zone outdoor airflow as a *people term plus an area term* (`Vbz = (Rp·Pz + Ra·A)/Ez`, with zone air distribution effectiveness Ez ≈ 0.9–1.0), so occupant density and floor area — both plan outputs — fix the air volume, hence the duct; and EN 15251's per-person rates of 2.5–7 L/s are the same lever in SI. WBDG's shading/ventilation notes put the consequence the other way: mechanical backup is what a plan owes when reach fails (EN-03). The NCC's mechanical/backup provisions and the code daylight-control requirements (EN-20) make the control wiring part of the same reservation.
- Source: ASHRAE 62.1 (Ventilation Rate Procedure formula, zone effectiveness; body text not machine-readable this pass — formula stated at structural level) https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/technical%20resources/standards%20and%20guidelines/standards%20addenda/62.1-2016/62_1_2016_s_20190726.pdf — T1; REHVA Journal, *Revision of EN 15251* https://www.rehva.eu/rehva-journal/chapter/revision-of-en-15251-indoor-environmental-criteria — T1/T3; WBDG, *Natural Ventilation* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/natural-ventilation — T1.
- Class: ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT
- Scope: all types with mechanical systems, i.e. every deep plan and every hospital/hotel/office
- Confidence: High for the mechanism; Medium for the per-type Rp/Ra values, which could not be read from the standard this pass (do not quote them)
- Grid translation: Reserve tiles before room placement: `riser_band` = a contiguous blocked set of declared size on every floor, aligned vertically (portal-stacking rule from the theory file); `perimeter_band` = the 2 tiles inside the façade line on bands whose WWR/loads demand local units; `duct_route` = a walkable-adjacent blocked corridor line from riser to each band, min 2 tiles wide (1.0 m) declared. Validation: `air_demand_L/s = Σ(occupancy × Rp) + Σ(area_tiles × 0.25 × Ra)` with Rp/Ra declared inputs; compare against a declared duct capacity per route and report `underserved_zones`. Emit `mechanical_load_statements` — one per residual gap from EN-18.
- Exceptions / failure mode: All-air-free (fan-coil + dedicated outdoor air, UFAD, chilled beam) changes the geometry of the reservation, not the need for it; VRF needs fewer, smaller routes. Symptom: the agent solves an acoustic or daylight conflict by adding a buffer room and thereby deletes the only duct route to the wing.

### EN-22 Keep plant, risers and wet services away from sensitive rooms by path, not by adjacency
- Rule: Plant noise and vibration travel through structure and air paths, so site plant by *separation path length and wall-plane count* against the most sensitive neighbour, not by "not touching". Put plant on low-value façade bands or in the shadow band with a service route, and never share a wall line with a sleep or recovery room.
- Evidence: Background-noise targets in the quietest rooms are close to the threshold of audibility (WHO <30 dB(A) at night in bedrooms; ~NC 25–35 for hospital patient rooms), which plant noise typically exceeds by a wide margin — hence the requirement is separation and enclosure, not absorption. Distance attenuation is limited indoors (practitioners note reverberant-field decay of less than 6 dB per doubling of distance), which is why the rule needs both distance *and* planes; flanking through junctions (EN-17) is the standard failure of distance-only design.
- Source: WHO Regional Office for Europe, *Noise* fact sheet https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/noise — T1; Commercial Acoustics, *NC Rating Chart* https://commercial-acoustics.com/sound-advice/noise-criteria-nc-rating-chart/ — T4; eNoiseControl, *Doubling of Distance Noise Reduction* https://www.enoisecontrol.com/acoustic-terminology/doubling-distance-noise-reduction-decibel/ — T4.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: hotels, hospitals, residential, offices; industrial inverts (process is the reason for the building)
- Confidence: Medium — the dB physics is solid, the specific siting judgement is practice-level
- Grid translation: For each `plant`-tagged tile-set and each sensitive room compute `path_tiles` (A* octile), `wall_planes` (blocked lines crossed) and `shared_boundary_tiles` (must be 0). Validation: `shared_boundary_tiles = 0` for plant↔sensitive; `path_tiles ≥ P_min` and `wall_planes ≥ 2` (declared P_min, e.g. 12 tiles = 6 m); require a `service_route` from plant to the nearest boundary or riser that does not cross sensitive rooms (the maintenance-corridor consequence). Report the plant's `air_demand` and `exhaust` need so the route reaches the outside.
- Exceptions / failure mode: In a hospital, plant *must* be near the spaces it serves (risers, duct lengths) — so the constraint is "as close as allowed", and the answer is a technical-occupancy buffer zone rather than distance. Symptom: an agent pushing risers to the plan corner and creating an unservable middle.

### EN-23 Validate comfort against the adaptive band, and give the deep-plan fraction a purge or a backup
- Rule: Where a space is claimed to be free-running or mixed-mode, check it against the adaptive comfort band rather than a fixed setpoint, and be explicit that the band only applies where occupants control the openings. Any floor area outside that reach needs a stated fallback before the plan is signed off, because comfort failure — not daylight failure — is what closes buildings.
- Evidence: The adaptive model and its limits are published: ASHRAE 55 adaptive comfort gives `Topt = 0.31 × prevailing mean outdoor temperature + 17.8` with an 80 % acceptability band of ±3.5 °C and a 90 % band of ±2.5 °C, applicable for prevailing means of roughly 10–33.5 °C; EN 16798-1 gives `Topt = 0.33 × running mean + 18.8` with Category I +2/−3 °C, Category II +3/−4 °C, Category III +4/−5 °C over a 10–30 °C range, both restricted to naturally ventilated, occupant-controlled spaces in the 1.0–1.5 met / 0.5–1.0 clo envelope. Passive-House-style overheating discipline is quantified as a criterion set: PHI "Classic" limits include ≤15 kWh/m²a heating demand, ≤10 W/m² peak heating load, ≤0.6 ACH₅₀ airtightness and ≤60 kWh/m²a renewable primary energy on treated floor area.
- Source: Climate4Buildings, *Adaptive Thermal Comfort Model: ASHRAE 55 and EN 16798* https://climate4buildings.com/guides/adaptive-thermal-comfort — T3; biobuilds, *Passive House Certification: criteria and classes* (PHI Classic) https://www.biobuilds.com/blog/passivhaus/certification — T4 (reporting the PHI criteria); WBDG, *Natural Ventilation* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/natural-ventilation — T1.
- Class: STANDARD
- Scope: all occupied types; the adaptive model is void for operating-theatre, laboratory, archive, data and cold-storage rooms
- Confidence: High that these are the published bands; Medium as reproduced here (secondary pages), so quote against the standards themselves before asserting a number in a report
- Grid translation: Cannot compute temperatures; instead compute the **exposure/backup partition**: `free_running_area_tiles` = tiles within the ventilation reach of an openable vent (EN-09/EN-19), `conditioned_backup_area = floor_tiles − free_running_area_tiles`. Validation: declare `strategy ∈ {fully_conditioned, mixed_mode, free_running}`; require `free_running_area ≥ 0.7 × floor` for `free_running`, and for `mixed_mode` require each non-reachable room to carry an explicit equipment record (EN-21). Also emit `occupant_control = true/false` per room: adaptive claims with `occupant_control = false` (locked façade, security-limited, tagged access) are reported as invalid, since the model's applicability condition fails.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Airtightness (a PHI criterion the grid cannot express at all) is the usual hidden dependency — a plan that maximises operable wall can be hard to seal. Record it as a construction consequence, not a plan benefit.

### EN-24 Report one daylight-sufficiency proxy and one excessive-sunlight proxy per floor, both in tiles
- Rule: Score every floor with two complementary numbers — the share of regularly occupied area that is usefully lit, and the share exposed to too much direct sun — because the second is the price of the first. Report them together, always with the metric definitions, or the pair will be gamed.
- Evidence: The two modern climate-based metrics are defined precisely: spatial daylight autonomy sDA(3/50%) is the percentage of the analysis area receiving at least 300 lux for at least 50 % of annual occupied hours, and annual sunlight exposure ASE counts area exceeding 1000 lux of *direct* sun for more than 250 occupied hours; the IES standard for both is LM-83, and LEED v4's daylight credit excludes from the study any area with ASE greater than 10 % (v4.1 relaxes this with a shading exception). The lux-based successor to daylight factor is already in the European standard: EN 17037's 300 lx median over ≥50 % of the reference plane and 100 lx over ≥95 %, for 2190 h — and VELUX's practice note recommends "minimum 300 lux for most of the room area" via a climate-based target. Note that the specific sDA percentage-to-points ladder could not be retrieved from the USGBC credit page this pass — do not quote point thresholds.
- Source: IES LM-83-12 (sDA and ASE) preview https://store.accuristech.com/ies/products/preview/1853773 — T1; ClimateStudio docs, *LEED v4 Daylight Option 1* (ASE = >1000 lux for >250 occupied hours; v4 excludes >10 % ASE areas) https://climatestudiodocs.com/docs/daylightLEEDOpt1.html — T3; USGBC, *Daylight* credit page https://www.usgbc.org/credits/new-construction-schools-new-construction-retail-new-construction-data-centers-new-9 — T1 (thresholds not machine-readable this pass); VELUX DEIC https://www.velux.com/healthy-buildings/research-and-knowledge/deic-basic-book/daylight/daylight-requirements-in-building-codes — T3.
- Class: STANDARD
- Scope: all types; the acceptable band differs sharply by type
- Confidence: High for the metric definitions; Medium for the exclusion percentages; Low for point thresholds (not retrieved)
- Grid translation: Define the tile proxies explicitly, since the host has no sky model: `sDA_proxy = |{tiles : glazed_within(d_daylight) AND exposure_band ≠ low_sun_glare_band}| / regularly_occupied_tiles`, and `ASE_proxy = |{tiles within 2 tiles of an equator-facing or E/W-band glazed run in the unshaded sub-band}| / regularly_occupied_tiles`. Validation: report the pair per floor and per revision, with `d_daylight` stated; require `ASE_proxy ≤ 0.10` of occupied tiles unless every tile above it carries a shading record (EN-11) or a re-classified use; require both numbers to move together or the daylight gain is being bought with glare.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Skylit and borrowed-light areas are excluded from ASE by definition (they receive no direct sun through a vertical opening) — a proxy that ignores this will over-penalise toplight schemes. Retail, atrium and pool halls legitimately run high.

### EN-25 Treat the façade band and the interior band as different HVAC zones with different equipment
- Rule: The same plan geometry that creates a daylit band creates a high-load band: the perimeter swings with the sun and the outside air, the core only carries internal gains. Model the floor as at least (N, E, S, W, core) zones, size each band's equipment from its own area and glazing, and let that drive the riser and route design — a single all-floor system is the sign that the zoning was never done.
- Evidence: Code-anchored inputs exist for each term of the zone load: glazing limits and SHGC by orientation (IECC C402.5.3, which carries orientation-specific fenestration provisions and skylight exceptions), the geometric daylight zone that lighting controls must follow (C405.2.3.2, EN-20), and the ventilation rate procedure whose people-plus-area terms set the airflow (EN-21). WBDG's orientation statements supply the qualitative direction: south is the beneficial face and the one easiest to control. The specific perimeter-band depth commonly quoted in energy-modelling practice (15 ft ≈ 4.6 m) is **`source NOT established`** and **Confidence: Low** — this file previously leaned on ASHRAE 90.1 *Appendix G*, which audit 05 A2 identifies as the wrong document family (Appendix G is the Performance Rating Method; the perimeter-zone convention belongs to the ASHRAE Handbook *Fundamentals*, which is not open-access). Three open-source probes this pass did not retrieve a quotable page carrying the metre figure, so no clause is being guessed. The code-derived daylight band of 1.0 × head height (EN-20) is the defensible stand-in; the 9-tile / 4.6 m band is a flagged design convention only.
- Source: UpCodes, *C402.5.3 Maximum U-factor and SHGC* https://up.codes/s/maximum-u-factor-and-shgc — T1; UpCodes, *Sidelight Daylight Zone* https://up.codes/s/sidelight-daylight-zone — T1; WBDG, *Daylighting* https://legacy.wbdg.org/resources/daylighting — T1; ASHRAE 62.1 formula as above — T1.
- Class: ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT
- Scope: all mechanically conditioned types
- Confidence: High for the zoning principle and its code inputs; Low for the 4.6 m perimeter depth
- Grid translation: Partition the floor into `zone_tiles` sets: four perimeter bands of declared depth (default = the 4-tile code daylight band; alternative 9-tile/4.6 m band, flagged) plus `core = floor − Σ perimeter_bands`. Per band report `area_m² = tiles × 0.25`, `glazed_tiles`, `occupancy`, and derive `airflow_L/s` and a unit-count proxy. Validation: (a) `Σ band_area = floor_area` with no double counting at corners (assign corner tiles to the higher-load band and state the rule); (b) each band's service demand reachable from a riser (EN-21); (c) report the equipment asymmetry — core needs continuous cooling, perimeter needs heating diversity — as a consequence of the shape, so a plan that fattens the core raises internal gains and lowers perimeter loads and must be read as a mechanical decision.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Small buildings, single-tenant shells and all-air systems with VAV may run one zone; then declare it and drop the check. Symptom: an agent maximising "efficient compact shape" while ignoring that a fat core in a warm climate is a permanent internal-gain machine.

### EN-26 Publish the assumption block; no environmental rule may be scored on an undeclared input
- Rule: Because the grid carries no heights, no section, no materials, no climate and no sun, every environmental statement this file produces is conditional on a declared assumption set. Emit that set with the design — storey height, sill/head, glazing area per façade tile, openable area per vent tile, daylight multiplier in use, WWR caps, exposure ranks, ventilation regime — and refuse to report any percentage without it.
- Evidence: The disagreement among authoritative sources is itself the reason. The same quantity, "how deep does sidelight reach", is published as 1.0 × head height (IECC/90.1 daylight zone), 1.5 × head height (BRANZ), ~2.5 × the glazing height (WBDG), and as a lux-hours criterion with no depth multiplier at all (EN 17037 / LM-83 sDA). Orientation tolerance is given as ±15° (WBDG) and ±30° (BRE-derived practice). Cross-ventilation reach is given as 45 ft (WBDG) and as 4–5 × ceiling height, i.e. 10.8–13.5 m (CIBSE AM10 via NCC practice). A percentage reported without its inputs is not comparable across revisions and cannot be audited.
- Source: as cited in EN-03, EN-04, EN-02, EN-09 above (WBDG, UpCodes, BRANZ, REHVA/EN 15251, CCC/NCC, Anstey Horne/BRE).
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: universal
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Maintain a `declared_assumptions` record alongside every design output; store it in the project state so a revision diff is meaningful (identical assumptions → comparable scores). Validation: every metric line in the report carries a pointer to its assumption; every metric whose assumption changed between revisions is reported as *not comparable* rather than as an improvement.
- Exceptions / failure mode: The degenerate case is a design brief with genuinely unknown heights — then report the metric as a *function* of the assumed storey height (e.g. "single-aspect cap = 6 tiles at 3.0 m floor-to-floor; 8 tiles at 3.6 m") rather than choosing silently.

## Climate → orientation → plan chain

The spec's chain, made operational. Each step has an input, a decision, the tile measure that proves it, the artefact it emits, and the rules that bind. Run it top to bottom; the failure mode of this domain is entering at step 5.

**1. Climate** (input, not a decision) → declare `governing_load`, `hemisphere`, `prevailing_wind` (or "unknown"), `ventilation_viable`, `external_noise_sources`. Emit `climate_profile`. Rules: EN-01, EN-10 (site noise is part of the climate record here). Grid artefact: a key/value record. If `governing_load` is blank, stop.

**2. Orientation** → rotate the plate so the *useful* façade gets the long dimension and the *shallow* axis carries the flow path. Decide the tolerance and record the achieved bearing. Rules: EN-02, EN-09, EN-13. Measures: `façade_tiles_by_direction`, `dev_from_equator`, `narrow_axis_bearing`, `low_sun_band_share`. Artefact: the orientation table — tiles per band × exposure rank × solar band weight. Chain effect: this table is the *budget* every later step spends against.

**3. Envelope (shape/compactness)** → choose the plate outline: depth, aspect, notches, wings, courtyard. Compactness is traded against reach, so both must be reported together. Rules: EN-03, EN-14, EN-08. Measures: `plate_depth`, `P/A`, `φ`, `notch_count`, `opposed_aspect_share`, `shadow_band_tiles`, `shaded_share` per band. Artefact: 2–3 candidate outlines with the paired daylight/gain metrics from EN-24 — never one.

**4. Openings** → place glazing and vent runs on the chosen outline: how much, where, at what orientation, shaded or not. Rules: EN-06, EN-11, EN-12, EN-19, EN-20. Measures: `glazed_tiles/façade_tiles` per band → WWR, `vent_tiles_available ≥ 0.0227×room_tiles`, `control_band` tiles, `openable_fraction`. Artefact: the façade allocation sheet: per band, glazed tiles, vent tiles, shaded tiles, and the rooms that inherit them.

**5. Room placement** → spend the allocation: top-value rooms on the equator-facing, quiet, shaded bands; non-daylight programmes in the shadow band; buffers between noisy and sensitive. Rules: EN-04, EN-05, EN-07, EN-10, EN-16, EN-17, EN-18. Measures: `daylit_share` per room, `wall_planes`, `path_tiles`, `door_share`, `aligned_door_pairs`, `loud_band_habitable_share`, the EN-18 gap register. Artefact: the gap register — every room whose target is unmet, with the reason and the lever left.

**6. Circulation** → the leftover bands are circulation's; make that a decision rather than a residue. Corridors are the standard absorber of bad façade (noise, low sun, no view) and double as acoustic buffers and duct routes. Rules: EN-07, EN-16, EN-19 (borrowed air), EN-21. Measures: circulation in the loud/exposed band, corridor width vs duct reservation, `buffer_room_between` coverage for high-ΔL pairs, threshold count unchanged or improved. Artefact: circulation plan with its dual purpose annotated.

**7. Mechanical systems** → convert the residual gaps into equipment and routes, and report the cost of the plan shape in plant terms. Rules: EN-21, EN-22, EN-23, EN-25. Measures: `free_running_area_tiles` vs `conditioned_backup_area`, per-band airflow and unit counts, riser alignment, `underserved_zones`. Artefact: `mechanical_load_statements` — one line per gap, each naming the plan change that would have removed it.

**Chain inversion table** — what changes when the climate declaration changes. The same levers, different signs:

| Lever | Heating-dominated (cold) | Cooling-dominated (hot-dry) | Hot-humid | Temperate mixed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Long axis | equator-facing, wide glazing on that band | N–S long axis, protect E/W | either, but shallow plate | equator-facing within ±15–30° |
| Envelope | compact: minimise `P/A`, few junctions | compact + massive: low `P/A`, high attenuation | **open**: maximise `P/A` of the flow path, low enclosure | moderate `P/A` |
| Openings | large glass on the equator band, capped elsewhere | small openings, high-level, deeply shaded | large openings on flow bands, low SHGC needed | operable everywhere |
| Room placement | daylit rooms on the equator band; services to the pole band | sleep/long-occupancy off E/W; shaded courtyards | long-occupancy on the flow path, cross-vent units | dual-strategy rooms (purge + glass) |
| Circulation | thermal buffer/lobby on the exposed band | shaded court, covered route as the sun path | corridor as the stack/venturi route, open ends | corridor as both buffer and purge path |
| Mechanical | heating distribution; perimeter units on glass bands | low plant, high occupant control; backup for dust/storm | dehumidification dominates — ventilation limits bite hardest | mixed-mode plant + controls (the code daylight zones still apply) |

Note the honest caveat the table conceals: on this host only the *plan* column of each row is actionable. Mass, attenuation, SHGC and airtightness are not modelled, so where the table implies them, the corresponding claim is recorded as unverifiable (EN-15, EN-26).

## Weak or contested

- **The daylight depth multiplier is not one number — it is four.** Code (IECC/90.1) says the daylit zone is 1.0 × head height; BRANZ says useful light reaches ~1.5 ×; WBDG says ~2.5 × the *glazing height*; EN 17037 rejects depth multipliers entirely in favour of lux-hours. On the host grid those are 4, 6, 6 and "not computable" tiles. This file's default (6 tiles for long-occupancy rooms) is a *choice*, deliberately in the middle, and must be reported as such (EN-04, EN-26). Do not present the 2–2.5 × head-height figure the brief names as a standard: the retrieved WBDG sentence is 2.5 × the distance between head and sill, which is a smaller number at typical sill heights.
- **±15° vs ±30° from due south.** Both are published (WBDG 15°, BRE-derived practice 30°). They are not in conflict so much as aimed at different purposes — solar *control* versus solar *access* — but the skill should not quote either without saying which problem is being solved.
- **Cross-ventilation reach: 45 ft vs 4–5 × ceiling height.** WBDG's 45 ft (13.7 m ≈ 27 tiles) is a plan-width statement; the CIBSE AM10-derived 4–5 × height is a *section*-dependent statement that collapses to 11–13.5 m at 2.7 m clear but to 18 m at 3.6 m. They agree at ordinary office ceiling heights and disagree in tall spaces. Report both numbers, and report which ceiling height the design assumed.
- **WWR conclusions do not transfer across climates.** The +100 kWh per +20 % WWR result is one simulated office in subtropical southern Hunan; the "20–40 % of façade gives the most daylight" result is one glare survey. Both are directionally useful (more glass costs energy and can exceed comfort) and neither should be quoted as a universal optimum. There is no retrieved source that supports a single global WWR cap; EN-12's band values are declared targets, not code.
- **External-vs-internal shading: only the direction survives.** The qualitative ordering — external rejects gain before it enters, internal blinds stop glare but not heat — is the T1 WBDG statement and is what EN-11 now carries. The "12–18 °C vs 8–13 °C peak reduction" magnitudes previously attached to EN-11 came from a shutter/awning vendor's trade-association campaign blog citing an unnamed BBSA-supported study with no reference list, no construction type, and no boundary conditions; audit 05 A4 judged them implausible as peak *operative-temperature* reductions in a heavyweight conditioned room. They have been **removed** — Confidence Medium on the direction, magnitude **not established**; retrieve a measured simulation or field study before restoring any number here, and never restore "independent" as a descriptor of that source.
- **Adaptive comfort coefficients were read from a vendor page, not from ASHRAE 55 or EN 16798.** The forms (`0.31 T + 17.8`, ±3.5/±2.5 °C; `0.33 T + 18.8`, Cat I/II/III) are consistent with what those standards are widely documented to contain, but the primary text was not retrieved: quote against the standard before publishing a number. The *applicability* conditions (naturally ventilated, occupant-controlled, 10–33.5 °C) matter more to this project than the coefficients do, because locked façades and tagged access invalidate the model (EN-23).
- **ASHRAE 62.1 per-type ventilation rates: deliberately absent.** The Rate Procedure structure (people term + area term, divided by zone effectiveness) is confirmed; the Table 6-1 numbers are not, from any source retrieved this pass. EN-21 and EN-25 therefore ask the agent to declare Rp/Ra as inputs rather than inherit them. The same caution applies to every "X L/s per person" figure other than EN 15251's 2.5–7 L/s band.
- **Hospital shared-vs-single room noise is confounded.** The 49.9 dB / 44.7 dB averages and the 2–3× event count are consistent with the layout hypothesis (corridor and bed-mate proximity), but they come from a review of studies differing in unit type, staffing model, case mix and measurement protocol. Use them to argue *direction* (shared rooms are louder and noisier in events), not to promise a dB saving from a layout change. Ulrich's 1984 view study is used the same way and additionally carries long-standing methodological criticism (small retrospective sample, non-random allocation as re-analysed in later literature) — directional support only, never a number.
- **NC ratings and EN 12464-1 lux/UGR tables were read from vendor reproductions of standards.** Values are plausible and mutually consistent, but the tiers are T3/T4, so the type-specific table below is a *starting* assignment for the agent, not a citation to a specification.
- **Thermal-bridge magnitudes could not be sourced.** EN-14 and EN-26 assert only that a less compact plan has more envelope and more junctions. The commonly quoted "10–30 % of heat loss" and the default `y`-value of ~0.1–0.2 W/m²K were **not** retrieved from BR 262, EN ISO 14683 or any national calculation document this pass, so no percentage of that kind may be claimed.
- **The 4.6 m / 15 ft perimeter zone depth is `source NOT established` (Confidence: Low).** This file previously pointed at ASHRAE 90.1 *Appendix G*, which audit 05 A2 identifies as the wrong document family: Appendix G is the Performance Rating Method, and the 15 ft figure is an air-conditioning design convention belonging to the ASHRAE Handbook *Fundamentals* perimeter-zone practice — a different document. Three open-source probes this pass (energycodes.gov prototypes, an OpenStudio prototype-model PDF, and two search sweeps) did not yield a quotable page carrying the figure, and no clause is being guessed. The code-correct daylight-zone alternative (`1.0 × head height = 4 tiles` from IECC/ASHRAE 90.1 §9.3.3 sidelighting logic; the C405.2.3.2 daylight-zone definition in EN-20) remains the defensible stand-in, and the 9-tile / 4.6 m band is offered only as a flagged heuristic option. Do not restore the metre value as a citation until an ASHRAE Handbook *Fundamentals* page or a code official-text screenshot establishes it.
- **Whole-category limitation: everything here is a proxy.** The grid has no heights, no section, no sun path, no materials, no climate data and no window tile. sDA, ASE, DGP, UGR, D₂,S, DnT,w, SHGC and operative temperature are all *unmeasurable* on this host; the tile proxies in this file correlate with them by assumption, and the assumptions are listed in EN-26. An agent that reports "sDA 62 %" from a tile count is lying; it may report "62 % of tiles within 6 tiles of a glazed run".
- **Gaps this pass could not close.** No primary source for: any shading-device dimension table (Olgyay/ASHE); the BRE 2H/3H and solar-azimuth sunlight criteria in their own words; openable-area rules outside Australia; the Scottish/UK solar-gain glazing-limit formula (which would have given a genuine orientation-weighted glazing cap — highest-value follow-up); museum/gallery and clinical room lux limits (needed to complete the type table below).

## Type-specificity audit

Environmental demand is where building type bites hardest: the same façade, the same plan depth and the same wall line are a good office and a bad hotel. Retrieved anchors for the differences: EN 12464-1 per-type lighting (office 500 lx/UGR 19 · classroom 300/19 · ward 100/19 · examination 500/19/Ra 90 · retail 300/22 · **hotel room 100/22**) — https://www.techlumen.gr/en/guide/lighting-standards-guide/; WHO bedroom <30 dB(A) night and classroom <35 dB(A) — https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/noise; practice NC bands (private office 30–35, open plan 35–40, patient room 25–35) — https://commercial-acoustics.com/sound-advice/noise-criteria-nc-rating-chart/; England Part E separation values (40 dB Rw internal, 45 dB DnT,w+Ctr separating) — https://plansmadeeasy.org/building-regulations-part-e/; measured ward noise (shared 49.9 dB vs single 44.7 dB, 2–3× events/night) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11107945/; domestic daylight targets (BREEAM ≥1.5 % average DF in living rooms; Denmark 10 → 15 % glass/floor; BRANZ ≥10 % and 30 lx for 75 % of the year) — VELUX DEIC, BRANZ Level.

| Plan move | Hotel floor | Hospital floor | Office floor | Residential floor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deep plate (>27 tiles) | Tolerated only if corridors, not guest rooms; every bedroom beyond reach becomes a blackout + mechanical room, i.e. a product downgrade | Normal: day rooms and wards on the perimeter, services and circulation inboard; but the core must be riser-served, not just empty | The classic deep-plate office: pays EN-20 control zones and EN-25 core cooling; viable to 36 tiles (daylight cap, rounded DOWN) | Rarely acceptable: single-aspect rooms beyond 6 tiles fail the domestic daylight floor (EN-06) |

| Corridor absorbs the loud/no-view band | Strongly right: also the acoustic buffer between lifts and rooms (EN-16, EN-17) | Right, and it is the clean/dirty route too | Right: also the service and riser route | Neutral: short corridors, so little band to spend |

| Interior rooms accepted | Only as back-of-house (linen, staff, plant) — never sold | Expected (store, plant, sterile storage, some imaging) | Expected (meeting rooms, print, phone booths, stairs) | Amenity failure and often a code failure |

For this repository's hotel subject specifically: EN-05, EN-06, EN-08, EN-13, EN-16 and EN-17 are the rules where a hotel plan systematically diverges from an office plan, and the divergence is driven by three facts — the guest room is a *sleep* room (so noise and darkness targets dominate the daylight target, per WHO/EN 12464-1 above), it is repeated on every floor (so the party-wall and door-run acoustics compound across the whole stack), and it is sold individually (so an unlit or loud room is a revenue loss, not a planning compromise). An agent that applies the office reading of "good façade = more glass" to a hotel floor will produce a plan that scores well on every daylight proxy in this file and fails the type.
