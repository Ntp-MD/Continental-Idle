# Research file — Domain AN: Inclusive-use sequencing and measurable accessibility outcomes

Scope: the **usability-sequence layer** — the trips a design must let a disabled, older, accompanying
or cognitively-impaired person *complete*, and the plan-observable properties that decide each one.
The dimensional minima already owned elsewhere are NOT restated here, not once:
`human-scale.md` HS-01…HS-28 owns body/fixture clearances, turning squares, door clear widths, latch-side
maneuvering, sanitary clear floor space, reach bands, effective width and route continuity as geometry;
`building-codes.md` CODE-18…CODE-23 owns the accessible-route width, door maneuvering table, ramp slopes,
reach ranges, refuge scoping and the hospitality accessible-room ladder; CODE-27/HS-24 own tile rounding;
`spatial-analytics.md` SA-03…SA-25 own integration/choice/isovist arithmetic and the flag idiom;
`fire-safety-quantification.md` FQ-04…FQ-21 own flow, ASET/RSET and refuge sizing arithmetic;
`multi-scale.md` MS-16 owns the cross-floor accessible-route portal test;
`hotel-operating-standards.md` HO-05 owns the accessible-key scoping table. Where a number is needed
below, the rule cites that ID and moves to the sequence consequence. This file is the only one that asks
"can this person actually do the thing", and it declares the gap in `coverage-map.md` §5 closed only for
the sequencing part — the code-minima part stays where it is.

Host grid, unchanged from the corpus: `1 tile = 0.5 m`; `4 tiles = 1 m²`; tile states ONLY
`walkable | blocked | door`; wall = one blocked tile; rooms = 4-connected flood-fill of walkable tiles;
doors = door-tile runs belonging to no room; movement = octile A* (diagonals only when both corner
neighbours are open); vertical = lift/stair portals with queues; NO heights, NO sections, NO furniture
layer (HS-27), NO luminance or acoustic channel.

## Rules

### AX-01 The unit of accessibility is a completed trip, not a satisfied dimension
- Rule: Decompose every occupancy into an ordered task chain — arrive from street → threshold → ticket/reception/queue → vertical travel → corridor → door → bathroom transfer → bed transfer → seating → dining/servery → emergency egress with assistance — and score the design per link. A link passes only if the user can start it, do it, recover from it and leave it without a step, a third-party rescue, or abandoning the device. Publish the chain, not a compliance percentage.
- Evidence: The lived-experience literature consistently reports failure at links, not at dimensions: an interpretive-phenomenological study of 20 community-dwelling adults aging with long-term physical disabilities (mean age 59.8; 18 used a mobility aid — 10 cane/walker, 6 powered wheelchairs, 2 manual) found that "faulty residential entry systems that required external rescue to re-enter effectively imprisoned several individuals", that degraded pavement, absent curb transitions, poor illumination and missing resting benches "elevated injury risks and deterred travel", and that poorly planned wheelchair routes forced "extensive pre-trip planning" and eliminated spontaneous outings (opened). A scoping review of 21 publications (study samples n = 4 to n = 2,762) recorded "lack of transfer accessibility" and missing ramps/door handles/curb cuts as the mechanisms limiting participation, with one included framework explaining 63 % of the variance in participation. The Universal Design principle that governs this is "Size and space for approach and use … accommodate … the use of assistive devices or personal assistance".
- Source: Heeb Desai, Hamlin, Eyler, Putnam, Stark, Doering & Morgan, *The Role of the Built Environment in the Community Participation of Adults Aging With Long-Term Physical Disabilities: A Scoping Review*, J Aging Environ 2023 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10961975/ — T2 (opened); same group, qualitative IPA study, *Disability & Society* 2023 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11872231/ — T2 (opened); Centre for Excellence in Universal Design (National Disability Authority, Ireland), *The 7 Principles* https://universaldesign.ie/about-universal-design/the-7-principles — T2 (opened; principle 7 and its guidelines quoted).
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: universal; the link set is type-specific (see ## Task-chain register)
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: For each user class and floor, emit `trip_links[]` = {link, room_ids, door_runs, route_tiles, verdict}, and `first_broken_link` = the earliest failing link. Report the chain; never average link verdicts into a percentage.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A single-storey, grade-entry building legitimately has no vertical-travel link — omit it explicitly rather than passing it vacuously. Cross-referenced IDs used as inputs throughout this file: HS-*/CODE-* (dimensions and duties), MS-16 (cross-floor route), HO-05 (accessible-key scoping), FQ-19/FQ-21 (assisted egress and refuge arithmetic), SA-*/HB-* (metrics, decision points, queues), PP-22 (route erosion). Symptom: a floor that "meets accessibility" in 22 of 24 checks and is unusable because the bathroom door cannot be locked from inside.

### AX-02 Report the first broken link, never a compliance score
- Rule: The headline of an inclusive-use report is `first_broken_link` with its geometry and its user class, plus the list of links that were never testable on the grid. A summary percentage is admissible only alongside it. Where a link cannot be evaluated, emit `unknown` with the missing input named — the corpus's three-valued verdict rule applies here.
- Evidence: The accessibility-research record is unambiguous that partial credit hides the failure: the Australian public-bathroom study found users "avoided disabled bathrooms due to limited space" and that approach was "heavily disrupted by poor layouts that force meticulous outing planning" — i.e. a facility scoring well on fixture provision still terminated the trip upstream. Code agrees structurally: 2010 ADA Standards §206.3 requires that "accessible routes shall coincide with or be located in the same area as general circulation paths", so an alternative accessible path that is not the one everybody uses does not satisfy the route duty.
- Source: Access Board, *2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design* ch. 2 §206.3 (T1, US, 2010; opened this session with the §206 sub-clauses read verbatim) https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch02/ ; public-bathroom usability findings as reported in *Access Insight* (Autumn 2024) summarising University of Technology Sydney research (3D scanning, video re-enactment, interviews, online survey of 200 Australian wheelchair users) https://access.asn.au/access-insight-autumn-2024-new-uts-research-lifts-the-lid-on-how-wheelchair-users-access-public-bathrooms-reprint/ — T3 (secondary report of a T2 study; primary paper not opened). This repo: `validation.md` VA-03 (three-valued verdicts).
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE (report contract)
- Scope: universal
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Emit `inclusive_use_report = {user_class, first_broken_link:{link_id, tile_xy, room_id, reason}, unknown_links:[{link_id, missing_input}]}`. A report containing a numeric score without a `first_broken_link` field or an explicit `none` fails review.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A design can be genuinely blocked by a missing input (no furniture layer, HS-27) — say so; symptom is a green dashboard on a plan with no declared bed, so no transfer space exists to check.

### AX-03 Keep a per-user-class trip ledger; the classes differ in chain, not in width
- Rule: Run the chain once per user class — wheelchair/scooter user, walker-or-rollator user, ambulant-with-pain user, parent with a pushchair, low-vision user, blind or low-vision-plus-cognitive user, someone who needs staff assistance, and an unaccompanied child — and record which links each class *cannot* complete unaided. Do not treat "accessible" as one boolean.
- Evidence: WHO's age-friendly framework is built on eight domains of the environment as experienced by older people — "outdoor spaces and buildings, transportation, housing, social participation, respect and social inclusion, civic participation and employment, communication and information, and community support and health services" — and the multi-city focus-group work behind it found that "physical accessibility, service proximity, security, affordability, and inclusiveness were important characteristics everywhere" across 8 groups per city in 11 cities in 11 countries (148 participants, 99 women / 49 men, aged 55+; 229 features elicited, 48 named by more than a third of groups, 20 shared across all four domains). The measured-device literature shows the classes are physically distinct: scooters are the longest device category measured, not manual chairs.
- Source: WHO, *Age-friendly Cities framework* (8 domains) https://extranet.who.int/agefriendlyworld/age-friendly-cities-framework/ — T1 (opened); Plouffe & Kalache, *Towards Global Age-Friendly Cities: Determining Urban Features that Promote Active Aging*, J Urban Health 2010 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2937125/ — T2 (opened; participant counts as rendered on the page — see ## Weak or contested item 1); D'Souza, Steinfeld, Paquet & Feathers, *Space Requirements for Wheeled Mobility Devices in Public Transportation*, Transp. Res. Rec. 2145(1):66–71, 2010, DOI 10.3141/2145-08 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8375507/ — T2 (opened).
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: universal
- Confidence: High (method), Medium (the specific WHO sample rendering)
- Grid translation: `class_ledger[cls] = {links_completed_unaided, links_needing_assistance, links_impossible}`; emit a matrix with classes as rows and links as columns, three-valued. Cross-check the assistance column against the staffing model (FQ-19 for fire, HO-* for service).
- Exceptions / failure mode: Some classes legitimately require assistance (AX-24); the design error is making assistance the *only* way through a routine link. Symptom: an "accessible" entrance whose only compliant door is power-operated and locked behind a staff desk.

### AX-04 Test every pinch point against the largest routine device, not the code's reference device
- Rule: For each pinch (door run, corridor bottleneck, lift landing, aisle, space beside a bed or WC), ask whether the plan admits the measured 95th-percentile device of the class the space actually serves, not the anthropometric reference device the standard drew. Publish the governing device and its footprint with every pass claim. Where the space is dimensioned from the reference device only, mark the pass as `reference-device pass` and state the residual risk.
- Evidence: A coordinate-probe study of 369 adults (195 manual-wheelchair users, 146 powered-chair users, 28 scooter riders) measured occupied device envelopes: manual chairs mean 681 mm wide (SD 59) with a 95th percentile of 782 mm, and 1 141 mm long (SD 131) / 1 342 mm; powered chairs 707 (SD 75) / 828 mm wide and 1 196 (SD 123) / 1 399 mm long; mobility scooters 650 (SD 89) / 840 mm wide and 1 212 (SD 107) / 1 435 mm long. The authors conclude that the clear floor space prescribed by US transportation accessibility standards is inadequate and recommend 860 mm × 1 480 mm (34 in × 58 in) to accommodate ≥ 95 % of users. Their own limitations are stated: relaxed 15–20-minute seated posture, northern-climate clothing, static rectangles with no dynamic turning, and a small scooter cohort.
- Source: D'Souza, Steinfeld, Paquet & Feathers 2010 (T2, opened; values as reported there). Jurisdiction of the studied population: United States transit users.
- Class: STANDARD (measured device envelope) / FACT (the tabulated values as published)
- Scope: universal; the governing device varies by use (scooters in transit and retail, powered chairs in care, pushchairs in family hospitality)
- Confidence: High for the direction (real devices exceed the reference device), Medium for the exact envelope (single study, self-declared limitations)
- Grid translation: The measured envelope 860 × 1 480 mm rounds up to 2 × 3 tiles (1.0 × 1.5 m); the 95th-percentile *scooter* length 1 435 mm needs the same 3 tiles. So the "largest routine device" test on this grid is `rect 2×3 clear, plus the approach axis free`, and any pinch narrower than 2 tiles in the device's travel direction is a fail for device users. Compute against the furniture layer (HS-27), not the room outline.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Bariatric and large-outdoor-power devices sit above the measured 95th percentile (the study's own distribution has a long tail); a plan that admits exactly 860 × 1 480 mm is a 95 % plan, not a 100 % plan. Symptom: "meets the accessible WC dimension" with a 1 435 mm scooter and one tile of door reveal.

### AX-05 Where the code's clear-floor unit and the measured device disagree, buy the extra tile
- Rule: Compare the clear-floor unit that HS-07/HS-21 already fix on this grid against the measured device envelope of AX-04 and state the deficit per fixture class; where the deficit exists, the design decision is to add a tile rather than to argue the point. Record the choice and its cost in tiles, so the plan's residual risk is visible.
- Evidence: The corpus's clear-floor unit is transcribed in HS-07 from ADA §305, with its tile translation given there, and the turning square in HS-04/HS-08. The measured-device data (AX-04) put the 95th-percentile *width* of a powered chair and a scooter above the width limb HS-07 records — i.e. the width limb is the one under pressure, and the study's recommended width exceeds the transcribed unit. This is a real disagreement between two instruments, not a conversion error, and per the corpus rule against averaging conflicts both numbers stay published and the *wider* one becomes the design test where the space serves device users.
- Source: This repo: `human-scale.md` HS-04, HS-07, HS-08, HS-21, HS-24 (numbers owned there, not restated here); D'Souza et al. 2010 (T2, opened) for the measured widths; Access Board 2010 ADA Standards ch. 3 §305 https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch03/ — T1 (US, 2010), cited as the instrument HS-07 transcribes.
- Class: HEURISTIC (the decision rule) on STANDARD inputs (both numbers)
- Scope: any space whose user is seated in a device
- Confidence: Medium-High
- Grid translation: `device_admits_clear_space = (clear_w_tiles × 0.5 ≥ 0.86) and (clear_l_tiles × 0.5 ≥ 1.48)`; report `deficit_tiles` per fixture so the reviewer sees what a 1-tile upgrade costs. Where the fixture row already passes HS-07's unit but fails this test, emit `reference_device_pass_only = true`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: In a small dwelling bathroom the upgrade may be impossible without losing the turning square (HS-04) — then the honest output is a documented trade-off, not a redefinition of the measured envelope. Symptom: a plan that claims "fits a wheelchair" off a 0.76 m-wide unit that a 828 mm powered chair does not enter.

### AX-06 Which side you can enter a space from is a separate requirement from its area
- Rule: For every standing, transfer or parking space, publish its entry edges — forward, lateral-left, lateral-right, or blocked — alongside its area, and require at least one entry edge to be continuous with the route. A space that is big enough and unenterable is the characteristic grid-blind failure.
- Evidence: The standard itself makes entry direction dimensional: 2010 ADA Standards §802.1 gives a single wheelchair space as "36 inches (915 mm) wide minimum", and §802.1.3 requires that "where a wheelchair space can be entered only from the side, the wheelchair space shall be 60 inches (1525 mm) deep minimum" — the same function costs 50 % more depth purely because of the entry edge. §802.1.5 adds that "wheelchair spaces shall not overlap circulation paths", which is the entry-edge rule expressed as a graph constraint. The bathroom usability study found the interior failure to be objects and layout ("accessories like dryers blocking grab rails", "interior space often cramped") rather than missing square metres.
- Source: Access Board, 2010 ADA Standards ch. 8 §802.1, §802.1.3, §802.1.5 — T1 (US, 2010, opened) https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch08/ ; *Access Insight* Autumn 2024 reporting UTS research (T3, opened; primary study not opened).
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (the §802 asymmetry, US) / DESIGN PRINCIPLE (the generalisation)
- Scope: universal; the numeric asymmetry is US assembly seating
- Confidence: High (quoted clauses); Medium (generalisation beyond assembly seating)
- Grid translation: For each declared space, compute `entry_edges ⊆ {N,E,S,W}` from adjacent walkable tiles not occupied by another required space, and require `|entry_edges ∩ route_edges| ≥ 1`. Emit `space_id, area_tiles, entry_edges, depth_needed_tiles`. Cross-check with SA-22's aperture-slot logic rather than re-deriving it.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Corner spaces legitimately have two entry edges; a space entered only across another user's clear floor space (HS-07's overlap note) has none. Symptom: a "2 × 3 transfer zone" drawn behind a door swing.

### AX-07 The door is a five-step sequence; the plan must survive all five
- Rule: Model door use as approach → orient to the correct side → operate the hardware → control the leaf → resume propulsion, and test each step against what the plan already fixes in HS-05/HS-06 and CODE-19. The usability additions are: hardware must be reachable and operable with one hand while seated, the leaf must be controllable without a free hand being needed to hold it, and the recovery space on the far side must exist *with the leaf open*.
- Evidence: The measured satisfaction data are harsh on this link: in the Australian study of 200 wheelchair users, the door-and-lock step was the reported failure — "doors and locks, where operation and security scored under 50 % satisfaction". The standards answer structurally: 2010 ADA §703.4.2 places the tactile room sign "alongside the door at the latch side", which is only useful if the latch side is reachable, and §505.4/§505.10.1 require handrail gripping surfaces at "34 inches (865 mm) minimum" top height extending "12 inches (305 mm) minimum" horizontally above the landing — a continuous handrail is the device that lets a rollator or low-vision user survive steps 1 and 5. Interior door opening force and maneuvering clearances are already owned by HS-06/CODE-19 and are not restated.
- Source: *Access Insight* Autumn 2024 (UTS research, n = 200 survey) — T3 (opened; primary not opened); Access Board 2010 ADA Standards §703.4.2 (T1, opened) https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch07/ and §505.4, §505.10.1 (T1, opened) https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch05/ — US, 2010. This repo: HS-05, HS-06, CODE-19 (duty and clearances).
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE (sequence) / CODE REQUIREMENT (quoted dimensions)
- Scope: every door on an inclusive route; hospitality guest-room and bathroom doors are the highest-frequency cases
- Confidence: High for the standard's clauses, Medium for the < 50 % figure (single secondary report)
- Grid translation: Per door: `latch_side_tile_identified` (requires a declared hinge side in the furniture/door register — HS-27), `approach_tiles_with_leaf_open`, `recovery_tiles_beyond_run ≥ entry_tiles`, `hardware_reachable_from_route` (flag; AFF is off-grid), `power_or_low_effort` (flag). A door with no declared hinge side cannot be evaluated: emit `unknown`, not `pass`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Sliding and automatic leaves remove steps 2 and 4 but add a dwell-time dependency on the lobby queue (MS-23/VT-*). Symptom: a hotel guest room whose door closes and latches behind a seated user with 1 tile of recovery space.

### AX-08 Queue, servery and waiting links must not be a separate accessible world
- Rule: Where the chain passes a queue, a counter or a servery, the accessible position is the same position everyone else reaches — same head of the queue, same counter height range, same view of the screen or stage. Provide the waiting geometry inside the queue body, not in a bay off to one side, and give the wheelchair position the same shelter and the same sight of the service point as the standing position.
- Evidence: §206.3 requires accessible routes to "coincide with or be located in the same area as general circulation paths"; §221.2.1/§221.2.3 scope wheelchair spaces by seat count (the render read shows, for example, 6 spaces for a 301–500-seat assembly) with "at least one companion seat complying with 802.3 … for each wheelchair space" and companion seats "equivalent in size, quality, comfort, and amenities to the seating in the immediate area", plus §802.2 lines-of-sight protection — the code's own model of access is *equivalence in place*, which is Universal Design principle 1 ("Provide the same means of use for all users: identical whenever possible; equivalent when not"; "Avoid segregating or stigmatizing any users"). §902.3 sets the reachable surface itself: "the tops of dining surfaces and work surfaces shall be 28 inches minimum and 34 inches maximum above the finish floor". HB-10 owns the queue pocket sizing; the equity placement is this rule's addition.
- Source: Access Board 2010 ADA Standards §206.3 (ch. 2, T1, opened), §221.2.1/§221.2.3 (ch. 2, T1, page render partial — see ## Weak or contested item 4), §802.2/§802.3 (ch. 8, T1, opened), §902.3 (ch. 9, T1, opened); Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, *The 7 Principles* (T2, opened).
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (US, quoted limbs) / DESIGN PRINCIPLE (the generalisation)
- Scope: reception, ticketing, F&B servery, bars, theatre and lounge seating, baggage and check-in
- Confidence: High for the quoted clauses, Medium for the table rows the page rendered only partially
- Grid translation: Emit `queue_pocket_adjacent_to_accessible_route = true/false`; `wheelchair_position_has_companion_tile` (a route-width-compliant tile adjacent, per HS-03's two-way band cited by ID); `sight_of_service_point` via SA-06/SA-09 covisibility, computed from the position tile rather than the room centroid. Flag `segregated_bay` when the accessible position is not on the same branch of the queue graph.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Security- or hygiene-limited counters legitimately separate; then state the compensating provision. Symptom: a lobby with one "accessible desk" that is at the far end of the room and out of sight of the entrance.

### AX-09 Count redundant accessible vertical links; a lift-only building has a single point of failure per floor
- Rule: An accessible route that depends on one lift bank is one device away from being an inaccessible building. Publish per floor the number of *independent* accessible vertical links, the accessible route to each, and the fallback chain when the lift is out of service or on fire recall — including whether any accessible room is reachable without vertical travel.
- Evidence: The scoping rule is explicit that stories must be connected: §206.2.3 requires "at least one accessible route … to connect each story and mezzanine in multi-story buildings and facilities", with the small-building exemption rendered as "in private buildings or facilities that are less than three stories or that have less than 3 000 square feet (279 m²) per story, an accessible route shall not be required to connect stories". The corpus already records the operational conflict — CODE-23 states that this host's lift-only vertical can "make a plan look compliant while being operationally unsafe" because recall may remove the lift from occupant use, and FQ-19 prices the staff-assisted queue that replaces it. Hospitality practice adds the qualitative dimension: the qualitative IPA study found that participants' fear of being trapped was about *entry and exit systems*, not about travel distance.
- Source: Access Board 2010 ADA Standards §206.2.3 — T1 (US, 2010; opened, but note the exemption is expressed for *private* buildings and the public-facility limb was not surfaced on the page read) https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch02/ ; this repo: CODE-23, MS-16, MS-23, VT-*, FQ-19 — intra-repo; Heeb Desai et al. 2023 (T2, opened).
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (US) / ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT (redundancy)
- Scope: multi-storey; decisive for hotels and care settings
- Confidence: High (duty), Medium (exact exemption wording as rendered)
- Grid translation: Emit `accessible_vertical_links_per_floor`, `lift_dependency_steps`, `accessible_room_reachable_without_lift` (compute by removing each lift portal in turn and re-running the route BFS of MS-16). Minimum reporting: `1` is a flag, not a pass, in any building with sleeping rooms.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A single-storey or ramp-served plan has none; a maintenance-access-only level is not a user link. Symptom: 300 keys, 10 accessible keys on floor 8, zero ground-floor accessible rooms — every accessible booking is a lift dependency.

### AX-10 The arrival chain starts at the parking bay, and the bay is where the chain most often breaks
- Rule: Score arrival as four links — vehicle bay → access aisle → footway/route across the car park → building threshold — and require each to be level, unobstructed and connected. A compliant bay whose aisle is shared with a manoeuvring lane or drains across a cross-fall has already failed the trip before the door.
- Evidence: 2010 ADA Standards make the arrival links dimensional and level-specific: §502.2 "car parking spaces shall be 96 inches (2 440 mm) wide minimum", van spaces "132 inches (3 350 mm) wide minimum"; §502.3.1 "access aisles shall be 60 inches (1 525 mm) wide minimum" and §502.3 permits "two parking spaces … to share a common access aisle"; §502.4 states "changes in level are not permitted" with the drainage exception "slopes not steeper than 1:48 shall be permitted", and requires aisles "at the same level as the parking spaces"; §502.7 requires that "cars and vans, when parked, cannot obstruct the required clear width of adjacent accessible routes"; §503.2 scopes a passenger loading pull-up of "96 inches (2 440 mm) wide minimum and 20 feet (6 100 mm) long minimum" with a 60 in aisle (§503.3.1) and 114 in (2 895 mm) vertical clearance (§503.5). Scoping is only ~2 % of spaces (§208, as rendered on the page read). Field evidence agrees on the link: the qualitative study's participants named "misplaced designated parking" among the barriers that removed spontaneous outings, and this corpus's PP-22 records an accessibility consultancy ranking parking-with-crossfall as "the number one exterior barrier nationally".
- Source: Access Board, 2010 ADA Standards ch. 5 §502/§503 and ch. 2 §206–§208 — T1 (US, 2010, opened this session) https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch05/ ; Heeb Desai et al. 2023 qualitative study (T2, opened); this repo PP-22 — intra-repo.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (US values) / BUILDING-TYPE CONVENTION (hotel drop-off)
- Scope: universal where the site has parking or a set-down point; jurisdiction-specific values
- Confidence: High for the quoted clauses; Medium for the §208 percentage (page render terse)
- Grid translation: Emit per bay: `bay_tiles` (96 in → 5 tiles; 132 in → 7 tiles; aisle 60 in → 4 tiles, all rounded up per CODE-27), `aisle_shared_by_two_bays`, `aisle_on_accessible_route`, `parked_vehicle_intrudes_route_tiles` (declared vehicle footprint as blocked tiles), `level_change_tiles_in_aisle = 0`. Report `arrival_chain_break` at the first failing link.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Valet-only drop-offs and on-street parking transfer the duty to the highway authority — say which authority and stop claiming the link as designed. Symptom: a hotel port-cochère whose accessible set-down is 40 m from the door and crosses the driveway.

### AX-11 Shared-route arithmetic: a device user plus a pedestrian is not two pedestrians
- Rule: Where an inclusive route carries two-way movement, size width from the measured device envelope plus the passing body, and place passing places on the spacing interval set by the *tighter* governing instrument, not the looser. Publish the arithmetic: device width, other-user width, allowance, resulting tiles, interval.
- Evidence: AX-04's measurements give the device limb: 95th-percentile widths of 782 mm (manual), 828 mm (powered) and 840 mm (scooter). Body-width bands are owned by HS-03 and protrusion deduction by HS-21 (cited, not restated). On interval the two read instruments disagree by a factor of three: the US passing-space provision is recorded in CODE-18, while NCC 2019 Volume One Part D3 requires passing/turning provision every 20 m along accessways. Both are minimums for the same failure — being unable to get past — so the design test adopts the metric interval and reports the jurisdictional gap rather than choosing silently.
- Source: D'Souza et al. 2010 (T2, opened); ABCB, *National Construction Code 2019, Volume One, Part D3 Access for people with a disability* https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/2019/ncc-2019-volume-one/section-d-access-and-egress/part-d3-access-people-disability — T1 (Australia; edition 2019 → ## Weak or contested item 3), opened this session for the 20 m interval, the 2 m reversal bound, the 850 mm clear door opening, the 3.6 m continuous ramp-rise cap, the 11/4/15 mm carpet limits, the 80 %/95 % hearing-system coverage and the alternating left/right transfer duty; this repo HS-03, HS-21, CODE-18.
- Class: STANDARD (arithmetic) / CODE REQUIREMENT (interval, jurisdiction-branded)
- Scope: any route carrying device users plus pedestrians; hotel guest corridors and lobbies are the common case
- Confidence: High for the arithmetic, Medium for the cross-jurisdictional interval choice
- Grid translation: `passing_place_spacing_max_tiles = adopted_interval/0.5` → 20 m = 40 tiles (report the governing jurisdiction's interval as its own tile value rather than converting the other one); a passing place is HS-04's clear square (cited). Two-way device-plus-pedestrian: the p95 device width (AX-04) plus the passing-body band HS-03 derives, plus an allowance ⇒ test at 3 tiles, report 4 as comfort. Emit `passing_place_count`, `max_gap_tiles`, `route_width_hist` (HS-21's effective width, by ID).
- Exceptions / failure mode: One-way service corridors legitimately omit passing places — but then a device user cannot use them at all, which must appear in the class ledger (AX-03). Symptom: a 5-tile corridor 30 m long with no widening, which A* traverses happily.

### AX-12 The "no passing place" failure is a graph property and must be checked as one
- Rule: Beyond width, check reversibility: on every inclusive route no segment may require a user to reverse further than the governing bound, and no route may end in a pocket too short to turn in. Run it on the route graph (cut vertices, bridges, dead ends), not per room, and report the offending segment's endpoints.
- Evidence: NCC 2019 Part D3 states the requirement as a distance — reversal must be possible within 2 m — alongside the 20 m passing/turning interval: the same duty expressed as spacing rather than width. Australia's referenced standard for the geometric detail is AS 1428.1, which Part D3 invokes for circulation, glazing and fittings (figures not read here). The corpus's graph machinery already exists: AG-13 (cut vertices and bridges as the repair map), AG-14 (circuits over trees), SA-25 (`dead_end_encounter` flag idiom).

- Scope: universal on any route a device user traverses; the correct test for pushchairs and trolleys too (HS-19)
- Confidence: Medium-High (interval read; underlying AS figures not)

### AX-13 Spend the level-change budget where the user is least able to absorb it
- Rule: Order the chain by tolerance: a level change is least acceptable at the arrival bay and threshold (transferring from a vehicle, carrying, or alone) and most acceptable inside a room the user chose to enter. Wherever a change exists on a route it must be a declared ramp or lift with its run computed, and no link may mix strategies silently.
- Evidence: §502.4's "changes in level are not permitted" with only the ≤ 1:48 drainage exception is the standard treating arrival as the strictest case, and §505.10.1's requirement that ramp handrails "extend horizontally above the landing for 12 inches (305 mm) minimum" exists because the ramp's end is where control is lost. Ramp gradient, run and landing geometry belong to CODE-20 and HS-20 and are not restated; NCC 2019 Part D3 adds a different cap type — a maximum 3.6 m continuous rise for a ramp run — i.e. the metric code limits an unbroken ramp by height as well as by gradient.
- Source: Access Board 2010 ADA Standards §502.4, §505.10.1 — T1 (US, 2010, opened); ABCB NCC 2019 Vol. One Part D3 — T1 (Australia, 2019, opened); this repo CODE-20, HS-20, HS-22, GT-* (slope translation).
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT / DESIGN PRINCIPLE (the ordering)
- Scope: universal
- Confidence: High for quoted limbs; Medium for the tolerance ordering (a design argument, not a code text)
- Grid translation: Per route emit `level_change_events = [{link_id, tile_xy, rise_m, strategy: ramp|lift|none, run_tiles}]`, taking `run_tiles` from the governing gradient already transcribed in CODE-20 and translated in HS-20 (cited, not re-derived); hard check `arrival_link_changes = 0`; `max_continuous_rise_m ≤ 3.6` where the AU rule governs. Flag any link with an undeclared strategy.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Thresholds and drainage falls are sub-grid (PP-22) — declare as attributes and mark the link `off_grid_check_required` rather than passing it. Symptom: a "level entry" drawn across a 0.5 m curb tile.

### AX-14 Decompose the bathroom trip; the failures are objects and sequence, not area
- Rule: Score the hygiene link as enter → manoeuvre → lock → transfer → reach controls and disposal → wash → dry → exit, checking each step against the furniture register rather than the room outline. Where the standard's area is met but a step has no tile, the room fails.
- Evidence: The UTS multimethod study (3D scanning of accessible bathrooms, video re-enactment, interviews, plus a 200-respondent online survey of Australian wheelchair users) reports exactly this decomposition: doors and locks "scored under 50 % satisfaction"; transfer "poses fall risks when reaching low-mounted paper or performing sit-to-stand moves on wet floors"; "interior space, often cramped with accessories like dryers blocking grab rails"; approach "heavily disrupted by poor layouts that force meticulous outing planning or complete avoidance", with participants "avoiding disabled bathrooms due to limited space". Its conclusion is a call to "replace rigid standards with customizable, user-centered layouts". Compartment and fixture geometry are owned by HS-07/HS-08 and are not restated.
- Source: *Access Insight* (Autumn 2024), "New UTS research 'lifts the lid on how wheelchair users access public bathrooms'" (reprint) https://access.asn.au/access-insight-autumn-2024-new-uts-research-lifts-the-lid-on-how-wheelchair-users-access-public-bathrooms-reprint/ — T3 (secondary report of a T2 study; **primary paper not opened this session, so n = 200 and the sub-50 % figure travel at this tier**). This repo: HS-07, HS-08, HS-26.
- Class: HEURISTIC (the check) / EXPERIENCE-BASED ADVICE (the step list)
- Scope: every accessible or ambulant hygiene space; the same decomposition serves dressing, changing and wellness rooms
- Confidence: Medium (strong method, secondary provenance)
- Grid translation: Per hygiene room emit `clear_space_intrusions` (declared furniture tiles inside HS-07's clear space), `grab_rail_side_kept_clear`, `recovery_tile_behind_pan`, `disposal_and_paper_within_reach_band` (HS-23's horizontal reach translation, by ID), `wet_floor_exit_tile_count`, `lock_operable_from_seated_position` (flag; mechanism off-grid → human review). Report the failing step, not the room.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A private en-suite legitimately omits public features (radar lock, change table); a "family" or "ambulant" variant is a different chain and must be labelled, not scored against this one. Symptom: an accessible WC passing area with the bin in the transfer space.

### AX-15 Bed transfer is side-specific, and a property must offer both sides
- Rule: Specify per accessible sleeping room which long side of the bed is the transfer side and whether the clear zone is at head, side or both; then require a property-level mix. A block of identical right-handed accessible rooms is one-handed provision, and a user with a single-side limitation cannot use half of it.
- Evidence: The code instinct exists in the metric jurisdiction: NCC 2019 Part D3, in the Class 2/3 sanitary passages read this session, requires that where multiple accessible units are provided, transfer-oriented fittings alternate left and right. The scoping ladder for *how many* accessible rooms a hotel provides is owned by HO-05 and CODE-22 — including the roll-in vs transfer shower split, which is why bathroom variant and bed variant must be enumerated together. Bed-side clearances belong to HS-16, cited not restated.
- Source: ABCB NCC 2019 Vol. One Part D3 — T1 (Australia, 2019; opened for the alternation duty); this repo HO-05 (2010 ADA Table 224.2 read there), CODE-22, HS-16, HS-27.
- Class: BUILDING-TYPE CONVENTION (hospitality) / CODE REQUIREMENT (AU, sanitary fittings)
- Scope: hospitality, residential-with-care, healthcare sleeping rooms
- Confidence: Medium-High (the alternation duty is read for sanitary fittings; extending it to bed side is design inference and is labelled as such)
- Grid translation: Per room emit `transfer_side ∈ {L, R, both}`, `clear_side_tiles ≥ HS-16 band`, `head_approach_tiles`; per floor and property emit `transfer_side_variant_count`, `roll_in_variant_count`. Flag `single_variant_block` when accessible rooms ≥ 2 and `transfer_side_variant_count = 1`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Party walls legitimately pin the side — record the reason and place at least one opposite-handed room within the dispersion rule (CODE-22). Symptom: 12 accessible keys, identically mirrored, wardrobe on the transfer side.

### AX-16 Assembly and dining links have their own chain: arrive, park, see, be served, leave with a companion
- Rule: A wheelchair space is usable only if five sub-links hold — route in, park without intruding on the circulation that serves the space, see over standing heads, reach or be reached by service, and exit past the same door the companion uses. Score each; do not credit the count.
- Evidence: 2010 ADA Standards carry the sub-links explicitly: §802.1's 36 in (915 mm) minimum width; §802.1.3's 60 in (1 525 mm) depth "where a wheelchair space can be entered only from the side"; §802.1.5's rule that "wheelchair spaces shall not overlap circulation paths"; §802.2's requirement that "spectators seated in wheelchair spaces shall be afforded lines of sight over the heads of seated spectators"; §802.3's companion-seat equivalence ("equivalent in size, quality, comfort, and amenities to the seating in the immediate area"); §221.2.3's tolerance that "in row seating, two wheelchair spaces shall be permitted to be located side-by-side"; and §902.3's "28 inches minimum and 34 inches maximum" band for dining and work surfaces. Sightline validation itself needs a section the host lacks (HS-14's declared blind spot).
- Source: Access Board, 2010 ADA Standards ch. 2 §221, ch. 8 §802, ch. 9 §902 — T1 (US, 2010, opened) https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch08/ · https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch09/ .
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (US) / DESIGN PRINCIPLE (the five-link formulation)
- Scope: restaurants, bars, cinemas, auditoria, conference rooms, pool decks, wellness
- Confidence: High for quoted clauses; Low for line-of-sight outcomes (unverifiable on the grid)
- Grid translation: Per space emit `space_tiles` (36 in → 2 tiles; 60 in side-entry depth → 4 tiles, rounded up per CODE-27), `overlaps_route_tiles` (hard fail), `companion_tile_adjacent`, `row_exit_distance_tiles`, `servery_route_from_space_tiles`; emit `lines_of_sight = unverifiable (no Z axis, HS-14)`. Cross-check capacity against HO-17 rather than inventing covers.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A standing terrace or pool deck has no seat to pair — record `not applicable` with the reason. Symptom: four compliant wheelchair spaces in the back corner, none with a companion seat, each blocking the cross-aisle.

### AX-17 Contrast and illuminance are plan-observable decisions even though the grid cannot measure them
- Rule: For every threshold, decision point, hazard edge and control the chain passes, record the surface pair that must contrast and the illuminance the task needs, then evaluate as a declared attribute. Never let "the grid cannot see luminance" become "the design has no contrast requirement".
- Evidence: Canada's hosted accessibility standard gives the numbers: CAN-ASC-24 *Wayfinding and signage* cl. 10.2.1/10.2.2 set Michelson (Cm) / Weber (Cw) contrast thresholds by task — orientation "≥ 30" Cm and "≥ 45" Cw; hazard identification and reading text "≥ 60" Cm / "≥ 75" Cw; glossy control surfaces "≥ 70" Cm / "≥ 82" Cw — cl. 10.4 caps flooring pattern contrast at "Cm = 20 % (Cw = 33 %)", cl. 10.1.2.1 requires "100 lx" minimum on interior pathways with "50 lx" outdoors and "100 lx" at entryways (10.1.5), and cl. 11.4.2.4 requires tactile warning plates to hold an LRV gap "greater than 30 points for integrated units and greater than 40 points for discrete units" against a baseline lighter value of 50 points. Dementia Australia asks for "a minimum light level of 300 lux", hardware that "contrasts with the colour of the door", support bars in "a clear contrasting colour to the wall", mirrors removed or covered, and signage carrying "both the word 'toilet' and a symbol". The US limb is qualitative but mandatory: §703.5.1 requires "a non-glare finish" and "light characters on a dark background or dark characters on a light background".
- Source: Canadian standards database, *CAN-ASC-24 Wayfinding and signage* s.10/s.11 https://accessible.canada.ca/standards-and-technical-guides/standards-and-technical-guides-database/can-asc-24-wayfinding-and-signage/10-general-requirements-lighting-and-contrast — T1 (Canada; edition/year not surfaced on the page → ## Weak or contested item 5); Dementia Australia, *Designing dementia-friendly care environments* https://www.dementia.org.au/professionals/designing-dementia-friendly-care-environments — T2 (opened, page dated 2026-09-24); Access Board 2010 ADA Standards §703.5.1 — T1 (US, opened); Community Health Partnerships (NHS Scotland) inclusive-design lighting tool (faces lit, stair shadows used to separate tread from riser, avoid reflective floors, light entrance lobbies for transition) https://communityhealthpartnerships.co.uk/what-we-do/inclusive-design/interactive-tool/lighting/ — T2 (opened).
- Class: STANDARD (Canada, quoted) / CODE REQUIREMENT (US finish limb) / HEURISTIC (the recording discipline)
- Scope: universal; decisive at thresholds, stair nosings, sanitary rooms and servery lines
- Confidence: High that a task-graded numeric contrast regime exists; Medium for citation completeness
- Grid translation: Per link emit `sensory_pair = {element, surface_a, surface_b, target_Cm_min, target_LRV_delta_min, lux_design, task}` from a declared finishes schedule; two computable checks: `contrast_pairs_missing` (decision points and hazard edges with no pair) and `lux_zones_declared`. The ratio itself is `not_evaluable_on_grid` — the flag is the deliverable.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Patterned carpet and polished stone are legitimate for other reasons — the record must show the flooring-contrast cap was weighed. Symptom: a lobby with a dark rug whose edge reads as a hole.

### AX-18 A blind route needs continuity of edge, not a scatter of tactile tiles
- Rule: Treat non-visual travel as its own chain: find the entrance, follow an edge or guide line, detect each hazard edge before committing, confirm each decision point by touch or sound. A route with no continuous reference and at least one unmarked hazard edge fails regardless of width.
- Evidence: The US standard restricts tactile treatment rather than encouraging it: §705.1.1 sets dome "height of 0.2 inch (5.1 mm)", §705.1.2 "center-to-center spacing of 1.6 inches (41 mm) minimum and 2.4 inches (61 mm) maximum", and §705.2 applies detectable warnings where "platform boarding edges shall be 24 inches (610 mm) wide" — a hazard-edge treatment at transit platforms and vehicular areas, not a wayfinding surface. UK/Australian practice is richer and separately codified: blister/corduroy/lozenge/linear-guide patterns with published geometry (blister centres "66.5 mm" apart; corduroy bars "6 ± 0.5 mm high, 20 mm wide, 50 mm centre-to-centre"; directional bars "5.5 ± 0.5 mm high, 35 mm wide, spaced 45 mm"; platform-edge lozenges to a 400 mm depth at least 500 mm from the track edge) with colour set for luminance contrast against the pavement; Australia references AS/NZS 1428.4.1 for warning tactile markers through NCC Part D3.
- Source: Access Board 2010 ADA Standards §705 — T1 (US, 2010, opened) https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch07/ ; Wikipedia, *Tactile paving* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactile_paving — T4 (opened; attributes the geometry to UK DfT guidance via BS 7997:2003, JIS T 9251:2001 and AS/NZS 1428.4 — the primary DfT PDF returned undecodable binary) `NEEDS VERIFICATION:` DfT *Guidance on the Use of Tactile Paving Surfaces* clause text; ABCB NCC 2019 Part D3 — T1 (opened, for the AS/NZS 1428.4.1 reference).
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (US hazard limb) / STANDARD (AU/UK taxonomy, via secondary) / HEURISTIC (the continuity test)
- Scope: public routes, platforms, car-park crossings; care and hospitality guest routes
- Confidence: High for §705 as quoted; Low-Medium for paving geometries (T4 transcription)
- Grid translation: A warning band resolves to 1–3 tiles (610 mm → 2 tiles; 400 mm depth → 1 tile), so emit per hazard edge `hazard_edge_tiles`, `warning_band_tiles_present`, `band_width_tiles`; per route `edge_guidance_ratio = continuous_reference_edge_tiles / route_tiles`; flag `unguided_hazard_edge`, `route_no_reference_edge`. This is a presence test — pattern geometry goes to human review.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Over-application is a real defect: blisters everywhere warn nothing, and §705's narrow scoping is the counter-example. Symptom: a "tactile wayfinding scheme" of full-route blisters that neither rolls nor reads.

### AX-19 A sign only works if it is reachable, in view before the commitment point, and describing the choice
- Rule: For each decision point on an inclusive route, require three things jointly: a sign located on the latch side of the door it names, visible from the approach far enough back that the user can still choose to turn, and content that matches the choice actually available. Any one missing is a wayfinding failure the plan can be blamed for.
- Evidence: The standard sets the mounting facts: §703.4.2 places tactile signs "alongside the door at the latch side", §703.4.1 fixes the reading zone by the "baseline of the lowest tactile character" between "48 inches (1220 mm) minimum and 60 inches (1525 mm) maximum", §703.2.5 bounds tactile character height "5/8 inch (16 mm) minimum and 2 inches (51 mm) maximum based on the uppercase letter 'I'", and §703.5.5 sends visual character height to a viewing-distance table (values not surfaced on the page read → ## Weak or contested item 6). Universal Design principle 3 requires use to be "easy to understand, regardless of the user's experience, knowledge, language skills, or current concentration level" and principle 4 requires redundant modes and adequate contrast. Behaviourally, HB-06 caps decision points per trip and HB-09 requires the destination in view before a sign is written — this rule's addition is that the *sign* and the *route* must be checked against each other, not separately.
- Source: Access Board 2010 ADA Standards §703.2.5, §703.4.1, §703.4.2, §703.5.1, §703.5.5 — T1 (US, 2010, opened); Centre for Excellence in Universal Design, *The 7 Principles* (principles 3 and 4 quoted) — T2 (opened); this repo HB-06, HB-09, SA-06/SA-09.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (mounting) / DESIGN PRINCIPLE (the joint test)
- Scope: all public routes; densest in lobbies, lift halls, and multi-exit floors
- Confidence: High for the quoted clauses; Medium for the joint test (design inference)
- Grid translation: Emit `decision_points = [...]` from HB-06's junction test; per point: `sign_tile_declared`, `sign_at_latch_side_ok`, `first_visible_tile`, `commit_tile`, `warning_distance_tiles = commit_tile − first_visible_tile`, `min_reading_distance_required` (from the declared character height × the §703.5.5 rule once its values are read; until then emit `unknown`). Flag `sign_after_commitment` and `no_sign_at_choice`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Landmark-based navigation legitimately replaces signage and is often better for cognitively impaired users — record it as the strategy instead of adding a redundant sign. Symptom: a directory at the lift that lists only the floor the user is on.

### AX-20 Every alert and every spoken instruction must have a second sense or a second person
- Rule: For each notification the chain depends on — fire alarm, call to order, queue number, staff instruction, door release, lift arrival — require either a redundant modality (visible + audible) or an explicit assistance path. Then check the acoustic conditions that make the audible limb usable, and report them as off-grid where the grid cannot.
- Evidence: §702.1 requires that "fire alarm systems shall have permanently installed audible and visible alarms complying with NFPA 72", and §219 scopes assistive listening with a receiver ladder (the render shows, for 501–1 000 seats, "20, plus 1 per 33 seats over 500 seats") and requires that "twenty-five percent minimum of receivers provided, but no fewer than two, shall be hearing-aid compatible in accordance with 706.3". Australia's Part D3 (read this session) sets the *coverage* of the listening system rather than the count — 80 % for induction loops against 95 % for receiver arrays. What the grid cannot compute is the room acoustics that decide whether speech is intelligible: reverberation and STI need volumes and finishes this host does not model (HS-14's no-Z limitation, SA-16's two enclosure measures), so the honest artefacts are the physical proxies — hard-surface share and travel distance from the talker position.
- Source: Access Board 2010 ADA Standards §702.1, §703, §706, §708 (ch. 7) — T1 (US, 2010, opened); ch. 2 §219 (T1, opened; page render partial); ABCB NCC 2019 Vol. One Part D3 (AU; 80 %/95 % hearing coverage) — T1 (opened); this repo HS-14, SA-16, EN-* (noise criteria owned in `environmental-design.md`).
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (US/AU limbs) / HEURISTIC (the acoustic proxies)
- Scope: assembly, dining servery, lift halls, egress notification, reception desks, care and hospitality corridors
- Confidence: High for the quoted clauses; Low for the proxies' predictive value
- Grid translation: Per alert emit `modalities = [audible, visible, tactile, staff]`, `coverage_share_ok` (against the applicable jurisdiction's basis — count vs area: name it); per room emit `hard_surface_share` (blocked/wall tile perimeter over room tiles, SA-16's physical enclosure recipe), `max_talker_listener_tiles`, and `acoustic_verdict = not_evaluable_on_grid`. Flag `single_modality_alert` on any egress or servery link.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A pool deck or spa legitimately cannot host a loop; state the substitute (visual signalling plus staff) in the class ledger. Symptom: a "fully alerted" spa whose only call system is a chime nobody can locate.

### AX-21 Cognitive accessibility is a property of the route graph and can be computed
- Rule: For users with memory, attention or orientation impairments, the design requirement is not signage volume but a plan that is legible: circular rather than dead-ended movement, destinations visible from the points where they must be found, few forks, and each fork offering a visibly different choice. Compute these from the graph and the visibility layer and report them per floor.
- Evidence: Dementia-specific guidance is explicit about the spatial properties rather than decorative ones: Dementia Australia requires "unobstructed sightlines between the bed and toilet", lighting "a minimum light level of 300 lux", removal or covering of mirrors, motion-activated guidance lighting that "turns on automatically when it detects movement", and identification carrying "both the word 'toilet' and a symbol"; Community Health Partnerships adds the shadow and reflection cautions (glare "pools of bright light and strong shadows", reflective surfaces that "can cause disorientation"). The corpus already owns the computable counterparts: AG-14's ringness (circuits over trees), HB-06's decision-point cap with asymmetric junctions, HB-07's rejection of the symmetric repeating plate, SA-09's covisibility matrix, SA-19's legibility proxies — this file's contribution is applying them as *inclusion* tests with named user classes.
- Source: Dementia Australia (T2, opened); Community Health Partnerships, NHS Scotland (T2, opened); this repo AG-14, HB-06, HB-07, SA-09, SA-19, SA-25.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE / HEURISTIC (the mapping to metrics)
- Scope: care, hospitals, schools, large hotels and airports — any building where users are unfamiliar, stressed or medicated
- Confidence: Medium-High (guidance is qualitative; the metrics are established in this corpus)
- Grid translation: Per floor emit `ringness_public_skeleton` (AG-14), `decision_point_count` and `asymmetry_ok` (HB-06/SA-15), `covisibility_bed_to_toilet` (SA-09 over the declared furniture positions), `symmetric_plate_flag` (HB-07), `night_route_lighting_declared`. Bands come from SA-24/SA-25 rather than being redefined here.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A highly legible plan can also be a surveilling one; HB-18's caution on buying supervision with sightlines applies, and private rooms must not be made transparent to satisfy this test. ### AX-22 Egress for an inclusive population is a chain with three distinct links, and each has an owner
- Rule: Report per floor the inclusive egress chain — reach a defended refuge or a protected stair, communicate and be located, and be moved by an identified resource — and name which corpus rule owns each link's arithmetic. Do not blend it into the general egress verdict: a floor can pass FQ-* capacity and still have no defended place for the population it scopes.
- Evidence: The egress code limbs are already transcribed in this corpus and are used as inputs, not repeated: the accessible-means-of-egress scoping and the two-way-communication-and-backup-power duties at the elevator landing are quoted in FQ-21 (US, WA-adopted IBC §1009 family; NZ place-of-safety density), and the staffed-movement rate that prices the movement limb is FQ-19's (NZ C/VM2, per-patient per-crew-pair service time). CODE-12 and FQ-21 own the refuge place; CODE-23 owns the lift-recall conflict. What is new here is the notification limb read at source this session: §702.1 requires that "fire alarm systems shall have permanently installed audible and visible alarms complying with NFPA 72" — i.e. the chain cannot be completed by sound alone. Field evidence on evacuation chairs is thin but real: a controlled-drill study (Szulc, Cisek & Król, *Safety & Fire Technology* 60(2), 2022) ran twelve exercises across two levels and two stairwells comparing fully mobile occupants, crutch-restricted occupants and one disabled participant moved in an evacuation chair, and found the chair produced 10.47 %, 28.71 % and 47.07 % faster clearance depending on stairwell and departure floor — relative figures only; the report gives no absolute per-floor time and does not specify crew size.

- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (US limbs) / ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT (staffed movement) / HEURISTIC (the chair evidence)
- Scope: every storey not open to grade; hotels, hospitals, offices with unknown occupant mixes
- Confidence: High for the scoping and alarm limbs (read in this corpus and at source); Low for chair timing transferability
- Grid translation: Per floor emit `refuge_tiles`, `refuge_capacity_vs_scoped_spaces` (using FQ-21's arithmetic, not a new one), `wheelchair_spaces_in_scope` (counted from HO-05 and the assembly table of §221), `comms_and_power_declared`, `staff_pairs_available` (roster input), `t_assist_per_person_s` (from FQ-19's rate where the jurisdiction adopts it), `evacuation_mode ∈ {refuge_and_assist, horizontal_exit, lift_dependent, whole_building}`; flag `no_refuge_on_floor` and `single_assistance_resource`.

### AX-23 Price inclusion in tiles, per floor, and put the invoice in the report
- Rule: Accessibility consumes area that no revenue line owns; make it a named ledger rather than a residue. Publish per floor the tiles spent on inclusive use — passing places, turning squares, door maneuvering, refuge, widened arrival links, accessible room variants and their bathrooms — with the driver rule cited, so that every later "value engineering" cut is attributable.
- Evidence: The drivers are all already in the corpus and each is a real area cost: HS-04's turning square, HS-06/CODE-19's maneuvering clearance, HS-08's compartment sizing (with its wall-tax arithmetic), HS-21's effective-width deduction, CODE-12/FQ-21's refuge, HO-05's accessible-key scoping and the AX-16 finding that a side-entry wheelchair space costs 60 in of depth against 36 in of width — a 50 % area premium produced purely by which side the user can enter from. `architecture-theory.md` TH-13 already states that the junction turning square "starts to eat the floor plate — this is the real cost of accessibility compliance"; EC-* owns the ledger discipline this rule plugs into.
- Source: This repo: HS-04, HS-06, HS-08, HS-21, CODE-12, CODE-19, FQ-21, HO-05, TH-13, EC-*, AG-12; Access Board 2010 ADA Standards §802.1/§802.1.3 — T1 (US, opened) for the width/depth asymmetry.
- Class: STANDARD (reporting discipline) / FACT (the arithmetic)
- Scope: universal; the largest per-floor line items are hotels, hospitals and civic
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Emit `inclusive_use_cost_tiles = Σ{passing_place, turning_square, door_maneuver, refuge, arrival_widen, accessible_room_delta}` with each item carrying `driver_rule_id`, plus `inclusive_use_cost_share_of_plate`. - Exceptions / failure mode: Shared geometry must be counted once — a passing place that is also the door maneuvering clearance is one allocation with two drivers. Symptom: a cost ledger that double-counts the same 4 × 4 square three times and then "proves" accessibility is unaffordable.

### AX-24 Count the assistance the design requires, and name the resource that supplies it
- Rule: Some links only complete with a second person: holding a door, operating a lock, transferring in a wet room, lifting a pushchair over a threshold, deploying an evacuation chair, explaining a route. Count them per user class, and for each count declare who supplies the help and when it is unavailable. An assistance requirement is a legitimate design decision; an undeclared one is an operational defect.
- Evidence: The evidence base treats assistance as the norm rather than the exception: Universal Design principle 7's guidelines end with "provide adequate space for the use of assistive devices or personal assistance", and the scoping instruments assume an attendant — §802.3's companion seat for every wheelchair space, §221.2.1's dispersion of those spaces, §503's passenger loading zone sized for a vehicle that stops to set someone down. The measured-device work notes that real users travel with the device plus its stowage and their clothing envelope, and the qualitative study records participants whose "support networks" were themselves part of the accessibility system during closures. The staffing consequence in an emergency is priced by FQ-19's queue arithmetic; the routine-hour consequence is HO-*'s labour ledger.
- Source: Centre for Excellence in Universal Design (T2, opened); Access Board 2010 ADA Standards §503, §802.3, ch. 2 §221 — T1 (US, opened); Heeb Desai et al. 2023 qualitative study (T2, opened); this repo FQ-19, HO-12/HO-13, CODE-24.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: universal; care, hospitality and transit are the densest cases
- Confidence: Medium-High
- Grid translation: Extend the class ledger with `steps_requiring_assistance = [{link, task, resource_type: staff|companion|family|equipment, availability_window}]` and emit `assistance_load_per_shift` where the link is routine (not emergency). Cross-check against the roster model (HO-13) so the plan does not require help nobody is scheduled to give.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A 24/7 front desk makes some assistance links genuinely safe; a part-night reception does not. Symptom: an "accessible" self-catering unit whose only compliant bathroom needs a second person for the transfer and whose support line closes at 20:00.

### AX-25 Compliance is a floor and usability is a different test: satisfy the dimensions, then run the chain
- Rule: Publish both verdicts and keep them separate — a dimensional verdict against HS-*/CODE-*, and a task-chain verdict against AX-01's links. Never report the first as evidence for the second, and never let a chain failure be answered by re-measuring the dimensions.
- Evidence: Four independent lines show the two diverge. (i) The measured-device work (n = 369) concludes that the clear floor space prescribed in US transportation accessibility standards is inadequate for 95 % of actual device users — i.e. a compliant space is dimensioned off a smaller reference device. (ii) The Australian bathroom research (multimethod; 200-respondent survey arm) finds sub-50 % satisfaction with doors and locks and users avoiding compliant bathrooms. (iii) The 21-study scoping review attributes participation limits to transfer accessibility and route links, not to width. (iv) The jurisdictional disagreement in AX-11 — a 20 m passing interval in Australia against 200 ft in the US, for the same failure mode — shows that "compliant" is not a single answer even for the same plan. CODE-28 already states the principle for egress; this rule is its accessibility counterpart and supplies the mechanism.
- Source: D'Souza et al. 2010 (T2, opened); *Access Insight* Autumn 2024 reporting UTS research (T3, opened); Heeb Desai et al. 2023 scoping review (T2, opened); ABCB NCC 2019 Part D3 (T1, opened); Access Board 2010 ADA ch. 4 as recorded in CODE-18 (T1); this repo CODE-28, PP-22, VA-03.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: universal
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Emit two verdict blocks per floor: `dimensional_compliance = {rule_id, verdict, value, jurisdiction}` and `inclusive_use_chain = {user_class, link, verdict, evidence}`. A report that contains only the first is rejected by this file's own contract.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A chain failure is not licence to invent dimensions — the fix is a documented, cited over-provision (HS-24's report-both-numbers discipline) or an explicit risk note. Symptom: "users found it hard, so we called the corridor 5 tiles" with no source for the number.

- Source: This repo: VA-03, SA-25, SA-24, MS-16, PP-22, HS-21, HS-24, HS-27, CODE-27 — intra-repo.
- Class: STANDARD (report contract)
- Scope: universal on this host
- Confidence: High

### AX-27 Declare what the grid cannot check, and route it to a human or an occupant
- Rule: Keep an explicit off-grid list and attach it to every inclusive-use report: luminance and colour contrast, illuminance, sign character height and mounting height, door opening force and hardware, threshold upstands, handrail continuity, edge protection at changes of level, acoustic reverberation and STI, sightline over heads, lift car dimensions and door dwell, evacuation-chair operation and crew competence, and the whole sensory-cognitive experience of the space. - Source: This repo HS-14, HS-19, HS-23, HS-27, CODE-20; CAN-ASC-24 s.10/s.11 (T1, opened); Dementia Australia (T2, opened); *Access Insight* Autumn 2024 (T3, opened).

- Scope: this host, all types
- Confidence: High

- Exceptions / failure mode: None — this rule exists to prevent the failure of pretending otherwise. ### AX-28 The five-step inclusive-use pass, run before the plan is frozen

## Weak or contested

1. **The WHO age-friendly focus-group sample is reported two different ways by two reads of the same page.** One read rendered "8 focus groups per city, 11 cities in 11 countries, 148 participants (99 F / 49 M, 55+), 229 features with 48 raised by over a third"; another rendered "1 485 older adults, 250 caregivers and 515 service providers across 33 cities". Both are from the PMC article page, and neither is the paper's own table. **The 1 485/250/515 rendering is almost certainly the wrong one** (it does not match the study's design), but the file does not resolve it: use the *method* (multi-city focus groups feeding WHO's eight domains) and treat any participant count as unverified until the article's method section is read from the published PDF.
2. **Government PDFs are systematically unreadable to this toolchain, and that is this file's biggest blind spot.** The WHO age-friendly checklist, UK Approved Document M Vol. 1, the DfT tactile-paving and *Inclusive Mobility* guidance, MHCLG's AD M research report, NIST TN 1923 and the RNIB *Building Sight* guide all returned binary streams, size limits or 403/404s. Consequence: the UK/AU *sequence* literature is represented here at T4 (tactile geometry via Wikipedia) or not at all; no UK dimension is asserted; the whole sensory layer rests on Canada plus two professional bodies. **Obtain:** the WHO checklist item text, AD M Vol. 1/Vol. 2 clause values, DfT tactile paving clause text, NIST TN 1923's survey percentages, RNIB *Building Sight* contrast/lux tables.
3. **The Australian figures are NCC 2019.** Part D3's 20 m passing interval, 2 m reversal bound, 850 mm door opening, 3.6 m ramp-rise cap and 80 %/95 % hearing coverage were read from the 2019 Volume One page; the NCC has been restructured since (including the "National Construction Solution" presentation the brief names), and AS 1428.1 was revised in 2021. Any project using these numbers must re-stamp the adopted edition. They are also in genuine tension with the US passing-space provision already recorded in CODE-18, whose interval is roughly three times looser — a disagreement on the same failure mode, kept as a disagreement rather than averaged (AX-11).
4. **Chapter 2's page render is partial.** §208 came back as "2 percent of total", §219's receiver ladder arrived as one row ("501 to 1 000 | 20, plus 1 per 33 seats over 500 seats"), and §221.2.1.1's ladder surfaced only "301 to 500 | 6"; §224 and §226 were not present in the render at all. Those fragments are quoted with that caveat and are **not** usable as validator inputs — HO-05 owns the transient-lodging ladder (read there from the standard via a reproduction), and the dining-surface scoping percentage is `NEEDS VERIFICATION:` (read §226 from the standard's own text).
5. **CAN-ASC-24 is cited without its edition.** The clause numbers and the Cm/Cw/lux/LRV values were read from the government standards database page; the page render did not show the publication year or whether the entry is a proposed or final standard. Until the edition is stamped, AX-17's numbers are a *magnitude* reference (task-graded contrast thresholds, 100 lx interior routes) and not a compliance test.
6. **§703.5.5's viewing-distance/character-height table was not surfaced**, so AX-19's `min_reading_distance_required` cannot be computed — the rule records the requirement and returns `unknown`. Obtain the printed table before encoding any legibility check; the related sign *location* clauses (§703.4.1/§703.4.2) were read and are usable.
7. **The bathroom-evidence chain is T3, not T2.** The most quotable usability findings in this file (doors/locks under 50 % satisfaction, accessories blocking grab rails, avoidance behaviour, n = 200 survey) come from a professional-association article about a university study whose primary paper was not opened. They are strong enough to shape a heuristic (AX-14) and to justify running the chain, and too weak to appear in any validator as a threshold. **Obtain:** the UTS paper.
8. **Evacuation-chair evidence is comparative.** The 2022 Polish drill study reports relative clearance gains from 12 exercises, one of which involved a single disabled participant, and gives no absolute per-floor time and no crew requirement. Any RSET impact must therefore come from FQ-19's staffed-queue arithmetic (which is sourced to NZ C/VM2), not from the chair study; the chair evidence supports only "the device helps and the timing is not yet quantified". **Obtain:** NIST TN 1923's occupant-preference survey and a chair study with absolute times and crew counts.
9. **"Compliance ≠ usability" is asserted on a thin and partly secondary evidence base.** Four lines agree in direction (measured devices exceed the reference device; a scoping review; a qualitative study; the bathroom research), but none of them is a controlled comparison of a code-compliant space against its users' outcomes, and the D'Souza conclusion is specific to transit vehicle floors. The honest claim this file can support is: *compliant dimensions have been measured, in at least one setting, to be smaller than the 95th-percentile real device, and users report failing at links the dimensions do not describe.* It cannot support "codes produce unusable buildings" — that stronger claim is Imrie's literature and was not retrievable here at primary level.
10. **Three device rows are unsourced (rollator, pushchair, evacuation chair) and one whole standard is missing (AS 1428.1 geometry, ADA Part 38 reference device).** AX-04's "largest routine device" test is therefore operating on wheelchair-and-scooter data only, in a transit population, from a US sample. For a care home or a family hotel the governing device may be larger or differently shaped. Do not treat the register as complete; treat a missing device class as a flag, not as a pass.
11. **Inference load, stated plainly.** AX-09's redundancy requirement, AX-13's tolerance ordering, AX-15's extension of the sanitary alternation duty to the bed side, AX-21's mapping of dementia guidance onto graph metrics and AX-23's "report the cost" discipline are design arguments made by this file, not quotations from any instrument. Each carries `DESIGN PRINCIPLE` or `HEURISTIC` for that reason, and each is classed CODE REQUIREMENT only where a quoted clause does the work.
12. **No jurisdiction in this file covers mobility *in the room* for deafblind, neurodivergent or paediatric populations.** The corpus is silent, the opened sources are silent, and this file's answer is to emit `unknown` rather than to generalise from adult wheelchair data.
