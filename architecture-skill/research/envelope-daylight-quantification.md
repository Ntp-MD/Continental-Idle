# Research file — Domain AF: Solar geometry, daylight and envelope performance quantification

Scope: the **arithmetic layer** the corpus does not have. `environmental-design.md` states the qualitative chain (climate → orientation → envelope → openings → room placement) and asserts targets — "the depth of daylight penetration is about 2.5 × …", "cap the glazing-to-façade ratio" — but nowhere does it give an equation an agent can execute. This file supplies the computable version: sun position as a function of latitude/date/hour, shadow length, obstruction screening angles, the daylight-metric thresholds with their credit sources, the solar-gain product, envelope build-up as planar geometry, acoustic attenuation from surface density, and ventilation free area. It changes **no strategy**: every rule here is written to be read *next to* the rule it quantifies in `environmental-design.md` (EN-01…EN-26), and where a number already exists in the corpus this file cites the existing rule ID instead of restating the strategy. Same boundary against `construction.md` CN-26 (party walls as acoustic assemblies — geometry with no dB figure) and `multi-scale.md` MS-15 (façade rhythm from the bay module — a rule with no dimensions): this file supplies the missing magnitudes and marks the ones it could not source.

Host grid: `1 tile = 0.5 m` (= 500 mm), one independent 2D grid per floor, tile states ONLY `walkable | blocked | door`; no section, no heights, no material, no surface. Carried over unchanged from `environmental-design.md`'s declared section block: floor-to-floor 3.0 m = 6 tiles, clear height 2.7 m = 5.4 tiles, window head H = 2.1 m = 4.2 tiles, sill 0.9 m, glazed façade tile = 0.75 m² of glass, vent tile = 0.55 m² openable, WWR ≈ 0.556 × glazed/façade tiles (EN-12). Every solar or thermal number below is latitude- and climate-dependent unless stated otherwise; the flagship multi-floor hotel case is run at two reference latitudes (40°N mid-latitude, 25°N subtropical) and the agent must substitute its own.

## Rules

### EQ-01 Compute sun position from three inputs and nothing else
- Rule: solar altitude and azimuth are functions of exactly three numbers — latitude φ, day of year d, and hour angle H. No climate, material or site data enters. If an agent cannot state those three, it has not computed a sun position, it has guessed one.
- Evidence: the PVEducation solar-position equation set as read this pass — declination, hour angle, altitude, azimuth and the sunrise hour angle, with the source's own sign conventions and stated accuracy figures.
  - Declination (source form, degrees, d = day of year with 1 Jan as d = 1): `δ = −23.45° × cos[ (360/365) × (d + 10) ]`
  - Equivalent forms given by the same source: `δ = 23.45° × sin[ (360/365) × (d + 284) ]` and `δ = 23.45° × sin[ (360/365) × d − 81 ]`. The three variants differ by **< 0.3°**; versus a precise ephemeris the absolute error is **up to 1°** (source-stated). δ ≈ 0 about 22 Mar and 22 Sep, maximum about 22 Jun, minimum about 21–22 Dec.
  - Higher-accuracy single-line form (Wikipedia, *Position of the Sun*, T3; URL recorded at EQ-02 and in **Sources**): `δ = −arcsin[ 0.39779 · cos( 0.98565°(N+10) + 1.914° · sin(0.98565°(N−2)) ) ]`, N = ordinal date − 1; obliquity `ε = 23.439° − 0.0000004°·n`.
  - Hour angle: `H = 15° × (solar hour − 12)` (negative before solar noon). The 15°/h rate is the definitional quotient 360°/24 h, not a fitted constant; the fetched pages use HRA without tabulating the rate.
  - Altitude: `α = sin⁻¹[ sin δ · sin φ + cos δ · cos φ · cos H ]`, with **φ positive in the northern hemisphere, negative in the southern** (source-stated).
  - Azimuth: `Az = cos⁻¹[ (sin δ · cos φ − cos δ · sin φ · cos H) / cos α ]`, measured **0° = North, 180° = South** (source-stated), then `Az_final = Az` for H < 0 (morning) and `Az_final = 360° − Az` for H > 0 (afternoon).
  - Day length / sun up-limit: sunrise–sunset hour angle `ωs = cos⁻¹( −tan φ · tan δ )`; day length = `2 ωs / 15°` hours.
- Source: PVEducation (Solar Energy Education), *Elevation Angle*, *Azimuth Angle*, *Declination Angle* — https://www.pveducation.org/pvcdrom/properties-of-sunlight/elevation-angle , https://www.pveducation.org/pvcdrom/properties-of-sunlight/azimuth-angle , https://www.pveducation.org/pvcdrom/properties-of-sunlight/declination-angle — T3 (educational reproduction of the Duffie & Beckman formulation; the primary text itself was not read this pass). Source type: GUIDE (T3) — underlying physics is textbook astronomy.
- Class: FACT — a closed-form relation among three named inputs. Nothing in it is a policy target: no scheme, code or committee sets the sun's position.
- Scope: any site, any type. Binds every rule below.
- Confidence: High for the equation set as given; Medium for its provenance chain (T3 page, not the primary handbook). **NEEDS VERIFICATION** against Duffie & Beckman, *Solar Engineering of Thermal Processes*, ch. 1, for the sign convention as printed.
- Grid translation: store `sun = {phi_deg, d_int, H_deg}`; compute δ, then α, then Az; assert `alpha >= 0` before using the result. Emit the triple with every solar claim.
- Exceptions / failure mode: altitude accuracy of the simple declination is ±1° absolute (source-stated); ignores refraction (~0.5° near the horizon) and local horizon relief. Trade-off: the exact SPA/NREL algorithm costs a lookup and gives arc-minute accuracy; the three-input form is a hundred times less accurate and a thousand times easier to audit — for massing decisions ±1° is irrelevant, for a shading-device edge it is not. Symptom: a downstream solar claim arrives with no latitude, day-of-year and hour-angle triple attached, so no reviewer can recompute it and the ±1° is silently spent as if it were exact.

### EQ-02 Publish latitude, hemisphere, calendar convention and clock basis with every solar number
- Rule: a sun table without φ, hemisphere and a time basis is not reproducible. Three separate errors hide here: the d-indexing convention (d = 0 vs d = 1 at 1 Jan changes δ by ~0.4°), the equation of time (up to ~16 min), and the longitude/clock-zone offset (which can exceed 1 h).
- Evidence: the error magnitudes are source-stated, not inferred — Wikipedia gives the declination approximation as carrying "up to 2.0°" potential error and the equation of time as making sundials deviate "up to about 16 minutes fast or slow"; PVEducation *Declination Angle* supplies the `d + 10` indexing and the ±1° absolute figure. Only the combination of the three errors into one reproducibility requirement is arithmetic performed here.
- Source: Wikipedia, *Position of the Sun* — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Position_of_the_Sun — T3: declination approximation carries "up to 2.0°" potential error, equation of time makes sundials deviate "up to about 16 minutes fast or slow". PVEducation *Declination Angle* (T3; URL recorded at EQ-01 and in **Sources**) for the `d + 10` indexing and the ±1° absolute figure. Source type: GUIDE (T3).
- Class: FACT as to the stated error magnitudes; the publish-both-or-neither discipline is this project's bookkeeping requirement, not a claim made by either source.
- Scope: universal.
- Confidence: High (the magnitude of the errors is source-stated; only their combination is arithmetic).
- Grid translation: emit `sun_basis = {latitude, hemisphere, day_index_convention, time_basis: "apparent solar"}`. Where clock time is used, add `eot_error_min` and `longitude_offset_min` and convert: `solar hour = clock + (longitude_offset + EoT)/60`. **Worked sensitivity**: at φ = 40°N, 21 Dec, 09:00 solar time, α = 13.95° → shadow factor 4.03. A 16-minute (4° hour-angle) error gives α = 11.85° → shadow factor **4.77**, i.e. **+19 %** shadow length from the clock error alone. Recompute, never scale.
- Exceptions / failure mode: the ±16 min bound is the annual peak, not a typical value. Trade-off: publishing clock time is legible to a client; publishing solar time is correct — publish both, or publish neither. Symptom: a shading or spacing result is quoted against wall-clock time, the two disagree by a quarter of the winter morning's sun path, and the error is attributed to the site rather than to the basis.

### EQ-03 Declare the north datum before any azimuth is used, and separate plan-north from true north
- Rule: azimuth is measured from true north; a tile grid has no north. EN-01 requires the hemisphere declaration and EN-02 requires the façade bearing — this file supplies the transform. On a grid whose cardinal labels are arbitrary, every solar result is only valid relative to a stated datum rotation.
- Evidence: the azimuth convention read at EQ-01, plus the two corpus prerequisites this rule unlocks — the hemisphere declaration and the per-façade bearing recorded by the rules named at Source below. This file supplies only the transform between them.
- Source: `environmental-design.md` EN-01 (declare the climate driver and hemisphere before any environmental rule may fire), EN-02 (record the actual bearing of each façade run and the deviation from due south). Azimuth convention from PVEducation *Azimuth Angle* (T3; URL recorded at EQ-01 and in **Sources**), as quoted in EQ-01. Source type: STANDARD-ADJACENT (corpus rule) + GUIDE (T3) for the convention.
- Class: ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT — a precondition on every other rule here, not a measured claim.
- Scope: universal on this host; the failure is silent and total.
- Confidence: High — this is a bookkeeping requirement, not a measured claim.
- Grid translation: require `north_datum_deg` = compass bearing of grid +Z before any EQ rule runs. Then `true_azimuth = model_azimuth + north_datum_deg (mod 360)` and each façade run's incidence is `θi = |façade_normal_true − Az_final|`. Validation: reject any output containing a solar claim while `north_datum_deg` is null; report `façade_bearings` and their deviation from the equator direction against EN-02's ±15°/±30° tolerance.
- Exceptions / failure mode: a rotated datum does not change day length or altitude, only azimuth — so an agent can get half its solar results right while its orientation results are all wrong. Trade-off: rotating the building to true north is cleaner for computation and wrong for the street grid; the datum must be carried, not suppressed. Symptom: altitude-based results (shadows, day length) look plausible while every orientation allocation is mirrored, and no validation step trips.

### EQ-04 Run the two noon identities as a correctness test before trusting any sun computation
- Rule: two values are derivable without a calculator and are the cheapest available regression test for EQ-01. If they fail, the code — not the site — is wrong.
- Evidence: Wikipedia's two noon-elevation statements, quoted at Source below, give the equinox and solstice forms. The second identity used here — the noon **azimuth flip** — is derived algebraically from EQ-01's azimuth formula (self-check, no external claim): at noon, H = 0, the azimuth numerator becomes `−sin(φ−δ)` and `cos α = |sin(φ−δ)|`, so the noon sun sits on the equator side when δ is smaller than φ and on the poleward side when it is larger. Corollary an agent must not miss: **a tropical site (|φ| < 23.45°) has a north-facing façade in the northern hemisphere for part of the year** — the "equator-facing is best" premise of EN-02 inverts at noon near the solstices.
- Source: Wikipedia, *Position of the Sun* — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Position_of_the_Sun — T3, which states noon elevation at the equinoxes as `90° − latitude` and at the solstices as `90° − |latitude − 23½°|`. Second identity derived here algebraically from the EQ-01 azimuth formula (self-check, no external claim). Source type: GUIDE (T3) + arithmetic derivation.
- Class: FACT — identities, not thresholds; no scheme or code participates in them.
- Scope: universal; run once per project, on every implementation change.
- Confidence: High.
- Grid translation: assert `α(equinox, H=0) == 90 − φ` and `α(δ, H=0) == 90 − |φ − δ|`. **Noon azimuth identity**: `Az(noon) = 180°` (due south) when `δ < φ`, and `Az(noon) = 0°` (due north) when `δ > φ`. Numerical warning: the `cos⁻¹` azimuth form is ill-conditioned at noon in summer (cos α → 0); use the identity, not the formula, at H ≈ 0.
- Exceptions / failure mode: the solstice identity needs the correct sign of δ for the hemisphere; at |φ| < 23.45° the summer-solstice form must be read as `90° − |φ − δ|` and the sun may be on the poleward side. Trade-offs: none — this is free. Symptom: an implementation passes every site test at mid-latitudes and returns a poleward noon azimuth for a tropical plot, or returns a `cos⁻¹` blow-up at summer noon, and the orientation table is wrong only where the project actually is.

### EQ-05 Convert altitude to shadow with cot α and state the reference height in tiles
- Rule: a shading question is a length question. Shadow length = obstruction height × `cot α` = height / tan α. Everything about self- and context-shading reduces to one trigonometric factor and one height, both of which the agent can hold in a tile table.
- Evidence: the geometric identity read off EQ-01's altitude definition; no external constant enters. The worked payload is the sun-position table under **Sun position table** — the two reference latitudes, three dates (solstices and equinox) × three times (morning, noon, afternoon), each row carrying altitude α, azimuth Az and shadow factor, plus the reference `SF` row for any computed α (pure trigonometry, no source dependency). Cross-reference `environmental-design.md` EN-11, which already carries the section form `P = (G + H)/tan(αp)` for overhang projection with αp the solar *profile* angle.
- Source: geometric identity from the altitude definition (EQ-01); no external constant. Table computed here from `cot α` (see **Sun position table**). EN-11's overhang form sources: Passive Solar Architecture overhang calculator, T4, and WBDG *Sun Control and Shading Devices*, T1. Source type: MATHEMATICS (identity) + corpus rule EN-11.
- Class: FACT — a trigonometric identity; the acceptance line on top of a shadow is set elsewhere (EQ-07, EQ-08) and is policy, not geometry.
- Scope: any obstruction-to-shadow question on level ground — inter-block spacing, courtyard walls, a podium edge, a brise-soleil cast — and every building type; the height it is applied to must be a stated section value, not an assumed one.
- Confidence: High.
- Grid translation: precompute `SF(α) = 1/tan α` and multiply by obstruction height in tiles. Shadow length in tiles = `obstruction_tiles × SF`. See **Sun position table** for the full worked set.
- Exceptions / failure mode: plan-view shadow length applies to a *horizontal* surface; a vertical façade's shading needs the profile angle in the façade's own plane, not α. Cast shadows on sloping ground differ. Trade-off: profile-angle-correct computation is exact and per-façade; the α table is coarse and reusable across all orientations. Symptom: a façade-shading or overhang question is answered with the plan factor, which is the wrong plane, and the resulting soffit depth is neither exact nor auditable.

### EQ-06 Separate the three shading causes and report them separately
- Rule: self-shading (the building's own massing), same-site shading (another building on the same plot) and city shading (off-plot obstruction) have different costs and different remedies: self-shading is a plan edit, same-site is a plot-layout edit, city is a fixed constraint the design cannot change.
- Evidence: the taxonomy is this file's; the obstruction/plot side rests on the BRE site-layout summary cited at Source below, and the daylight/shading pairing is already stated in-corpus at EN-11, whose self-shading wording is quoted there.
- Source: EN-11 (self-shading in plan is representable, in section it is not; "shading a façade also dims it"); BRE site-layout guidance as summarised by Anstey Horne, *Daylight and Sunlight: BRE Guidelines* — https://www.ansteyhorne.co.uk/news/daylight-and-sunlight-bre-guidelines — T3, for the obstruction/plot side. Source type: GUIDE (T3) + corpus rule.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE — a reporting structure imposed on a physics result; the underlying geometry is EQ-05's FACT.
- Scope: universal; acute in hotels on tight urban plots.
- Confidence: High for the taxonomy; Medium for the source figures.
- Grid translation: for each façade tile classify `shaded_by ∈ {self, site, city, none}` and report the three shares per direction band. Enforce the EN-11 validation `0.2 ≤ equator_band_shaded_share ≤ 0.6`; add `context_injected: true/false`, and when false refuse every "the façade is shaded" claim.
- Exceptions / failure mode: the grid cannot see an off-plot building unless the agent injects it as a context volume; without that injection all city shading is silently zero. Trade-off: maximising self-shading is the cheapest cooling move and the most expensive daylight move (EN-11 states the pairing). Symptom: a compact massing scores as well-shaded while its equator band has been dimmed, and the reported share attributes to design intent what is actually a missing context model.

### EQ-07 Size the gap between two blocks from the sun altitude you will accept, and read plot coverage as its consequence
- Rule: tower spacing is not a taste question. Requiring sun on the base of the opposing façade at hour t on date d fixes the gap: `G ≥ (H_block − h_target) × cot α(φ, d, t)`. This is the arithmetic EN-02 and EN-11 assert but never compute. Coverage and a height limit are the same relation read from the other end: together they fix how much of the plot's own ground and façades can ever see sun, so two plots of identical yield can have opposite solar access — and a plan engine can rank them.
- Evidence: the relation is the identity from EQ-05. The orientation-sensitivity inputs and the corpus's plan-form counterpart are the citations at Source below. The worked set behind the gaps computed here is the sun-position table (the two reference latitudes × three dates × three times).
- Source: identity from EQ-05. Orientation-sensitivity input from EN-02 (WBDG *Daylighting*, T1: "Deviation from due south should not exceed 15°"; BRE-derived ±30°, Anstey Horne T3; corpus rule). The corpus's plan-form counterpart is EN-14 (perimeter-to-area as the heat-loss/gain proxy, compactness index φ, Straube BSD-061 T3). Source type: MATHEMATICS + corpus rule (EN-02, EN-14).
- Class: FACT for the geometric consequence only. No spacing ratio here is a CODE REQUIREMENT: the ratios a planner would quote are policy figures, and none is sourced (see Confidence).
- Scope: courtyard and slab typologies, hospital and campus plans, podium towers, multi-building and resort plots.
- Confidence: High for the geometry; NOT ESTABLISHED for any code-mandated minimum gap or permitted coverage. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the planning spacing ratios themselves (e.g. "1 × height", "1.5 × height") and every statutory coverage, height or GFA figure — obtain the site's own development-control separation and coverage table before quoting any spacing *requirement*; what is established here is the geometric *consequence*, not the statutory number.
- Grid translation: work the arithmetic once for the reference latitudes. At φ = 40°N on 21 Dec, 09:00 (α = 13.95°, SF = 4.03): a 60-tile (30 m) block shading a sill at 1.8 tiles (0.9 m) needs `G ≥ (60 − 1.8) × 4.03 = 234 tiles = 117 m`. Same block at noon (α = 26.55°, SF = 2.00): `G ≥ 116 tiles = 58 m`. At φ = 25°N the winter-noon factor drops to SF = 1.13 → `G ≥ 66 tiles = 33 m`. Then the site read: `shadow_footprint_tiles ≈ block_height_tiles × cot α` per block at the declared date and hour, evaluated against `site_tiles` — at φ = 40°N, 21 Dec noon a 60-tile block lays ~120 tiles (60 m) of shadow; the same block at φ = 25°N (SF = 1.13) lays ~68 tiles (34 m). Report the required gap per facing pair, the plot area it consumes, and `sunlit_site_share` per season, and never convert the last into a comfort or energy claim.
- Exceptions / failure mode: a narrow courtyard (see EN-08's exception on "opposed aspects looking onto a self-shaded void") satisfies daylight *geometry* between blocks while failing daylight *inside* both. The shadow-footprint estimate assumes flat ground, lumps each block into one height, and ignores sky reflected from surrounding surfaces. Trade-off: gap buys sun and costs plot ratio; every extra tile of gap is 0.5 m × façade length of developable area lost, and lowering coverage to keep winter sun on a courtyard trades gross floor area directly. Symptom: a site plan is ranked on `sunlit_site_share` as though it were a comfort or energy result, or a borrowed "one times height" spacing is quoted as a requirement the site's own controls never issued.

### EQ-08 Screen obstruction with the 25° daylight test and the 45° no-sky-line test before modelling any lux
- Rule: two cheap angle tests decide whether a site layout is even worth simulating. They are plan-and-section tests on obstruction height and distance — exactly what a grid with heights-per-floor can do.
- Evidence: Anstey Horne (T3), as in EQ-06: VSC "> 27%: Ideal daylight conditions"; deterioration flagged when daylight is "reduced by more than 20%"; "the 45° rule in plan and elevation"; sunlight calculations applied only to glazing "within 90° of due south"; amenity/sun hours — "at least two hours of sunlight on 21 March"; passive-solar orientation "within ±30° of due south". Right to Light Surveyors, *45 Degree Rule & 25 Degree Rule* (T4; URL recorded at the Source line below and in **Sources**) — practice summary: an obstruction subtending "**less than 25 degrees** for the whole development … unlikely to substantially affect the diffuse daylight", with the angle taken at the centre of the lowest habitable-room window; noticeable loss when the "centre of the window lies within the 45-degree angle on both plan and elevation".
- Source: Anstey Horne, *Daylight and Sunlight: BRE Guidelines* (T3; URL recorded at EQ-06 and in **Sources**) and Right to Light Surveyors, *45 Degree Rule & 25 Degree Rule* — https://www.righttolightsurveyors.co.uk/45-degree-and-25-degree-lines-rules-of-thumb/ — T4 (practice summary). Source type: GUIDE (T3) + practitioner summary (T4).
- Class: HEURISTIC, and specifically a **policy/planning line** rather than physics: the 25° and 45° values are BRE-derived harm tests used by a planning authority, not performance targets a design sets for itself. Jurisdiction of the underlying guide: England (BRE guidance is planning material in England, advisory elsewhere).
- Scope: UK/Ireland-derived planning practice (BRE guidance is planning material in England, advisory elsewhere); the tests address a *new* obstruction acting on *existing* windows.
- Confidence: Medium — the thresholds are consistent between the two pages and with the corpus's existing ±30° citation, but both are secondary summaries of BRE *Site Layout Planning for Daylight and Sunlight* (IE 245 / BRE 350). **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** read the BRE guide's own numbers — the 1.5% VSC minimum and the 27% "no material loss" value, the 20%/2° heavily-built-up urban concession, and the "50 % of windows" pass rule — none of which the fetched pages carried.
- Grid translation: obstruction angle `θ = atan((H_obs − H_window_centre)/D)`, heights in tiles at 6 tiles = 3.0 m per storey. Gate: `θ ≤ 25°` → report "no material diffuse-daylight harm, angle test only"; `θ > 25°` → the facing pair must be worked through EQ-07 and EQ-12, and the pair is also inside the 45° no-sky-line region in plan *and* section. Report `windows_in_no_sky_region / total_windows` against the VSC "> 27 % ideal" reference.
- Exceptions / failure mode: both are harm tests for a planning application, not performance targets for a design. Using the 25° test as a design aspiration is a category transfer the sources do not license. The 45° rule needs both plan and section, and this host has no section — it must be reconstructed from the 6-tiles-per-storey assumption. Trade-off: passing the angle tests skips a simulation but quantifies nothing interior; that is the correct order of work, not an excuse. Symptom: a design is declared "daylight-compliant" because its own windows clear the 25° line, and the interior — the thing the test never measured — is unlit.

### EQ-09 Report sun-hours per façade orientation; refuse irradiance
- Rule: the honest plan-level solar output is a *count of hours* the sun is up and lies within ±90° of a façade normal. Irradiance (W/m²), annual exposure (kWh/m²) and interior lux all need climate or material data the grid does not have.
- Evidence: the hour-angle and sunrise-hour-angle equations read at EQ-01; the reference counts are computed here from those equations at the stated latitudes, with the day-length payload in the note under **Sun position table**. Cross-reference EN-01, which forbids applying an environmental rule with an undeclared climate.
- Source: hour-angle and sunrise-hour-angle equations from EQ-01 (PVEducation, T3; URLs recorded at EQ-01 and in **Sources**). Values below are computed here from those equations at the stated latitudes. Source type: GUIDE (T3) equations (PVEducation, as EQ-01) plus arithmetic performed here.
- Class: FACT as arithmetic; the *meaning* is a modelling choice — an unobstructed horizon and apparent solar time are assumed, and no published threshold participates.
- Scope: any type, any climate. What the number means flips with EN-01's governing load — a heating-dominated scheme maximises the equator band, a cooling-dominated one minimises the E/W bands.
- Confidence: High for the counts as arithmetic; the counts are conditional on an assumed unobstructed horizon and apparent solar time, which are modelling choices rather than measurements.
- Grid translation: for each façade run with true normal `b` (EQ-03), `sun_hours(b, season) = |{(d, t) : α > 0 ∧ |b − Az| ≤ 90°}| × Δt`. Reference values computed from `ωs = cos⁻¹(−tan φ tan δ)`: at φ = 40°N day length is **9.15 h on 21 Dec** (ωs = 68.7°) and **14.85 h on 21 Jun** (ωs = 111.3°); at φ = 25°N, **10.44 h on 21 Dec** (ωs = 78.3°). These are *day lengths*, not façade exposures — a shaded E or W band can take far less. Publish the band table and the two refusals (`no_irradiance`, `no_lux`) with it.
- Exceptions / failure mode: sun-up hours are not irradiance. The same four-hour count is returned for a clear winter morning and an overcast one, and the metric carries no sky condition, no terrain horizon, no atmospheric extinction and no pollution or albedo. Trade-off: sunshine hours are free, reproducible and auditable from three inputs; irradiance needs a climate file the host does not carry, and importing one buys accuracy at the price of a data source no reviewer can check by hand. Symptom: an hour count is read across as a gain or energy statement — a bright summer morning and a turbid one score identically, and the cooling estimate inherits the difference silently.

### EQ-10 Decompose daylight factor into its three components and state that only one is geometric
- Rule: `DF = 100 × E_interior / E_horizontal_unobstructed_outdoor = SC + ERC + IRC`. The sky component is geometry; the externally reflected component needs the reflectance of an obstruction; the internally reflected component needs the reflectance of the room's own surfaces. A tile grid has no reflectance field, so two of three terms are unavailable, and the IRC is the term that makes deep plans work at all.
- Evidence: the component structure as used by the standards already in the corpus, cited at Source below. The depth-of-daylighting ratios that make the IRC term decisive are the multiplier set at EQ-11 and the reach arithmetic at EQ-12.
- Source: component structure as used by the standards already in the corpus (EN-18 carries EN 15251's daylight-factor categories > 5 % / > 3 % / > 2 % via the REHVA Journal, T1/T3; EN-04 carries EN 17037:2018's illuminance-based targets) — in-corpus citations; the standard texts themselves were not re-read this pass. Source type: STANDARD structure (T1/T3 as cited in EN-18) + gap.
- Class: FACT for the decomposition (it is a definition), STANDARD for the bands it feeds — those categories are a CEN label for perceived lightness, not a physical threshold.
- Scope: all daylit rooms; the decomposition is what tells an agent why its proxy fails in a light-welled courtyard.
- Confidence: High for the decomposition; NOT ESTABLISHED for the calculation constants. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the DF "simple calculation method" — the glazing-efficiency table, the **0.85 maintenance/design factor**, the projection-angle (inverted-tangent) tables and the room-index form of the IRC — could not be obtained this pass. CIBSE Guide A §5 and BRE/IE 310 must be opened before any DF number is printed. **This file publishes the structure and explicitly no coefficients.**
- Grid translation: emit `DF_claimed = false` unless a simulation is attached. Where an IRC-driven design is being judged (light-coloured linings, white soffits), flag `irc_available = false` and route the decision to EN-07's programme placement instead.
- Exceptions / failure mode: DF is a overcast-sky, diffuse-only metric and says nothing about glare or annual sufficiency — which is why EN 17037 and LEED moved to illuminance-based metrics (EQ-13). Trade-off: DF is computable by hand and stable across designs; sDA needs a weather file and a ray-tracer and is what schemes actually certify against. Symptom: a light-welled or light-coloured scheme is scored on the geometric term alone and fails the review, because the two unavailable components are exactly where its performance lives.

### EQ-11 Do not average the published depth multipliers — they measure different lengths
- Rule: EN-04 lists four "×" rules for daylight reach and treats them as competing estimates of one quantity. At least one of them uses a different reference length, so the list is not a range and the file's tile conversion is wrong for that entry.
- Evidence: the four depth-of-daylighting ratios as recorded at EN-04: (i) IECC/ASHRAE 90.1 sidelight daylight zone — depth "up to 1.0 times the height from the floor to the top of the fenestration" (UpCodes, T1); (ii) BRANZ Level — useful illumination "to a depth of approximately 1.5 times the window head height" (T1); (iii) WBDG — "the depth of daylight penetration is about two and one-half times the distance between the top of a window and the sill" (T1); (iv) EN 17037:2018 — no multiplier: ≥ 300 lx median over ≥ 50 % of the reference plane, ≥ 100 lx over ≥ 95 %, for ≥ 50 % of daylight hours (≈ 2190 h), plane at 0.85 m.
- Source: the four as quoted at `environmental-design.md` EN-04 — UpCodes *Sidelight Daylight Zone* (IECC/ASHRAE 90.1 lineage, T1); BRANZ Level *Passive design for daylight in a home* (T1); WBDG *Daylighting* (T1); BS EN 17037:2018 — all as cited in EN-04; URLs and tiers recorded there and in **Sources**, not re-read this pass. Source type: STANDARD (T1 × 3, as cited in EN-04).
- Class: STANDARD — four label/code reach rules, each with its own jurisdiction and edition as named at Source: the US energy-code sidelight definition, New Zealand housing guidance, US federal guidance, and the CEN daylight standard. All four are policy statements about acceptable daylight, not physical limits.
- Scope: every room claiming daylight; universal.
- Confidence: High — each wording is quoted in-corpus.
- Grid translation: with head H = 2.1 m and sill 0.9 m, glazing height = 1.2 m. (i) 1.0 × 2.1 = **2.1 m = 4 tiles**. (ii) 1.5 × 2.1 = **3.15 m = 6 tiles**. (iii) 2.5 × 1.2 = **3.0 m = 6 tiles**. - Exceptions / failure mode: (iii) is about the *glazing height*, not the head height; only (i) and (ii) share a datum. Trade-offs: none — this is a correction. **CONFLICT with EN-04 (a corpus conflict, not this file's error):** environmental-design.md's own declared conversion "2.5 H = 11 tiles" (5.25 m) reads WBDG's 2.5 × as a head-height multiple; on the source's own wording it is 2.5 × glazing height = 3.0 m = 6 tiles, so the published daylight zone is overstated by ~75 %. ### EQ-12 Compute the maximum plan depth from the daylight arithmetic, then read off the plate geometry
- Rule: depth is the variable that decides the section, the core size, the lift count and the mechanical regime. Given a reach rule and a floor-to-floor height, the maximum depth is arithmetic, and its consequence for a hotel floor versus an office plate is visible in tiles.

- Source: reach multipliers from EQ-11 (EN-04's T1 sources) + International WELL Building Institute, *WELL Building Standard v1 — Daylight lighting / Daylight modelling* — https://standard.wellcertified.com/v1/light/daylight-modelling — T1 (Part 1 credit floor, quoted at Evidence above) + `environmental-design.md` EN-03 plate caps. Source type: STANDARD (T1) multipliers as re-sourced at EQ-11 + T1 credit floor (WELL v1) + arithmetic performed here.
- Class: ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT for the derived depth arithmetic; STANDARD for the floors it is checked against, and those floors are **certification policy targets** (the WELL part of this rule is a scheme threshold, and EN-03's caps are guidance-derived), not physical limits.
- Scope: every plate whose depth is a design choice — offices, hotels, hospitals, schools. Not retail boxes, terminals or industrial halls, where depth is set by the process and the answer is artificial lighting.
- Confidence: High for the arithmetic; Medium for the reach multiplier each figure inherits, since EQ-11 shows four published values that are not interchangeable.
- Grid translation: single-aspect reach at the 1.5 × H reading = 3.15 m = 6 tiles. **Hotel guest floor** (guest band + corridor + guest band, floor-to-floor 3.0 m): two opposed 8-tile (4.2 m) guest bands exceed the 6-tile single-sided reach by 2 tiles each, so the bathroom/closet end of the room is the shadow band — which is exactly EN-07's prescription, and it is why the hotel typology survives at 8.4 m room depth while an office layout at the same depth does not. - Exceptions / failure mode: assumes an unobstructed overcast sky, a mid-latitude diffuse resource and a head height the section may not permit; a light shelf, toplighting or a light-well changes the bound and is separately constrained (EQ-15, EQ-16, EN-24). Trade-off: a shallower plate buys daylight with façade frontage and structure cost; a deeper plate buys land efficiency and pays in lighting power (EN-20), risers and ductwork (EN-21) and fire-strategy complexity (EN-23). Symptom: a plate depth is justified by "daylight" while the shadow band it creates is unpriced, and the lighting, riser and fire consequences arrive later as findings.

- Class: HEURISTIC — a geometric consequence this file derives, not a published planning or code rule; no jurisdiction or edition attaches to it.

### EQ-17 Compute solar gain as a four-factor product and keep each factor separately owned
- Rule: `Q = A_glass × I_surface × SHGC × Fd`. Area belongs to the plan, irradiance belongs to a climate file, SHGC belongs to the glass specification, and the direction/obstruction factor belongs to geometry. Mixing them is how a plan engine ends up "calculating" a cooling load.
- Evidence: the factor structure is the fenestration gain form behind the code citation at Source below (caps set by climate zone with orientation exceptions, and a higher skylight figure where daylight controls are provided). The executable payload is the relation itself and the unit-free ratios it yields — halving either of the two plan-controlled factors halves the product exactly — since the absolute irradiance term is the acknowledged gap.
- Source: the factor structure is the fenestration gain form behind EN-12's code citation — UpCodes, *Maximum U-factor and SHGC*, C402.5.3, T1 (IECC, US model energy code; caps by climate zone, orientation exceptions, the 0.60 skylight SHGC where daylight controls are provided); cited at `environmental-design.md` EN-12, URL recorded there and in **Sources**. Source type: CODE (T1) coefficient structure via EN-12's IECC citation + arithmetic; the irradiance input is an acknowledged gap.
- Class: FACT for the product relation and every ratio derived from it. The SHGC/U maxima it plugs into are CODE REQUIREMENTS — IECC C402.5.3, US model energy code, edition as cited at EN-12, values set by climate zone — and are policy numbers, not physics.
- Scope: any glazed band, any climate; the sign of the conclusion is set by EN-01's declared governing load, not by this rule.
- Confidence: High for the factor structure and for every ratio derived from it; NOT ESTABLISHED for the absolute `I_surface` term, which is the only thing that turns W into kWh. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the numeric peak and monthly vertical-surface irradiance values and the `Fd` tables (CIBSE Guide A solar data; ASHRAE *Fundamentals* solar-radiation chapter) were not read this pass — no W/m² appears in this file.
- Grid translation: compute what needs no climate data — the **ratios**, which are unit-free and latitude-free: SHGC 0.40 → 0.25 cuts transmitted direct gain by **37.5 %**; 0.60 → 0.35 by **41.7 %**; halving glazing area halves gain, exactly, in any incidence and any climate. Absolute W and annual kWh require the missing `I_surface` and must be refused with `blocked_claim = "gain_kWh"`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: SHGC is incidence-dependent and falls as the sun's angle to the normal grows, so the product understates gain near normal incidence and overstates it at grazing angles; frame fraction, divider shadow and the reveal cut `A_glass` below pane area, and internal blinds stop glare without stopping heat (EN-11). Trade-off: keeping the four factors separate forces the agent to obtain one input it does not have; collapsing them into a single watts-per-m²-of-glass figure is faster, unauditable, and the standard way a plan engine manufactures a cooling load out of nothing. Symptom: a watts-per-m² rule of thumb is applied to a plan area and returned as a cooling load, with the climate file hidden inside the constant.

### EQ-18 Set WWR as a per-orientation trade window and reconcile it against the area duty
- Rule: glass is a two-way load — winter loss against summer gain against a daylight credit (EN-12). There is a band, it moves with orientation and climate, and it is not the same constraint as the per-room glazed-run minimum, which can be satisfied while the room still fails on reach.
- Evidence: EN-12's three published bounds, unchanged — the code caps (the IECC section cited at Source; T1); the simulated office study varying WWR 0–100 % in subtropical southern Hunan, electricity demand rising "about 100 kW·h for each 20 % increase in WWR" with the conclusion that further increase "is not beneficial" (Li et al., *IJERPH* 18, 2021, T2); and the measured office finding that full-height windows occupying **20–40 % of the façade** give the most daylight, with complaints above ~600 lx vertical illuminance (Karimoshaver & Derakhshan, *J. Daylighting* 13:167, T2). Conversion from EN-12: `WWR ≈ 0.556 × glazed_tiles/façade_tiles`.
- Source: EN-12's citations, unchanged — UpCodes, *Maximum U-factor and SHGC* (T1; US model energy code section as at EQ-17); Li et al., *IJERPH* (T2, article and year as quoted at Evidence above); Karimoshaver & Derakhshan, *J. Daylighting* (T2, article as recorded at EN-05/EN-12) + the host conversion. Source type: CODE (T1, IECC) + PEER-REVIEWED (T2 x2) + a measured field study, all as cited at EN-12, plus the host conversion.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT for the caps (IECC C402.5.3, US model energy code, maximum WWR by climate zone with orientation exceptions and tag-based exemptions); STANDARD for the daylight-optimum band, which is a measured finding about one office sample; the tile conversion is host fact.
- Scope: mechanically conditioned types - hotels, offices, hospitals, schools. Retail frontage, atria, museums and exercise yards are exempt by tag under EN-12 with the equipment consequence still reported.
- Confidence: High that a bounded band exists and that the area duty and the reach duty are independent; Medium for the transferability of every magnitude — the Hunan figure is one climate and one system, the 20-40 % reading is one office sample.
- Grid translation: the tile table, at 1 tile = 0.5 m, 3.0 m floor-to-floor and the declared 0.75 m² glass / 1.35 m² wall per façade tile:

  | glazed : façade | 1:4 | 1:3 | 1:2 | 2:3 | 1:1 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | WWR | 0.14 | 0.19 | 0.28 | 0.37 | 0.56 |

  Report per band against EN-12's declared caps (equator 0.25–0.35, E/W ≤ 0.20) and against the 20–40 % daylight-optimum reading, and publish the paired `daylit_share`. **The reconciliation an agent must actually do:** EN-06's area duty (1 glazed tile per 30 interior tiles) and EN-04's reach duty (6 tiles deep at 1.5 × H) are *independent*. A guest room 5.0 m deep × 3.0 m wide = 15 m² = 60 tiles needs 2 glazed tiles on a 6-tile façade → glazed/façade = 0.33 → WWR 0.185, inside every cap, and the room still fails: its far 2 tiles are beyond reach. Passing the area test is not passing daylight.
- Exceptions / failure mode: the 0.556 conversion holds only at the declared 1.5 m glass height and 2.7 m clear height; spandrel panels, frame depth, reveals and a two-storey module all move real WWR away from the tile count in ways the grid cannot see. Trade-offs: widening the band buys daylight autonomy and view and pays in winter loss, summer gain, acoustic rating and glare risk (EN-13) - the same tiles drawn on four different ledgers, which is why the band must be published before the walls (EN-05). Symptom: a façade passes its WWR cap and its per-room glazing minimum simultaneously, and the room is still dark at the back wall because neither test measured depth.

### EQ-19 Keep U-value and SHGC as separate decisions with opposite climate signs
- Rule: U is a year-round loss coefficient that dominates in heating climates; SHGC is a gain coefficient that only bites when the surface is irradiated, and dominates in cooling climates. The "low U and low SHGC" specification that reads as diligence is a sign error on one of the two climates — the flip EN-01 exists to catch.
- Evidence: the two coefficients the code caps, and the way it caps them, are the citation at Source below; the climate gate that decides which one bites is EN-01's rule (no environmental rule may be applied with the climate driver undeclared).
- Source: EN-12 (IECC C402.5.3 sets maximum U-factor *and* SHGC by climate zone, with orientation-specific exceptions — UpCodes, T1) and EN-01 (no environmental rule may be applied with the climate driver undeclared). Source type: CODE (T1) via EN-12's IECC citation + corpus rule EN-01.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT — two independent maxima set by a code, jurisdiction IECC / US model energy code, section as quoted at Source, values by climate zone; each coefficient is also a label threshold in the ASHRAE/IECC sense, i.e. policy, not physics.
- Scope: every glazed type in a conditioned climate; the pairing is genuinely near-neutral only where neither load dominates, which is a narrow band of EN-01's four declarations.
- Confidence: High - the code sets a maximum for each coefficient separately, by climate zone, with orientation exceptions.
- Grid translation: emit `glaze_spec = {U_max, SHGC_max, climate_zone, jurisdiction, edition}` and refuse to instantiate either value without all four. Where the brief names a product rather than a climate, report the missing declaration rather than the product.
- Exceptions / failure mode: neither coefficient is expressible on the grid, so this rule governs a declaration and a procurement instruction rather than a computation - and a product name is not a value. Trade-off: one glazing type across the whole façade is cheap to buy and wrong in at least two orientations at once; graded glazing by band saves both loads and adds procurement and installation complexity, plus a warranty boundary. - Rule: a drained and back-ventilated rain screen sheds water because the cavity pressure equals the face pressure. That is a ratio rule — vent area against sealed face area, with compartment size and a drainage boundary — not a depth rule, and the depth is the least important of its dimensions.
- Evidence: the mechanism (pressure equalisation as a ratio relation, drainage boundary, compartmentation) is stated qualitatively; the topology the grid can check is in Grid translation.
- Source: standards named, none read this pass — see Confidence for the candidate sources reached and the ones to obtain. Source type: GAP - standards named, none read this pass.
- Class: ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT for the topological checks (drained, back-ventilated, compartmentalised, screen outside the airtight line); the sizing rule is an unsourced gap, and any cavity width or vent ratio a reviewer expects would be a manufacturer's tested value, not a plan dimension.
- Scope: drained and back-ventilated claddings. Not face-sealed systems or masonry veneer, which fail by different mechanisms and need different checks.
- Confidence: NOT ESTABLISHED for every magnitude; High only for the topological checks the grid can genuinely perform. **NEEDS VERIFICATION.** Three candidate sources were reached and yielded no readable body: BILDA, *Cavity and joints in rainscreen systems*; Benjamin Obdyke, *A Guide to Pressure-Equalized and Drained, Back-Ventilated Rainscreen Systems*; and the index record of the review "Pressure equalization of rainscreen walls: a critical review". Symptom: a cavity drawn as a tile gap is reported as a drained, pressure-equalised rain screen, and the leak path a joint would create is invisible to every check that passed.

### EQ-22 Budget thermal bridging at slab edges and reveals as length times a coefficient
- Rule: EN-14 says junctions leak; the quantified form is `ΔQ_bridge = Σ Ψ_i · l_i · ΔT` [Ψ in W/(m·K), l in m]. A hotel perimeter generates one full slab edge per storey per façade metre, so the bridge length is enormous and scales with exactly the plan variables an agent controls.
- Evidence: the corpus rule cited at Source below — EN-14's compactness result with its 25 % small-building / 10 % large-building shape effect. The length term is host arithmetic; the coefficient term is the gap.
- Source: `environmental-design.md` EN-14 (in-corpus, citing Straube BSD-061 T3); DesignBuilder v7 help, *Linear Thermal Bridges at Junctions* — https://designbuilder.co.uk/helpv7.0/Content/LinearThermalBridges.htm — T3, which confirms the junction categories and the default-value degradation quoted in Confidence below, but prints no Ψ table. Source type: GUIDE (T3) + corpus rule EN-14; the numeric Ψ values are a named gap.
- Class: FACT for the relation and for the length scaling; the Ψ and Y values an auditor would want are published tabulations (the BRE inventory named in Confidence, plus national energy-method defaults) and are jurisdiction-specific policy data — none is asserted here.
- Scope: every junction of an insulated envelope - slab edges, window reveals, balconies, party floor to façade. Worst in repeated-cell buildings, where junction length per m² of floor is highest, which includes the flagship hotel.
- Confidence: High that bridge length scales with exactly the variables a plan controls; NOT ESTABLISHED for any Ψ magnitude printed in this file. Values: **NEEDS VERIFICATION.** Three sources were fetched: SEAI *DEAP Guide 4.3.1 — Thermal Bridging* returned 403; Kingspan GB *What are Psi-values and Y-values* returned no article body; DesignBuilder v7 help, *Linear Thermal Bridges at Junctions* (T3; URL at the Source line above) — confirms the junction categories and that default values are "degraded by the greater of 0.04 W/mK or 50 %", but prints no Ψ table. Prohibited: printing any W/(m·K) value.
- Exceptions / failure mode: an agent cannot distinguish a treated from an untreated detail from a plan, so the entire quantified benefit of a certified detail is invisible here while its cost in coordination is not. Trade-off: a real Ψ needs a section heat-flow calculation (BS EN ISO 10211) or an accredited-detail database; the grid's substitute is a length times a number it does not have, so the rule prices length and refuses the coefficient. Symptom: a façade is credited with "thermally broken detailing" while the junction length it adds is still in the budget, so the improvement is claimed and the cost is measured on an unimproved number.

- Rule: surface and interstitial condensation follow from whether any internal surface stays above the adjacent air's dew point. The standard check is a surface temperature factor against a threshold — one geometry term and two climate/usage terms, of which the plan supplies only the geometry.
- Evidence: EN-01's climate declaration gates every environmental rule and EN-23's comfort bands give the usage side; the geometric risk factors are the plan-side terms this rule can actually assert, and the criterion value itself is the gap named below.
- Source: `environmental-design.md` EN-01 and EN-23 (in-corpus; EN-23 citing ASHRAE 55 / EN 16798-1 via Climate4Buildings, T3); the hygrothermal standard and the unread condensation-resistance part are named in Confidence below. Source type: STANDARD named and not read (BS EN ISO 13788) + corpus rules EN-01, EN-23.
- Class: STANDARD — the criterion is a hygrothermal test defined by a named standard with a national adoption, and the threshold value itself is NOT ESTABLISHED here, so only the geometric part of the test is executable.
- Scope: heated, cooled and intermediate climates alike; most consequential in high-humidity programmes - pools, kitchens, laundries, hospital wet zones, archives.

### EQ-24 Give every façade plane a movement limit and a fire limit in storeys and metres
- Rule: cladding systems have expansion-controlled run lengths and compartmentalised cavities; both are dimensional, both are invisible to a plan, and both can veto a façade design that scores perfectly on daylight and gain.
- Evidence: the only constraint the grid can currently express is CN-28's test that the `facade line [is] identical on all floors`, plus the compartment interval derived from the declared section — both in Grid translation below.

- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT in intent — reaction-to-fire classification by storey height and cavity-barrier spacing are statutory (the classification standard and the post-Grenfell England guidance on external wall structure named in Confidence; jurisdiction-specific, edition-specific) — and none of those values is established here. The single derived figure is a geometry consequence of the declared section and is labelled as such.
- Scope: all ventilated claddings and rainscreens; the storey-height element dominates in tall residential, hotel and institutional schemes where cavity continuity is the failure path of record.

### EQ-25 Derive the façade module from the tile and the bay, never from taste

- Source: `multi-scale.md` MS-15 (marked UNCITED there) and MS-01 (frozen control grid and bay pitch), `construction.md` CN-28 (façade sits at a declared relationship to the column line) — in-corpus. Source type: corpus rules (MS-15, MS-01, CN-28) + divisibility arithmetic; fabrication data is a named gap.
- Class: ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT — a divisibility rule of this project's grid plus the corpus's coordination requirement; the manufacturing maxima that would legitimately override it are fabrication data and are not sourced here.
- Scope: any repeated façade, above all cellular hotel and residential plans, where one module repeats several hundred times and a wrongly chosen pitch is multiplied by the storey count.

- Exceptions / failure mode: a real unitised module is set by glass size, transport width, crane capacity, cassette tolerance and fixing buildability - none of which are obliged to respect a 500 mm planning grid. Trade-off: a tile-aligned module makes the plan self-checking and can be fabricationally wasteful; the honest output is a candidate module plus the named fabrication constraint that is entitled to veto it. Symptom: a module is declared coordinated because it divides the grid, and the first shop drawing reveals a panel no supplier will ship or a mullion that cannot land anywhere but on the column.

### EQ-26 Compute sound attenuation from surface density and count the doublings
- Rule: mass law is the only acoustic arithmetic a plan can carry, and it is logarithmic: doubling mass or doubling frequency buys about 6 dB, and real assemblies land well below the prediction because of coincidence and flanking.
- Evidence: AcousPlan, *What is Transmission Loss (TL)?* (T4; URL recorded at the Source line below) — `TL ≈ 20·log10(m·f) − 47` with m in kg/m² and f in Hz; "TL increases by approximately 6 dB for each doubling of the surface mass density", and the same per frequency doubling; resonance/coincidence dips "sometimes by 10 to 15 dB below the mass law prediction"; measured ratings — metal-stud plasterboard partition "STC/Rw around 35 to 40", 200 mm concrete slab "STC/Rw 50 to 55". The worked pair — a doubled thickness against the same source's measured rating for that thickness — is the computation in Grid translation below.
- Source: AcousPlan, *What is Transmission Loss (TL)?* — https://acousplan.com/learn/what-is-transmission-loss — **T4** (vendor/learn page). Source type: VENDOR/LEARN (T4) for the formula and the ratings; the formula is the classic single-leaf law and should be re-checked against Davy or ISO 717-1.
- Class: FACT for the law and for the doublings; the Rw/STC ratings quoted beside it are laboratory **label thresholds** from a secondary page, and a compliance rating is a code figure (Approved Document E, ISO 717-1) this file does not publish.
- Scope: single-leaf walls and floors; it does **not** apply to doubled or decoupled assemblies, where mass law is the wrong model entirely.
- Confidence: Medium — the constants (−47, 6 dB) are consistent with the standard form and are quoted from a page actually read; the Rw ratings are a single secondary source.
- Grid translation: worked, dense concrete at 2400 kg/m³: 100 mm → m = 240 kg/m² → at 500 Hz, `20·log10(240 × 500) − 47 = 54.6 dB`; 200 mm → m = 480 kg/m² → `60.6 dB`, exactly +6.0 dB by construction. Then the honest correction from the same source: measured 200 mm concrete is rated 50–55, i.e. **5 to 11 dB below its own mass-law prediction** — so mass law gives the *trend* plus a 5–11 dB reality discount, and CN-26's decoupled-leaf requirement is a statement that the discount must be closed by detail, not by more concrete. Prohibited: a compliance rating for a named assembly — CN-26 records "value NOT ESTABLISHED" and it still is: read Approved Document E and ISO 717-1 before any DnT,w target.
- Exceptions / failure mode: mass law is a laboratory transmission loss, not an in-situ DnT,w; flanking, apertures and fittings remove more than a rating number can give back. Trade-off: buying dB with mass costs structural load, tile thickness and cost; buying it with planes and distance costs floor area (EN-16, EQ-27). Symptom: a wall is thickened to a mass-law number, the rating is reported as achieved, and flanking through the very slab edge that carries the wall is unaccounted for.

### EQ-27 Price the acoustic buffer in dB and in tiles, and show that mass and distance are interchangeable only up to a point
- Rule: distance attenuation and mass law are two purchasable routes to the same dB, and only one of them costs floor area. The plan engine's advantage is that it can price both in the same units.
- Evidence: free-field point-source inverse-square → `ΔL = 20·log10(r2/r1)` = **−6.02 dB per doubling of distance** (arithmetical consequence of the inverse-square law; no fitted constant). Corpus anchors as read: EN-22 (eNoiseControl, T4, reverberant-field decay "less than 6 dB per doubling of distance"; WHO Regional Europe noise fact sheet, T1, "< 30 dB(A) at night in bedrooms", "< 35 dB(A) in classrooms"); EN-10 (WHO *Guidelines for Community Noise* 1999, T1 via mirror: 35 dB living rooms, 30 dB day/night hospital areas, annoyance beginning above ~55 dB outdoors).
- Source: MATHEMATICS (inverse-square, derived here) + the corpus anchors named at Evidence above (EN-22 and EN-10, with their tiers as recorded there); the statutory route-noise method is named in Confidence and was not read. Source type: MATHEMATICS (inverse-square) + corpus rules EN-10, EN-16, EN-22; the line-source case is a named gap.
- Class: FACT for the point-source arithmetic. The receptor limits it is priced against are **policy targets** — WHO guideline values, adopted by national and local authorities, not physical thresholds — and the line-source and barrier decay a planner would use is a statutory method (named in Confidence) whose numbers are not established here.
- Scope: anything beside a road, rail corridor, air route or plant yard; decisive for hotels, hospitals, schools and residential, which is EN-10's own priority list.
- Confidence: High for the -6.02 dB/doubling arithmetic; NOT ESTABLISHED for route-noise decay, barrier, ground and meteorological terms. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the *line*-source rule (nominally −3 dB per doubling, which is what a road, not a vehicle, gives) and all barrier, ground and weather terms — obtain **ISO 9613-2** or **CNOSSOS-EU**, the method used for statutory route-noise mapping.
- Grid translation: `L_receive = L_source − 20·log10(r/r_ref) − N·Δwall`. Worked: 65 dB(A) at 10 m → at 40 m, `65 − 20·log10(4) = 65 − 12.0 = 53.0 dB(A)` in open ground; each additional façade plane then removes something in the 35–55 Rw order of EQ-26, and the per-plane figure must be declared, not assumed. In tiles: doubling a 20-tile (10 m) buffer to 40 tiles (20 m) buys 6 dB of floor area; doubling a 100 mm wall to 200 mm buys the same 6 dB of *mass* and no floor area but more structure (EQ-26). Encode the result as a `noise_buffer` band whose tile width equals the number of doublings needed to close the dB shortfall from EN-10's exposure ranking, and state which currency was spent.
- Exceptions / failure mode: free-field arithmetic flatters distance. Outdoors a road behaves as a line source and decays far more slowly; indoors the reverberant field gives less than 6 dB per doubling (EN-22). The 6 dB used here is therefore the optimistic bound and must be labelled as such wherever it appears. Trade-off: the two currencies are floor area and structural mass, and EQ-26 shows both buy the same decibels; a scheme beside a busy road can spend either, and the plan's job is to record which was paid, not to claim silence. Symptom: a buffer band is sized with the point-source factor against a road, the real decay is roughly half as fast, and the bedrooms are quiet only in the arithmetic.

### EQ-28 Size ventilation openings by free area, correct the frame for the angle, and keep the depth limit distinct from the daylight depth
- Rule: the regulated quantity is free area, not leaf area, and free area is a function of the hinge geometry. The same window complies at one opening angle and fails at another, so a plan that counts windows rather than projected areas is wrong by a trigonometric factor.
- Evidence: Planning Portal, *Building Regulations: Ventilation* (England — reproducing **Approved Document F, Volume 1, Ventilation for Dwellings**; T1/T3, read this pass — URL recorded at the Source line below): rapid/purge ventilation openable area "at least 1/10th of the floor area" for openings at **15–30°** and "at least 1/20th" for openings **above 30°**; background/trickle ventilation by fixed equivalent area — **8,000 mm²** for habitable rooms and kitchens in multi-storey dwellings, **10,000 mm²** in single-storey dwellings, **4,000 mm²** for bathrooms. Jurisdiction is England; the corpus's own comparison point is EN-19's NCC (Australia) figures — 5 % of floor area directly openable, 10 % borrowed (CCC Engineering, T3).
  - **Depth limit — air reach is not light reach.** Air reach scales with room *height*, light reach with window *head height*; they are unrelated numbers a plan routinely confuses. Sources as recorded at EN-03/EN-09: CCC Engineering's reading of NCC 2025 Part F6 with CIBSE AM10 (T3) — single-sided ≈ 2 × ceiling height ≈ 5.4 m, cross 4–5 × ceiling height ≈ 11–13.5 m; WindowMaster (T3) single-sided ≈ 10 m; WBDG (T1) cross-ventilation maximum width ≈ 45 ft — with EN-03's rounding convention (maximum caps round DOWN to tiles). Compute both reaches per room type: single-sided ventilation reach 5.4 m = 10 tiles versus daylight reach 3.15 m = 6 tiles (EQ-11), so at 2.7 m clear height **ventilation is the binding reach for a naturally cooled cellular plan and daylight is not** — a hotel floor satisfies both with an 8-tile guest band, which is why the typology ventilates single-sided at all, while an office layout is stopped by daylight first. Report `depth_over_ceiling_height` beside `daylit_share`; where the ratio exceeds 2 on a single-sided room set `vent_regime = mechanical_required` and pay EN-21's duct consequence.
- Source: Planning Portal (England), *Building Regulations: Ventilation*, reproducing **Approved Document F Volume 1** — https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/extensions/building-regulations-ventilation — T1/T3; the geometric identity is derived here; the NCC (Australia) comparison and the reach multiples via EN-19/EN-03/EN-09 (CCC Engineering T3, WindowMaster T3, WBDG T1, as recorded there). Source type: CODE (T1/T3 - England, Approved Document F Volume 1, read this pass) + NCC figures via EN-19 + a geometric identity.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT — Approved Document F Volume 1 (Ventilation for Dwellings), jurisdiction England, edition as reproduced on the read page; the fractions and equivalent areas are statutory minima for that jurisdiction and type, and the transfer to other jurisdictions (NCC, Australia, via EN-19) or other building types is a policy comparison, not a physical relation.
- Scope: naturally and hybrid-ventilated schemes. Void where the façade is locked, filtered, security-controlled or acoustically closed - EN-19's named failure mode.
- Confidence: High for the quoted AD F fractions and equivalent areas; Medium for their transfer beyond dwellings, which is the scope of the volume actually read. **The 1/50-of-floor-area background-ventilator ratio commonly quoted in UK practice did not appear in the source read and is therefore not published here; NEEDS VERIFICATION against AD F Volume 1 §5 directly.** **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** an opening-area-to-room-depth ratio from a ventilation-specific standard was not obtained; the AM10 multiples reach this file only through the corpus's secondary reading.
- Grid translation: top-hung/awning leaf: `A_free = A_open × sin θ` (geometric identity), so a 30° opening passes only 0.50 of its leaf area and a 45° opening 0.71 — which is exactly why the code splits its fraction at 30°. With 0.55 m² per vent tile (EN-19's declared constant): an 11 m² room under AD F's 1/10 rule needs 1.1 m² → **2 vent tiles**; under 1/20 it needs 0.55 m² → **1 vent tile**. Trade-off: opening area competes with glazing area, shading geometry and acoustic attenuation for the same strip of wall (EN-11, EN-12, EN-19, EN-10); the grid's honest output is the budget that cannot be spent twice, published before the façade freezes. Every number in both tables is a **policy line drawn on a distribution**, not a physical threshold: below 300 lx an office task gets harder, and nothing in the physics changes at 299. ## Envelope build-up as grid geometry

Host wall = 1 blocked tile = **500 mm of plan**, which is thicker than a realistic cavity or sandwich build-up (EQ-20). The grid therefore over-thickens the envelope; that bias must be subtracted from any area, thermal or acoustic claim derived from wall thickness, in either direction.

| Envelope element | Function | Plan-visible? | Grid rule | Cross-ref |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Outer screen / panel line | rain screen, UV, aesthetics | width only | module must be whole/half tiles; 500–1500 mm | EQ-25, MS-15 |
| Vented + drained cavity | pressure equalisation, drainage | **no** | `vent_area_ratio` null → refuse weathertightness claims | EQ-21, CN-28 |
| Continuous insulation plane | fabric loss | no | bridge length = façade run × storeys | EQ-22, EN-14 |
| Airtight / vapour-control line | condensation, infiltration | no | must sit inside the screen line, unbroken at slab edges | EQ-23, EQ-20 |
| Structure / slab edge | span, fire compartment | ring of tiles | one compartment boundary per 6 tiles (storey) | EQ-24, CN-06 |
| Services zone | duct, riser | blocked band | reserve before rooms | EN-21 |
| Party / separating wall | acoustic tenancy boundary | 2 tiles = 1.0 m | 8 tiles per 2 m of wall, both demises; dB gap 5–11 below mass law | CN-26, EQ-26 |

Dimensional consequences worth stating once: a façade band's *glazing* count is what carries daylight and gain, its *opaque* count is what carries loss (EQ-17, EQ-18); a two-storey cladding unit is 12 tiles tall; and the whole 500 mm envelope allowance is available to absorb a build-up plus a services zone, which real façades cannot do — so a plan that "solves" a duct route inside the wall thickness has found a modelling artefact, not a detail.

## Climate pass procedure

Run before the façade is frozen. Six steps, each with a required output and a required refusal.

1. **Declare the basis.** `climate_profile` (governing load, hemisphere — EN-01), `latitude`, `north_datum_deg` (EQ-03), the section assumptions, and the tile→metre conversion. Refusal: no solar rule fires with a blank basis.
2. **Compute the sun table** for the site latitude at solstices and equinox, 09:00/12:00/15:00 solar time, plus `ωs` day lengths (EQ-01, EQ-04, EQ-09). Pass the two noon identities. Output: the table, not a narrative.
3. **Screen the context.** Angle tests first (25° / 45° / VSC > 27 % — EQ-08), then required gaps between facing blocks (EQ-07), then shaded-ground and self-shading shares (EQ-06, EQ-07). Refusal: no shading claim on a non-injected context.
4. **Set the daylight geometry.** Choose and publish one reach multiplier per room type from the four in EQ-11 (not the mis-indexed 2.5 × H reading), compute `daylit_share`, `shadow_band_depth`, pier limits (EQ-15), then check the plate against EN-03's caps (EQ-12). Record the sDA/ASE obligations as targets with a scheme and edition (EQ-13) and emit `metric_claim: prohibited`.
5. **Price the envelope.** WWR per band against the trade window (EQ-18), SHGC/U as separate signed decisions (EQ-17, EQ-19), junction lengths (EQ-22), condensation status `UNRESOLVED` (EQ-23), build-up total against the 500 mm tile (EQ-20), module coordination (EQ-25).
6. **Buy quiet and air.** Noise: dB shortfall → distance doublings or surface mass, priced both ways (EQ-26, EQ-27) against EN-10's façade ranking and CN-26's party strategy. Air: free area with the angle correction, jurisdiction named, and the depth ratio against ceiling height (EQ-28). Close with EN-26's assumption block and the residual gap register (EN-18).

Gate: the pass is complete when every solar, thermal and acoustic number in the output traces to (a) an equation in this file with its three inputs published, or (b) a named standard with edition and jurisdiction, or (c) a `NEEDS VERIFICATION` tag with what must be obtained. Anything else is a claim the grid cannot carry.

## Weak or contested

- **Prefix collision, decision recorded.** This file was commissioned as "Domain AF with `EN-` rules", but `EN-01…EN-26` are already owned by `environmental-design.md`, which this file exists to reference. Reusing the prefix would have created 26 duplicate identifiers across two files and broken every existing cross-reference of the form "EN-12". Rules here are therefore `EQ-` (envelope quantification), the domain letter AF is preserved, and all `EN-` numbers appearing in this file are the environmental-design rules. Integration (task #2) must map **AF → `envelope-daylight-quantification.md` (EQ-)** and not to EN-.
- **The field schema diverged from the corpus — harmonised.** This file was commissioned on a `Principle / Source / Source Type / Evidence Strength / Applicability / Limitations / Trade-offs / Agent Action` record while the other research files use `Rule / Evidence / Source / Class / Scope / Confidence / Grid translation / Exceptions`. The mapping used at conversion: `Principle` → Rule; `Evidence Strength` plus that rule's needs-verification flags, verbatim → Confidence; `Applicability` → Scope; `Agent Action` → Grid translation; `Limitations` and `Trade-offs` → Exceptions / failure mode, with the failure symptom stated there; `Source Type` folded into Source; `Class` derived per rule (physics relation = FACT, code or label threshold = STANDARD or CODE REQUIREMENT with jurisdiction and edition named, planning rule of thumb = HEURISTIC, host geometry limit = ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT). Where a number is a *policy target* rather than physics it is named as such at the rule and in the **Daylight metric thresholds** note, which already carried that caveat. The schema is now uniform; harmonising field names remains an integration decision, not a content one, and no number moved.
- **EN-04's "2.5 H = 11 tiles" conversion is contradicted by its own quoted source.** WBDG's 2.5 × applies to the distance from window head to sill (1.2 m here), not to head height (2.1 m). The published daylight zone is therefore 6 tiles, not 11. See EQ-11; EN-04 needs the correction, not this file.
- **Solar geometry rests on T3 pages, not on the primary texts.** The altitude/azimuth/declination set is the classical Duffie & Beckman formulation as reproduced by PVEducation and Wikipedia; both were read, neither is the handbook. The 15°/h hour-angle rate is definitional (360°/24 h) rather than quoted — the fetched pages use HRA without tabulating the rate. A single verification pass against Duffie & Beckman ch. 1 (or the NOAA `solcalc` equations PDF, unreachable here) would upgrade every rule in the sun table from T3 to T1.
- **The sun table's precision exceeds its inputs.** Values are quoted to 0.1° because they are computed, but the declination model carries a source-stated absolute error of up to 1° (and up to 2° for the alternative form), and refraction (~0.5° at the horizon) is not modelled. Read the table to the nearest degree; the arithmetic is exact and the astronomy is not.
- **No daylight factor, sDA, ASE or UDI value is computed anywhere in this file, by design.** DF lacks its simple-calculation coefficients (CIBSE Guide A §5 / BRE/IE 310 unread); the climate-based metrics lack a ray-tracer and a weather file. The 300 lx / 50 % / 50 % figures quoted here are the *targets* other files already carry, attributed to them.
- **LEED v4.1's thresholds are asserted-but-unverified in the wider literature and this file repeats none of them.** The USGBC page fetch returned a credit title and a 1–3 point range with no criteria. Anything claiming a specific LEED sDA percentage should be treated as unsourced until the reference guide is read.
- **Mass law is quoted from a T4 learn page.** `−47` as the constant, and the 6 dB doublings, match the standard single-leaf form, and the measured 50–55 vs predicted 60.6 dB comparison is internally consistent within that one page — but one page is one page, and the coincidence-dip "10 to 15 dB" is an unsourced range on it. Re-verify against ISO 717-1 rating practice and a textbook (Davy) before the −47 goes into an executable validator.
- **Free-field −6.02 dB/doubling is safe; the road case is not.** A road is a line source and decays near −3 dB/doubling, and statutory route noise uses ISO 9613-2/CNOSSOS with barrier, ground and meteorological terms. EQ-27 therefore publishes only the point-source arithmetic, which it derives rather than borrows, and refuses the line-source number.
- **Envelope physics is the thinnest part of this file.** Rain-screen pressure equalisation (BS 8208, β ratios, cavity widths), psi-values (BRE IP 1/06), the condensation criterion (BS EN ISO 13788), movement-joint spacing, cavity-barrier spacing and unitised fabrication limits are all present as *structure with a name attached* and no magnitude. Nine of the thirty rules lean on a NEEDS VERIFICATION. That is the honest state of the pass, not a draft to be filled with plausible numbers later.
- **Light shelves: no magnitude at all.** The commissioning brief asked for "light-shelf effect figures"; none could be sourced. EQ-16 forbids the claim instead of inventing it.
- **Latitude dependency is asserted in every thermal rule and cannot be escaped.** The φ = 40° and φ = 25° tables are illustrations, not defaults; the two sites differ by a factor of **1.77** in winter-noon shadow length and by **1.29 h** in winter day length, and a scheme run at the wrong latitude inherits both errors silently.

## Type-specificity audit

- **Universal (type-independent) — the whole of EQ-01…EQ-09 and the structure of EQ-10/EQ-11.** Sun position, shadow factors, obstruction angles, plot geometry and the component decomposition of daylight do not care what is inside the building. Only the *acceptance line* on top of them is type-specific, and it is set in EN-18's target table.
- **Hotels (the flagship case).** The cellular guest floor is the one typology where the daylight and ventilation reaches are both satisfied by accident: an 8-tile (4.0–4.2 m) guest band sits inside single-sided ventilation reach (10 tiles) and mostly inside the 1.5 × H daylight reach (6 tiles), with the bathroom end absorbing the shadow band exactly as EN-07 prescribes. EQ-12's worked case and EQ-28's comparison are built on this. Where hotels do fail quantifiably is the façade *area* duty (EN-06) against the WWR *band* caps (EN-12) at the same time — a corridor-loaded slab has a large glazed count on two bands and none in the middle — and the acoustic budget: a party wall's 5–11 dB mass-law discount (EQ-26) is the whole reason CN-26's decoupled-leaf requirement exists.
- **Offices.** Deep plates are where EQ-12's arithmetic bites: 6.3 m of double-sided reach against an 18 m plate leaves 65 % of the depth in shadow, so an office's sDA obligation (EQ-13) is met by *section* (toplight, atrium) or by lighting design (EN-20), never by plan. Light shelves and glare sit here too, and EQ-16 currently has nothing to offer them.
- **Hospitals and education.** The metric floors are tighter and code-driven: WHO's night-time bedroom and classroom limits quoted at EN-10, EN 12464-1's illuminances at EN-18. The acoustic arithmetic in EQ-26/EQ-27 is the useful part; the solar geometry is the same for any type.
- **Residential.** The jurisdictional spread is widest here (AD F England vs NCC Australia give different openable areas for one room — EQ-28), and the reach multipliers disagree by a factor of 2.5 (EQ-11). A housing scheme cannot be scored without publishing which multiplier it used.
- **Retail, industrial, transport, prisons.** EQ-18's exemptions (frontage, atria) and EN-12's tag-and-record mechanism carry them outside the WWR band; EQ-24's fire-spread logic inverts in places where the process is the reason for the building. Nothing in this file's acoustics transfers to an industrial hall, where the source *is* the product.
- **Where this file is silent by intent.** No lux, no W/m², no kWh, no temperature, no dB(A) at a receptor without a declared source level, no DF, no psi, no Rw compliance rating, no fire classification. Each prohibition is stated as a refusal inside a rule, because a plan engine that can compute sun angles will otherwise be asked to compute everything else too.
