# Research file — Domain AO: Indoor environmental quality outcomes (the exposure and comfort thresholds the quantities are supposed to hit, and what they do to people)

Scope: the **outcome layer**. Everything numerically upstream of this file is already owned. `mep-sizing.md` SV-01…SV-28 owns the *air rate* — outdoor-air two-term demand (people + floor, divided by `Ez`), extract air-changes, thermal load densities, airflow, duct calibre. `envelope-daylight-quantification.md` EQ-01…EQ-28 owns the *physics* — sun geometry, daylight-factor decomposition, climate-based metric thresholds (EQ-13), solar gain, mass-law sound attenuation (EQ-26/EQ-27), ventilation free area (EQ-28). `environmental-design.md` EN-01…EN-26 owns the *target tables already read* — UGR and illuminance by room and NC bands (EN-18), daylight-factor and PMV operative categories from EN 15251 (EN-18), the ASHRAE 55 / EN 16798 adaptive band and its applicability limits (EN-23), the NCC CO₂ 850 ppm ventilation surrogate and openable-area budget (EN-19), WHO community-noise limits (EN-10). `human-behavior.md` HB-* owns the *behaviour* — crowding, privacy, noise-source avoidance (HB-16), concentration protection (HB-23), the door weak-link rule (HB-24). None of those files owns the **health- or comfort-based outcome threshold itself, the evidence that the threshold changes what happens to occupants, or the honest effect size.** This file supplies those and nothing else: it never restates an air rate, a daylight/lux threshold or a behaviour rule — it cites SV-/EQ-/EN-/HB- by ID and moves on to the exposure limit that quantity is meant to serve and what exceeding it does to people.

Boundary, so nobody doubles up:

| Question | Owner | What this file adds |
|---|---|---|
| How much outdoor air a space needs (the rate) | SV-01, SV-02, EN-21 | the *outcome* the rate serves — the health-based pollutant guideline it must hold below, and the fact that CO₂ is a proxy for that outcome, not the outcome |
| Does supplied air reach the breathing zone | SV-01 `Ez`, EN-21 | effectiveness vs air-changes: the adopted `Ez` ladder (displacement 1.2 → mixing 0.7–0.8 → makeup-near-exhaust 0.5), short-circuiting, and the plan levers that move it |
| Daylight/lux metric thresholds (sDA/ASE/UDI/DF/300 lx) | EQ-13, EQ-15, EN-04, EN-18 | light **for people**: melanopic EDI and glare as a comfort outcome — the evidence and the distribution logic, not the lux table |
| Sound attenuation in dB, buffer priced in tiles | EQ-26, EQ-27, CN-26, HB-24 | sound as an **outcome**: reverberation / STI / background-noise criteria by room function with the standard named, and the plan consequence that follows |

| Behaviour at noise/privacy/crowding | HB-16, HB-23, HB-* | nothing — cited by ID, never restated |
| Ventilation / load / airflow magnitudes | SV-* | the effect-size literature those magnitudes are supposed to deliver |

Host grid, unchanged: `1 tile = 0.5 m`, `4 tiles = 1 m²`, `2 tiles = 1.0 m`; floor-to-floor 3.0 m, clear height 2.7 m (declared in `mep-sizing.md` §Inherited declarations). **There is no section, no height field, no temperature, no concentration, no lux sensor and no dB meter on the host.** A plan engine can compute *exposure geometry* — perimeter vs interior depth, distance-to-façade, façade aspect and orientation band, surface/finish absorption area by room, buffer placement, cross-flow opening positions from the EN-19 / EQ-28 openings — and *volume per occupant* (room area × the declared 2.7 m). It **cannot** compute a realised ppm, µg/m³, Bq/m³, °C, %PPD, melanopic EDI, STI or a satisfaction percentage. Those are simulation-or-measurement outcomes and are flagged, never asserted — the exact flag wording is IE-24.

Term/unit discipline: ppm are volumetric at 25 °C / 1 atm unless the source states otherwise; µg/m³ and mg/m³ are mass concentrations; 1 mg/m³ = 1000 µg/m³; Bq/m³ is radon activity; 1 pCi/L = 37 Bq/m³; formaldehyde 1 ppb ≈ 1.23 µg/m³ at 25 °C. Melanopic EDI is "melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance" at the eye, in lux-equivalent units. PMV is the Fanger predicted mean vote (−3 cold … +3 hot, 0 neutral); PPD the companion predicted-percentage-dissatisfied. Conversions are arithmetic; the **thresholds and effect sizes are cited**, and the distinction between a health-based guideline and a ventilation surrogate is carried in every air rule.

## Rules

### IE-01 Separate a health-based guideline from a ventilation surrogate — CO₂ is the surrogate, and the design decision is which one you are defending
- Rule: Before quoting any air number, classify it. A **health-based guideline** names a concentration below which adverse effects are minimal (WHO sets these for PM/NO₂/SO₂/CO/O₃ and for radon/formaldehyde). A **ventilation surrogate** is an operational indicator used when the real pollutant is expensive to measure; CO₂ is the canonical surrogate, and CO₂ itself at typical indoor levels is not the harm. `CO₂ ≤ 1000 ppm` — the number most clients carry into a brief — is **not a health limit** in any WHO or ASHRAE document read this pass: WHO's 2021 AQG set no CO₂ guideline at all (they cover PM₂.₅, PM₁₀, O₃, NO₂, SO₂, CO only), and ASHRAE 62.1 is a ventilation-rate standard framed around *perceived air quality / odour acceptability by a majority of occupants*, not a CO₂ cap. The design decision that turns on this: when CO₂ is high you are being told ventilation is low — the fix is the SV-01 rate / IE-10 effectiveness, **not** "reduce the CO₂ number", because CO₂ is not what makes people ill.
- Evidence: WHO 2021 AQG scope is explicitly PM₂.₅/PM₁₀/O₃/NO₂/SO₂/CO with no CO₂ value. EN 15251's own REHVA restatement has a section "use of increased CO₂ level as IAQ indicator" and states that the CO₂ figures "are not consistent with the required ventilation rates" — i.e. CO₂ is an *indicator*, tracked operationally, and it disagrees with the rate method. The corpus already carries the NCC indoor limit of 850 ppm over 8 h at EN-19 as a ventilation-compliance surrogate (Table F6V1), which is the right reading of a CO₂ number.
- Source: WHO global air quality guidelines 2021 (PDF) — https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/551b515e-2a32-4e1a-a58c-cdaecd395b19/content — **T1**; REHVA Journal, *Revision of EN 15251: Indoor Environmental Criteria* — https://www.rehva.eu/rehva-journal/chapter/revision-of-en-15251-indoor-environmental-criteria — **T1/T3**; `environmental-design.md` EN-19 — in-corpus. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the primary provenance of "1000 ppm" (traced in secondary literature to the ASHRAE 62 legacy 700 ppm-above-outdoor rule and to Ovelberg, 1858); obtain a dated ASHRAE 62 edition text before repeating "ASHRAE sets 1000 ppm".
- Class: FACT (the WHO/ASHRAE scope) / the classification rule is a DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: every air-quality claim; the load-bearing epistemics of this whole file
- Confidence: High (WHO and ASHRAE both decline to set a CO₂ health limit; the surrogate reading is their own)
- Grid translation: Store per room `iaq_basis ∈ {health_guideline, ventilation_surrogate}` and `co2_role = indicator`. Never let a design report a CO₂ target as if it were a pollutant limit; where the brief asks for "CO₂ < 1000 ppm", record it as `surrogate_max_ppm` and require the paired pollutant it is standing in for (`proxy_for ∈ {ventilation_rate, cooking_pm, occupancy_odor}`). Cross-ref the SV-01 rate it is meant to be tracking.
- Exceptions / failure mode: In dense, long-stay, low-outdoor-air spaces (cinemas, open-plan with recirculation) elevated CO₂ *per se* is associated with discomfort and reported "stuffy" perception even below any toxic level; keep it as a comfort indicator there, still not a toxicity limit.

### IE-02 Hold particulate, gas and ozone targets against the WHO 2021 guideline set and publish the averaging time with every value
- Rule: When a design claims "clean air", the reference values are the WHO 2021 air quality guideline (AQG) levels, and each is meaningless without its averaging time (an annual mean and a 24-h 99th-percentile are different animals). Quote the guideline as a matched pair; never present a daily number as if it were a long-term target. These are the outcome thresholds the SV-01 rate, the IE-11 filter class and the IE-12 source controls are all ultimately defending.
- Evidence: WHO 2021 Table 3.24 (read from the primary PDF): **PM₂.₅** annual 5 µg/m³, 24-h 15 µg/m³; **PM₁₀** annual 15, 24-h 45; **O₃** peak-season 6-month 60, daily-max-8-h 100; **NO₂** annual 10, 24-h 25 (1-h 200 from the still-valid 2005 set); **SO₂** 24-h 40 (10-min 500 still valid); **CO** 24-h 4 mg/m³ (2005 short terms 8-h 10, 1-h 35, 15-min 100). WHO states the 2005→2021 tightening explicitly for NO₂ (annual 40→10) and PM₂.₅ (annual 10→5).
- Source: WHO global air quality guidelines, PM₂.₅/PM₁₀/O₃/NO₂/SO₂/CO. Geneva: WHO; 2021 (Table 3.24) — https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/551b515e-2a32-4e1a-a58c-cdaecd395b19/content — **T1**; landing page https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240034228 — T1.
- Class: STANDARD (health-based, non-binding guideline; a jurisdiction may adopt a looser *standard*)
- Scope: ambient values used indoors as the target of entry-control; indoor generation of these pollutants is handled per-pollutant (IE-05, IE-12)
- Confidence: High (numbers read from the primary PDF)
- Grid translation: Not computable on the host. Emit `pm25_target_ugm3 = {annual:5, d24:15}` … as *declared outcome targets* beside the SV-01 rate, and `metric_claim: UNVERIFIED-needs-simulation-or-measurement` (IE-24) on any realised value. The plan-level proxy the engine *can* give is the outdoor-source entry distance and filtration class (IE-11/IE-12), not the µg/m³.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A "guideline" is a health recommendation, not an enforceable limit; the legally binding number is the local ambient standard, which in several jurisdictions sits at WHO *interim target* 1 (PM₂.₅ annual 35), i.e. 7× the guideline. Report which one the client is buying.

### IE-03 Radon is the one pollutant with an outcome threshold *and* a per-unit risk — and it is decided by geology, not by the plan, so it must be priced before the slab is drawn
- Rule: Radon is treated differently from every other IAQ item: it has a WHO reference level *and* a quantified exposure–response, and its mitigation (membrane, sump, sealed penetrations, depressurisation path) is a ground-floor/structure decision that is very hard to retrofit. Where the site is in a radon-prone area, the design action is source-blocked ground-floor construction, not dilution alone. Cross-reference the substructure/waterproofing logic at `construction.md`; the number belongs here.
- Evidence: WHO sets the reference at "national annual average residential radon concentration reference level of 100 Bq/m³" and quantifies risk: "the risk of lung cancer increases by about 16% per 100 Bq/m³ increase in long-term average radon concentration". The US action level WELL cites for buildings is 4 pCi/L ≈ 148 Bq/m³ — looser than WHO's 100 — so a code/WELL-compliant building is not automatically WHO-compliant.
- Source: WHO, *Radon and health* fact sheet, 25 Jan 2023 — https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/radon-and-health — **T1**; WELL v2 Air (radon < 4 pCi/L lowest occupied level) — https://standard.wellcertified.com/v2/air/air-quality-standards — **T1**. Conversion 1 pCi/L = 37 Bq/m³ is arithmetic.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT where a national radon plan adopts it / STANDARD (WHO reference) generally
- Scope: ground-contact floors in granite/shale/uraniferous geology; irrelevant on well-ventilated pile foundations over low-radon rock
- Confidence: High (WHO primary fact sheet); the 16%/100 Bq/m³ is WHO's own pooled figure
- Grid translation: Not computable. Emit `radon_prone_site ∈ {yes, no, unknown}` as a required site input; where `yes`, force a `substructure_mitigation: required` flag and a ground-floor sealed-plane requirement (link `construction.md`), and set `verdict: unknown (needs ground-gas survey)` per IE-24. A plan engine can flag *fraction of floor area with direct ground contact* (tiles on the lowest occupied level over earth) — that share is the exposure surface and is computable.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Ventilation "fixes" radon poorly in a leaky-floor building and the occupant is exposed for thousands of hours/year at night; dilution is the wrong primary control. Symptom: a high-radon home with a mechanical ventilation system still over the reference level because the source was never blocked.

### IE-04 Formaldehyde has a short-term sensory-irritation guideline and is controlled at the material, not the diffuser
- Rule: Formaldehyde is emitted by the building's own materials (board, adhesives, textiles), so it is a *source* problem; more air helps only by dilution and does not remove the emitter. The binding number is a short-term, any-interval cap — design for material selection and low-emitting assemblies (link `construction.md` finishes), not for an oversized duct.
- Evidence: WHO 2010 *Indoor air quality: selected pollutants* sets "a short-term (30-minute) guideline of 0.1 mg/m³ … as preventing sensory irritation", to be "not exceeded at any 30-minute interval during a day"; it deliberately did *not* select carcinogenicity as the endpoint for the number. 0.1 mg/m³ = 100 µg/m³. WELL v2's kitchen formaldehyde limit of 81 ppb ≈ 100 µg/m³ (1 ppb HCHO ≈ 1.23 µg/m³) independently lands on the same sensory-irritation basis.
- Source: WHO, *Guidelines for indoor air quality: selected pollutants* (WHO Regional Office for Europe, 2010), formaldehyde chapter — https://sknbs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/WHO-Document.pdf — **T1** (mirror of ISBN 9789289002134); WELL v2 Air quality standards — https://standard.wellcertified.com/v2/air/air-quality-standards — **T1**.
- Class: STANDARD
- Scope: all inhabited types; binds hardest in new/re-fitted, low-air-change, tightly sealed (retrofit/EPC-upgrade) builds
- Confidence: High for the 100 µg/m³ / 30-min value; Medium for cross-jurisdiction adoption
- Grid translation: Not computable. Emit `hcho_limit = 100 µg/m³ / any-30-min` as an outcome target and `control = source_selection` (materials), with `metric_claim: UNVERIFIED-needs-measurement`. The engine's only lever is a materials-spec flag, not geometry.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Cured formaldehyde off-gasses on a long, decaying tail; a "low-emitting" claim made at handover can drift — this is why the guideline is an any-interval cap and a post-occupancy measurement, not a design-time calculation.

### IE-05 Carbon monoxide: keep the ambient 24-h guideline and the older indoor guideline straight, and put the combustion source outside the air path
- Rule: CO has a WHO 2021 ambient AQG (24-h 4 mg/m³) *and* an older WHO indoor guideline (7 mg/m³) referenced inside the 2021 document; quote the one whose exposure route you are defending. Indoor CO is a point-source problem (unflued appliances, garage/car-park spillage into the dwelling, kitchen) — the control is source separation and the SV-05/SV-27 car-park/wet-cell paths, not general dilution.
- Evidence: WHO 2021 Table 3.24 gives CO 24-h AQG 4 mg/m³ (interim target 1 = 7). The same text cross-references "the air quality guideline of 7 mg/m³ for carbon monoxide indoors (WHO, 2014b)". WHO sets CO on a myocardial-infarction hospital-admission basis. The corpus already sizes the car-park extract at SV-05; the *outcome* being prevented is the CO dose to the floors above and adjacent.
- Source: WHO 2021 AQG (Table 3.24) — https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/551b515e-2a32-4e1a-a58c-cdaecd395b19/content — **T1**; in-corpus SV-05, SV-27.
- Class: STANDARD
- Scope: buildings with combustion appliances, attached garages, or car-park-to-living spill paths
- Confidence: High for the values; High for the source-control framing
- Grid translation: Not computable. Emit `co_source_present ∈ {appliance, garage, none}`; where a car park shares the structure with habitation, cross-reference the SV-05 extract as the mitigation and set `verdict` on the *separation path* (computable as tiles of buffer + floor above the spill zone), not on a ppm.
- Exceptions / failure mode: An attached garage ventilated as "office" area — the CO dose is a spill-path geometry failure, so it is partly computable even though the ppm is not.

### IE-06 A scheme-labelled target (WELL v2) is a policy line, and where it trails WHO it must be reported as a gap, not a pass
- Rule: WELL v2 and LEED state IAQ as *performance targets verified by testing*, usually anchored to the US EPA NAAQS rather than to WHO 2021. Where the two differ, publish the difference — do not let a WELL badge read as "healthy by WHO". This is the outcome-layer discipline EQ-13 already applies to daylight ("label every one a policy target"), extended to air.
- Evidence: WELL v2 Air quality-standards limits read this pass: PM₂.₅ < 15 µg/m³ (kitchen 35), PM₁₀ < 50, O₃ < 51 ppb, NO₂ < 53 ppb (kitchen 100), CO < 9 ppm (kitchen 35), formaldehyde < 81 ppb (kitchen), radon < 4 pCi/L. Against WHO 2021: WELL's PM₂.₅ 15 equals WHO's **24-h** value but is **3× WHO's annual 5 µg/m³**; WELL's NO₂ 53 ppb ≈ 100 µg/m³ is far above WHO's annual 10 µg/m³; WELL's radon 4 pCi/L ≈ 148 Bq/m³ is looser than WHO's 100. So a WELL-compliant building is *not*, on these three, WHO-compliant.
- Source: WELL Building Standard v2, Air — *Air quality standards* feature — https://standard.wellcertified.com/v2/air/air-quality-standards — **T1**; WHO 2021 AQG (Table 3.24) — https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/551b515e-2a32-4e1a-a58c-cdaecd395b19/content — **T1**.
- Class: STANDARD (voluntary certification = policy target)
- Scope: any project specifying a wellness label
- Confidence: High (both value sets read from primary pages); note WELL's page did not print the averaging time in the retrieved text — `NEEDS VERIFICATION:` the averaging windows from the full WELL v2 reference
- Grid translation: Not computable. In the per-space register (below), carry two columns — `label_target` (WELL/LEED) and `health_guideline` (WHO 2021) — and flag any room where the label passes but the guideline is exceeded. The verdict is on the pair, not either alone.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Certification limits are set where the *measured distribution* of real buildings lands, so they are achievable; treating a looser label as the health goal systematically under-protects. Report, don't average (EN-26's discipline).

### IE-07 Thermal comfort is a band with an accepted-deviation percentage, and the static model's acceptability floor is 80% of occupants
- Rule: Thermal comfort is never "a temperature"; it is a band defined by a model and an acceptability target. In the static (heat-balance) model, ASHRAE 55 sets the acceptable operative envelope by PMV in the range −0.5 < PMV < +0.5 and requires that **at least 80% of occupants be satisfied** (i.e. PPD ≤ 20%) for the space to be called acceptable. PMV is driven by six variables — air temperature, mean radiant temperature, humidity, air speed, metabolic rate (met), clothing insulation (clo) — so the same dry-bulb can be comfortable at 1.0 met/0.5 clo (office, summer) and cold at 0.8 met/1.0 clo (sedentary, winter dress). The design number is the *pair* (band + % dissatisfied), never the setpoint alone.
- Evidence: ASHRAE 55-2017 acceptability: the comfort zone exists where PMV is within −0.5 < PMV < +0.5, and the standard mandates "at least 80% of the occupants be satisfied"; PMV is a seven-point scale −3…+3; SET (standard effective temperature) is the "equivalent dry-bulb temperature of an isothermal environment at 50% RH giving the same physiological strain"; the six PMV inputs are air temp, radiant temp, RH, air speed, met and clo (aligned to ASHRAE 55 / ISO 7730). **The design-practice band PPD ≤ 10% (the stricter ~±0.5 target quoted in specifications) is carried from Fanger's PPD equation and was not re-read from a primary page this pass — treat 80% (≤20% PPD) as the verified floor.**
- Source: *Thermal comfort* (Wikipedia) summarising ANSI/ASHRAE 55-2017 and ISO 7730 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_comfort — **T4** (numbers attributed to the named editions, not to the standard text read directly); CBE Thermal Comfort Tool (calculates PMV/PPD/SET to ASHRAE 55-2017) — https://cbe.berkeley.edu/research/cbe-thermal-comfort-tool/ — **T2**. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the operative-temperature design ranges per season/clo/met *as printed in the ASHRAE 55 chart* — buy the standard's Section 5 / Figure.
- Class: STANDARD
- Scope: mechanically conditioned / fully controlled spaces (the static model); occupant-controlled free-running spaces use the adaptive band (EN-23)
- Confidence: High for the band/acceptability/SET/six-variable structure; Medium for the exact numeric operative ranges (not read from the standard)
- Grid translation: Cannot compute °C or %PPD. Emit `thermal_model ∈ {static_ashrae55, adaptive}` and the declared `met`, `clo` and `air_speed` assumptions per room (they change the band), plus the season. Then hand off to the exposure-geometry levers (IE-09). Set `verdict: unknown (needs comfort model)` for any PMV/PPD claim.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Specifying one temperature for a mixed-dress, mixed-activity building guarantees a dissatisfied tail; elevated air speed widens the acceptable band (a free-running lever EN-23 relies on) but a fixed setpoint design ignores it.

### IE-08 For occupant-controlled spaces the band is adaptive — cite EN-23, and treat its applicability condition as a hard plan constraint, not a suggestion
- Rule: Where a space is free-running or mixed-mode, comfort is judged by the adaptive band (ASHRAE 55 / EN 16798), whose operative ranges and 10–33.5 °C / 10–30 °C applicability and ±3.5/±2.5 °C acceptability the corpus already carries at **EN-23**. This file adds one outcome rule: the adaptive band is *void the moment the occupant cannot act on it*. Locked façades, security-limited windows, high-occupancy conference rooms with no operable opening, and hotel guest rooms on a controlled BMS all fail the applicability test, and a comfort claim built on them is invalid.
- Evidence: EN-23 records the adaptive model (`Topt = 0.31·T_outdoor + 17.8`, 80% ±3.5 °C / 90% ±2.5 °C, ~10–33.5 °C) and already states "adaptive claims with `occupant_control = false` … are reported as invalid, since the model's applicability condition fails." The comfort *outcome* literature (see IE-20 and the SBS record) is consistent that perceived *control* is itself a driver of satisfaction independent of the temperature.
- Source: `environmental-design.md` EN-23 — in-corpus (its own ASHRAE 55 / EN 16798 citations stand).
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE (the applicability test) / STANDARD (the band it points to)
- Scope: free-running and mixed-mode types; not for operating theatres, labs, archives, data halls
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Reuse EN-23's `occupant_control` and `free_running_area_tiles` outputs directly — this file does not recompute them. Add `adaptive_valid = (occupant_control ∧ strategy ≠ fully_conditioned)`. Where `adaptive_valid = false`, force a conditioned-backup record (EN-21, SV).
- Exceptions / failure mode: A designer keeps an adaptive comfort claim after specifying a fixed façade to win a cleaner elevation — the geometry and the comfort basis then contradict each other; the plan must reopen an operable fraction or drop the claim.

### IE-09 A plan controls the thermal outcome through four geometry levers — zone depth, perimeter-vs-interior, winter-sun exposure, and the gain split — and the loads themselves are SV's
- Rule: The architect cannot set the °C but chooses the conditions that make the °C expensive or cheap to hit. Four levers, each a tile decision: (1) **perimeter vs interior depth** — perimeter zones swing with outdoor temperature and solar, interior zones are gain-dominated; (2) **perimeter length / area ratio** — already EN-14's heat-loss/gain proxy; (3) **exposure to low winter sun vs high summer sun** — from the EQ-01/EQ-09 geometry (this file does not recompute it); (4) **the split between internal gains (people, lights, equipment — SV-07/SV-17) and fabric gains** — which decides whether the space needs heating, cooling or neither. Zone every room as `perimeter | transitional | interior` by distance-to-façade and price the thermal consequence per band.
- Evidence: The banding is already operationalised at EN-25 ("façade band and interior band are different HVAC zones with different equipment") and the load densities that feed the gains at SV-07/SV-17; EQ-12/EQ-13 give the daylight depth against which the perimeter/interior split is read; EN-14 gives the perimeter-to-area proxy. This file contributes only the framing that these four are the *outcome-relevant* thermal levers and the zone-by-exposure partition is how a plan engine reports them.
- Source: `environmental-design.md` EN-14, EN-25; `mep-sizing.md` SV-07, SV-17; `envelope-daylight-quantification.md` EQ-09, EQ-12 — all in-corpus.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: any multi-zone plan; the partition matters most in deep plates and cellular towers
- Confidence: High for the partition method; the numeric load consequences sit with SV-*
- Grid translation: `dist_to_fasade(room)` = min tiles to a glazed/opaque façade line. Zone: `perimeter` ≤ ~4 tiles (2 m) of the band EN-20/EN-25 already defines, `transitional` to the daylight-reach depth (EN-04/EQ-12), `interior` beyond. Report `area_by_zone`, `volume_per_person_by_zone = (zone_tiles×0.25×2.7)/occ`, and `gain_type` (perimeter = outdoor/solar-coupled; interior = internal-gain-dominated → SV-17). Hand the SW-Δ to SV-11's per-floor machine check.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Treating a deep plan as one zone — the core overheats from internal gains while the perimeter cools from the glass; the symptom is a single-thermostat complaint in an otherwise code-compliant plate.

### IE-10 Effectiveness, not air changes, decides whether the SV-01 air reaches the breathing zone — the adopted `Ez` ladder is 1.2 → 0.5
- Rule: SV-01 divides the ventilation demand by the zone air-distribution effectiveness `Ez`; that denominator *is* the outcome question — the fraction of supplied outdoor air that actually treats the breathing zone. Displacement and cool ceiling/floor supply reach `Ez` ≥ 1.0–1.2 (unidirectional, stratified flow), ordinary mixing 0.8–1.0, and makeup air drawn near the exhaust falls to 0.5, meaning you must supply double the air for the same breathing-zone quality. A room can meet its SV-01 rate in L/s and still fail the occupant if the air short-circuits from supply to return above the breathing zone.
- Evidence: Adopted ASHRAE-62.1 Table (NYC Mechanical Code 2022 §403.3.1.1.1.2, read this pass): ceiling/floor supply of cool air **Ez = 1.0**; ceiling/floor supply of warm air + floor return **1.0**; ceiling supply of warm air + ceiling return **0.8**; floor supply of warm air + ceiling return **0.7**; makeup air drawn opposite the exhaust **0.8**; makeup air drawn near the exhaust/return **0.5**. Elevated **Ez = 1.2** requires that "low-velocity displacement ventilation achieves unidirectional flow and thermal stratification" (with a ≤15 °F supply-to-space temperature delta and the jet reaching within ~4½ ft, i.e. 1.4 m).
- Source: UpCodes, NYC Mechanical Code 2022 §403.3.1.1.1.2 *Zone Air Distribution Effectiveness* — https://up.codes/s/zone-air-distribution-effectiveness — **T1** (adopted code; the ASHRAE 62.1 edition it mirrors was not separately confirmed). In-corpus: SV-01 (`Ez` in the Vbz formula), EN-21.
- Class: STANDARD (adopted code)
- Scope: mechanically ventilated spaces; the lever that separates an all-air design from a mixing design
- Confidence: High (values are code text as retrieved); Medium for the displacement-stratification conditions (single-code restatement)
- Grid translation: `Ez` is a *design-intent* attribute per room, set as `air_scheme ∈ {displacement, mixing_ceiling, mixing_floor_warm, makeup_near_exhaust}` with the retrieved numeric `Ez` attached; report `required_Vbz_at_Ez = SV01_rate / Ez`. The engine cannot compute realised ADPI (needs a tracer/CFD), so any "effective ventilation" claim is `verdict: unknown (needs CFD or tracer-gas)`. What the plan *can* show: supply/return adjacency (short-circuit risk if a supply tile abuts a return tile on the same ceiling band).
- Exceptions / failure mode: A displacement claim without the low-supply-temperature and stratification conditions just takes the 1.2 credit — the honest move is to withhold the `Ez` uplift unless the section conditions (absent on the host) are declared, per IE-24.

### IE-11 Filtration is classed toward a particle size, and MERV and ISO ePM1 are two rulers for that class — quote both or the number is meaningless
- Rule: A filter class is a *fractional removal efficiency toward a defined particle-size bin*, and the two families in use disagree. ASHRAE 52.2 (MERV) reports efficiency across the 0.3–1.0, 1.0–3.0 and 3.0–10 µm bins; ISO 16890 (ePM1) reports the constant efficiency toward the PM₁ fraction. Because most indoor particles by number are sub-micron, a MERV that sounds strong can be weak at PM₁ — so state the class in the family you tested and the size it refers to. Design the filtration grade as an outcome control (entry + recirculation), and read the air rate that carries it from SV-01/SV-02 (not restated here).
- Evidence: Cross-map read from a vendor comparison (ISO 16890 vs ASHRAE 52.2): **MERV 8 → ISOcoarse 90%**, **MERV 9 → ePM10 60%**, **MERV 11 → ePM2.5 50%**, **MERV 13 → ePM1 50%**, **MERV 14 → ePM1 75%**, **MERV 16 → ePM1 95%**. Same source notes MERV-11 "removes about 20%" of the fine (0.3–1 µm) fraction, and that "a comparison of the filter classes is only possible approximately" because the test aerosols differ. ASHRAE 52.2-2017 defines MERV on the 0.30–10 µm test aerosol.
- Source: *ANSI/ASHRAE Standard 52.2-2017* (public COVID-19 release) — https://www.ashrae.org/File%20Library/Technical%20Resources/COVID-19/52_2_2017_COVID-19_20200401.pdf — **T1** (test method + size bins; Table 7 percentages not machine-readable in the text extracted); Air Filter Supply, *ISO 16890 vs MERV* — https://www.airfiltersupply.com/pdf/ISO%2016890%20vs%20MERV.pdf — **T4** (cross-map); EMW Filtertechnik, *Comparison of filter classes* — https://www.emw.de/en/filter-campus/comparison-of-filter-classes.html — **T3/T4** (approximateness caveat). **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the official ISO 16890 ePM1 minimum-efficiency class boundaries (50/65/80/90%) from the standard text.
- Class: STANDARD (test families) / the cross-map is HEURISTIC (T4)
- Scope: mechanically filtered buildings; PM outcome control (IE-11/IE-12)
- Confidence: Medium (the specific % cross-map is vendor-derived; the family definitions are T1)
- Grid translation: `filter_class` is a specification attribute, not geometry. Emit the declared `ePM1_min%`/`MERV` at the AHU/terminal, cross-ref the SV-13 duct pressure-drop consequence (higher class → more static → bigger fan → SV-15 heat gain). The engine cannot compute indoor PM from a filter class (needs outdoor level + infiltration + generation), so `pm_outcome: UNVERIFIED-needs-measurement`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Sizing a system for MERV 13 then running it at a face velocity that voids the rated efficiency, or not budgeting the pressure drop — the filter's *class* is a lab number; delivered efficiency is a system-respect-the-spec question.

### IE-12 Indoor-generated particles need source separation and dilution, not just filters — and the peaks are orders of magnitude over the guideline
- Rule: The four levers for particulate outcome, in order of leverage, are **entry control, filtration, dilution (ventilation), and source separation** — and for indoor-generated particles only the last two help, because the source is inside. The magnitudes justify treating cooking as a major IAQ event: high-temperature cooking emits ultrafine particles at peak number concentrations orders of magnitude above clean indoor background, and ventilation cuts the peak roughly tenfold. So the *plan* lever — put the source where its plume can be captured or isolated from sleeping/long-stay rooms — is an outcome rule, not a hygiene rule.
- Evidence: A chamber study of domestic cooking (air fryers) reports peak ultrafine-particle number concentrations of order 10¹²–2.5×10¹³ #/m³ depending on food (high-fat/fried worst), emission rates 0.1–17.4×10¹² #/min, and that with the room's normal air change the peak "decrease[s] to 0.01–2.49×10¹² #/m³, … more than 10 times lower"; residual build-up raised UFP ~236% even with no food. (Air-fryer-specific; treat magnitudes as one cooking-method data point, not a universal.)
- Source: ACS ES&T Air 2026, 3, 473–487, *Quantification of VOCs, NOₓ and ultrafine particles emitted by domestic air fryers: a chamber study* — https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/128404/1/quantification-of-volatile-organic-compounds-(vocs)-nitrogen-oxides-(nox)-and-ultrafine-particles-(ufps)-emitted-by.pdf — **T2**. In-corpus: SV-06 (kitchen as airflow device), SV-02 (extract rates), EN-22 (path separation), HB-16.
- Class: EXPERIENCE-BASED ADVICE → FACT for the dilution order-of-magnitude; the "levers ranked" is a DESIGN PRINCIPLE
- Scope: kitchens (hotel F&B and residential), any indoor combustion/frying; high-occupancy dust
- Confidence: Medium for the absolute peaks (single niche appliance study), High for the direction (source inside → dilute/separate)
- Grid translation: The plan *can* act: emit `kitchen_adjacent_to_sleep_or_longstay` (a tile-adjacency test — cross-ref EN-22 path separation, HB-16), require `extract_present` at the source (SV-06/SV-02, not restated), and give each kitchen an `air_change_dilution_flag`. `pm_outcome: UNVERIFIED-needs-measurement` for any µg/m³; the geometry verdict is on capture-and-separation, which is computable.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A closed "efficient" kitchen with the extract *recirculating* into the apartment — filtration without dilution, source not separated; symptom: corridor/bedroom PM spikes at mealtimes diagnosed as "ventilation not running".

### IE-13 Light for people is melanopic EDI, not task lux — and the current numbers are an 18-expert consensus recommendation, not yet a code limit
- Rule: The circadian outcome of light is measured as **melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melanopic EDI)** at the *eye*, which is not the task-plane lux the daylight and lighting standards already own (EQ-13, EN-18 — not restated). A room can be visually bright (300+ lx on the desk) and circadianly poor if the light is warm, low and never reaches the eye. The daytime/evening/nighttime thresholds below are the published consensus recommendation; label them as a *target* whose standardisation is still maturing, and where you must cite a code, cross-reference the light-transmitted IP that adopts melanopic EDI.
- Evidence: Brown et al. 2022 (*PLoS Biology*, 18-expert workshop consensus) recommend: daytime **minimum melanopic EDI 250 lux at the eye**; evening, **from at least 3 h before bedtime, maximum 10 lux at the eye**; night, **maximum ambient melanopic EDI 1 lux at the eye** (up to 10 lux only where a nighttime visual task requires it). They coin/popularise the term "melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (melanopic EDI)".
- Source: Brown, Buckland, Hunter, Rojas Benavides, Shea, *Recommendations for daytime, evening, and nighttime indoor light exposure to limit impacts on health and wellbeing* — PLoS Biol 20(3):e3001571, 2022 — https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3001571 — **T2** (peer-reviewed consensus). **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the IES/CSA light-transmitted IP (adopting melanopic EDI as a specification metric) and its exact clause thresholds — not opened this pass; obtain before presenting 250 lux as a *standard* rather than a *recommendation*.
- Class: STANDARD (voluntary/consensus) — currently a recommendation, not a regulation
- Scope: long-occupancy day spaces (offices, wards, classrooms, homes), shift/24-h facilities
- Confidence: High that these are the published consensus values; Medium for their status as code
- Grid translation: Cannot compute melanopic EDI (needs spectral + eye-level position). The plan proxy it can offer is **eye-level exposure geometry**: distance-to-glazed-façade at seated eye height and the fraction of dayspaces within the daylight reach (EQ-11/EQ-12), reported as `melanopic_exposure_opportunity = high|med|low` by that distance, never as a lux value. `verdict: unknown (needs spectral/daylight simulation)`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Over-lit night wards and hotels: the evening/night "≤10 / ≤1 lux" ceiling is the harder and more health-relevant constraint and is the one lighting design forgets — the same fixture that gives safe egress can suppress melatonin.

### IE-14 The distribution of light in a room is an EQ concern, not a threshold — point at the metric, do not restate it
- Rule: Evenness of light across the room (spacing of openings, deep-plan falloff) is owned by EQ-15 (space openings for uniform light), EQ-11/EQ-12 (reach multipliers, plate depth) and EQ-13 (sDA/ASE/UDI) and EN-04; this file does not restate those numbers. Its only addition is the outcome reading: an uneven light field is what produces both the unlit deep interior (a circadian and satisfaction problem, IE-13) and the bright-at-the-window / dark-at-desk complaint, so the plan's job is to hold the *gradient* within the reach the EQ geometry already bounds.
- Evidence: EQ-15 states openings must be spaced "for uniform light, not only for enough light"; EQ-12 derives maximum plan depth from daylight arithmetic; EN-04 fixes one reach multiplier per room type. The distribution logic is theirs; the discomfort/satisfaction consequence is the outcome layer added here.
- Source: `envelope-daylight-quantification.md` EQ-11, EQ-12, EQ-13, EQ-15; `environmental-design.md` EN-04 — in-corpus.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE (outcome reading) 
- Scope: all daylit rooms
- Confidence: High (pointer rule; the numbers live at EQ-*)
- Grid translation: Use EQ-12/EN-04's computed `daylit_share` and per-band reach; report the `daylit_gradient` (glazed-band lux class vs interior-band) as the outcome proxy. Do not import a lux threshold — cite the EQ-13 metric ID.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Meeting a whole-room average sDA by flooding the perimeter and leaving the core in shadow — average passes, distribution fails; this is exactly why EQ-15 exists and why the outcome file reads the gradient, not the mean.

### IE-15 Glare is a comfort outcome with a class limit already in EN-18 — carry the class and the daylight-glare consequence, do not re-derive it
- Rule: Disabling and discomfort glare are bounded by UGR for artificial light (already tabulated per room at EN-18: offices/classrooms UGR ≤ 19, hotel bedrooms/retail ≤ 22) and by daylight-glare metrics for sunlit rooms. This file does not restate those UGR figures; it adds the outcome rule that the *same* aperture that earns the daylight/thermal credit (EN-12's >~600 lx vertical illuminance complaint threshold, EQ's sDA) is the one that earns a glare failure, so glare must be scored per band as the counterweight to the daylight gain, not after the façade is frozen.
- Evidence: EN-18 carries UGR ≤ 19 / ≤ 22 by room; EN-12 records the measured office finding that "full-height windows occupy 20–40% of the facade provide the most daylight" with "vertical illuminance above ~600 lux driving complaints" and the daylight-benefit saturation. The glare *outcome* is discomfort and visual-task degradation; the metric is the class, which the corpus already fixes.
- Source: `environmental-design.md` EN-12, EN-18 — in-corpus. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the UGR/DGP numeric bands against EN 12464-1 / CIE glare definitions from the standard text (EN-18's figures came from a vendor reproduction).
- Class: STANDARD (limits) / DESIGN PRINCIPLE (the gain-vs-glare trade)
- Scope: daylit and top-lit rooms; worst on low-sun east/west bands (EN-13)
- Confidence: Medium for the exact UGR class values (secondary), High for the trade-off rule
- Grid translation: Cannot compute UGR/DGP. Emit `glare_band` = E/W glazed tiles on unshaded façades (from EN-13) and the shading coverage `shaded_share` (EN-11); report a `glare_risk = f(EW_unshaded_glazed_tiles, daylit_share)` so the daylight gain and its glare cost sit on one table. `verdict: unknown (needs glare simulation)` for the number.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A glare fix (opaque blind) that also kills the daylight/melanopic exposure (IE-13/IE-14) — the three outcomes trade against each other and must be reported together, never solved one at a time.

### IE-16 Reverberation time is the acoustic outcome a plan can act on, and it is set by volume per occupant and absorption area — the Sabine relation, not the dB attenuation
- Rule: Speech intelligibility and "lively vs dead" are governed by reverberation time `RT`, and `RT` is a geometry/material quantity: `RT ≈ 0.161·V/A` (V room volume, A total absorption area in sabins). So the plan controls RT through **volume per occupant** and **finish/absorption area**, before anyone measures it. The functional criteria: classroom/speech-critical spaces want short RT (≈0.4–0.8 s across the standards read) and a speech-transmission target, while sports/assembly tolerate more. This is the *outcome criterion*; the transmission-loss and buffer-in-tiles arithmetic stays at EQ-26/EQ-27 and the party-wall assembly at CN-26 — not restated here.
- Evidence: School-acoustics criteria read from a standards-comparison page: **BB93:2015** RT60 ≤ 0.6 s (primary), ≤ 0.8 s (secondary), 0.8–1.2 s (assembly), ≤ 1.5 s (sports); **DIN 18041:2016** 0.4–0.6 s (Group A classrooms), and mandates **STI ≥ 0.60** for Group A speech-critical rooms; **ANSI S12.60-2010** ≤ 0.6 s (≤ 283 m³), ≤ 0.7 s larger. Background-noise ceiling ≤ 35 dBA for classrooms (BB93 / ANSI S12.60). The Sabine form `RT60 = 0.161·V/A` is the universal relation. **Note (honest correction):** ISO 3382-3 is the *open-plan-office* measurement part, not the classroom RT/STI standard the brief implied — the classroom numbers above come from BB93/DIN 18041/ANSI S12.60.
- Source: AcousPlan, *Acoustic Design for Schools: BB93, DIN 18041, and ANSI S12.60* — https://acousplan.com/blog/acoustic-design-schools-complete-guide — **T4** (reproduces the standards; clause numbers named not read from the standards); ISO 3382-3 scope — https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/#iso:std:iso:3382:-3:ed-1:v1:en — **T1** (title/scope only). **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** BB93 and DIN 18041 numeric limits and clause IDs from the standards themselves; ANSI S12.60 STI value.
- Class: STANDARD (criteria) / FACT (Sabine relation)
- Scope: speech-critical rooms (classrooms, lecture halls, open-plan offices, restaurants), gyms, pools
- Confidence: Medium for the exact limits (T4 reproduction), High for the volume/absorption control logic
- Grid translation: `RT` is *directionally computable*: `V(room) = tiles×0.25×2.7 m³`, and a declared `absorption_area = Σ (finish_surface_tiles × alpha_finish)` using a published α table → `RT_est = 0.161·V/A`, emitted as a *preliminary estimate with the α basis declared*, `verdict: preliminary (needs reverberant measurement)`. Report `volume_per_occupant = V/occ` (a plan number) per speech room; low volume-per-occupant + hard finishes is the failure signature.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A tall, hard-surfaced atrium restaurant that "looks right" and is unintelligible — RT grows with volume and shrinks with absorption; the plan can pre-empt it with `absorption_area ≥ target` (ceiling/wall baffles priced as finish tiles), which is where the volume-per-occupant number earns its keep.

### IE-17 Background noise and the acoustic separation outcome are already tabulated — cite the bands (EN-18/EN-10) and price the weak links (CN-26/HB-24), don't restate them
- Rule: The room-function NC / background-noise bands sit at **EN-18** (private offices ~NC 30–35, open plan ~35–40, hospital patients ~25–35) and the WHO community-noise limits at **EN-10**; the transmission-loss-from-mass arithmetic and buffer-in-tiles at **EQ-26/EQ-27**; the party-wall assembly at **CN-26** and the door weak-link rule at **HB-24**. - Evidence: HB-24 "never let a door run be the weakest link" is the corpus statement of exactly this outcome; EN-10/EN-18 hold the target bands; EQ-26's mass-law and EQ-27's dB-in-tiles pricing hold the attenuation arithmetic. Nothing here re-derives them; the added rule is that the *rating you quote must be the weakest-component rating*, which the target-table convention can silently obscure.
- Source: `environmental-design.md` EN-10, EN-18; `envelope-daylight-quantification.md` EQ-26, EQ-27; `construction.md` CN-26; `human-behavior.md` HB-24 — all in-corpus.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE (weakest-link outcome) / STANDARD (the bands it points to)
- Scope: hotels, residential, hospitals, schools, offices with speech privacy
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Emit per adjacency `path_weakest = min(Rw_wall, Rw_door, Rw_glazing, buffer_attenuation)` where the dB component values are declared inputs (link EQ-26/CN-26/HB-24), and require `path_weakest ≥ target(NC/privacy band from EN-18)`. The engine can compute *which* component is the min and *how many* door/glazing tiles sit on the path; the dB values remain `[in]`.

### IE-18 Ventilation → performance: the effect is real, small, and heterogeneous — quote the meta-analysis, not the anecdote
- Rule: When selling "ventilation improves productivity", the honest number is a small pooled effect with large between-study spread, from a heavily caveated meta-analysis — not a single-office doubling. The effect supports the SV-01 investment directionally; it does not support a specific ROI percentage per tenant. Report it as evidence of *benefit*, with the confidence and the caveats intact.
- Evidence: Kuramochi et al. 2023 pooled **5 studies / 3,679 participants** on ventilation and intellectual productivity: overall **SMD 0.20**; translated, **~13.7% faster** arithmetic, **~16.1% fewer** arithmetic errors, **~3.5%** improved cognitive pacing; CO₂ fell from 952–4140 ppm to 501–983 ppm and airflow rose from 1.4–5.7 to 6.5–9.9 L/s·person. Heterogeneity **I² = 84%** (speed) / 55% (errors); funnel plots showed **no evidence of publication bias**; authors caution against extrapolation (region/language filtering, narrow demographic, no pre-registration, likely attenuation above ~10.7 L/s·person).
- Source: Kuramochi, Takiguchi, Takeda, *Meta-Analysis of the Effect of Ventilation on Intellectual Productivity* — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10139101/ — **T2**.
- Class: FACT (as reported)
- Scope: office-type cognitive work; the mechanism (lower CO₂ / higher OA) generalises but the magnitude does not
- Confidence: Medium — small pooled set, very high heterogeneity (I² 84%), simple tasks
- Grid translation: Not a plan quantity. Cite it once, in the *justification* column of the priority table (IE-22), as "the ventilation-rate investment is evidence-supported but the effect is small and highly variable" — never as a headline percentage per tile.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Quoting SMD 0.20 as "20% productivity" — it is a standardised effect size on a task, not a percentage, and I²=84% means some studies showed no effect; the misreport is worse than silence.

### IE-19 Daylight → learning and view → recovery: promising, correlational, and contested — report the design and the doubt together
- Rule: The two most-quoted "design pays" claims — daylighting improves student progress, and a view speeds patient recovery — come from observational studies with confounders the authors could not fully remove, and both remain contested. Present them as *suggestions strong enough to shape the plan, weak enough to never guarantee an outcome*, and always name the design and its criticism.
- Evidence: Heschong Mahone Group (Daylighting in Schools 1999; follow-up *Daylighting Impacts on Human Performance in School* 2002) reported students in daylit classrooms progressing **15–23% faster** over a school year on standardised tests, with the models explaining **~25% of the variance** in progress and daylight variables significant at >90–99%; the studies are *correlational multiple-regression on three districts* and the authors control for covariates but cannot establish causation, which is the standing criticism (teacher/facility/curriculum confounds; selection). The corpus already flags this methodology debate (failure-patterns). **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the oft-cited "20% math / 26% reading" figures from the 1999 report specifically — only the 15–23% follow-up band was read from the PDF this pass.
- Source: HMG, *Daylighting Impacts on Human Performance in School* (2002) — https://www.livingdaylights.nl/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/HMG-2002.-Daylighting-Impacts-on-Human-Performance-in-School.-1.pdf — **T3**; `failure-patterns.md` (in-corpus, names the debate).
- Class: HEURISTIC (the direction) / FACT (as reported, with its correlational label)
- Scope: schools, healthcare; used rhetorically across all types
- Confidence: Low for the causal claim, Medium that a daylight–performance association exists
- Grid translation: Not a plan quantity. Feed IE-13/IE-14 (daylight exposure opportunity) as the *designable proxy*; cite this literature as the reason to protect daylit depth (EN-03/EQ-12) without promising a test-score number.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Certifying a building on a claimed "learning boost %" from a specific daylight design — the evidence does not carry a deterministic coefficient; the honest claim is "daylit to the reach, which the association literature suggests helps".

### IE-20 Sick Building Syndrome is multi-factorial and the ventilation link is moderate — treat it as a composite outcome, not a single pollutant
- Rule: SBS (the cluster of headache/eye-throat/respiratory irritation and non-specific symptoms that remit on leaving the building) is the outcome the whole IEQ stack serves, and its causes are jointly ventilation, thermal comfort, humidity, pollutant load *and* psychosocial/organisational factors. It is therefore the one thing you cannot pin on a single number — which is exactly why the proxy-vs-limit distinction (IE-01) matters and why a design should not claim SBS is "solved" by one pollutant passing.
- Evidence: WHO defined SBS (WHO Regional Office for Europe, 1984 — carried in-corpus at `failure-patterns.md`); the reviews that connect it to ventilation (the Fisk lineage, and the symptom-side of IE-18's Kuramochi set) find an association that is real but modest and heterogeneous, and the psychosocial contribution is consistently large. This file supplies the *outcome-layer reading* — SBS as a composite — rather than a new number.
- Source: `failure-patterns.md` (WHO 1984 SBS citation, in-corpus); IE-18 (Kuramochi) — in-corpus.
- Class: STANDARD (definition) / DESIGN PRINCIPLE (the composite-outcome reading)
- Scope: offices, schools, healthcare — high-occupancy conditioned buildings
- Confidence: Medium (association well established; attribution across causes is diffuse)
- Grid translation: Not computable. Emit `sbs_risk_factors_present = {ventilation_low, thermal_uncontrolled, pollutant_source_inside, humidity_out_of_band, control_absent}` as a checklist and report a *risk profile*, never a probability. The absence of control (IE-08) is on the list because it is an outcome driver, not only an applicability condition.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Blaming a mechanical system for an SBS complaint that is psychosocial or work-organisation; the honest plan-level statement is "the physical risk factors are controlled", which is weaker and truer.

### IE-21 Under a constrained budget, fund the irreversible and the source-controlled first — rank IEQ spend by evidence and by how hard it is to retrofit
- Rule: When budget forces IEQ cuts, rank investments on two axes — strength of outcome evidence and irreversibility — and spend accordingly. Radon and substructure source control, kitchen source separation, ventilation effectiveness (choosing displacement / fixing short-circuit routing) and classroom reverberation absorption are cheap-to-build-now / expensive-or-impossible-later and evidence-backed. Deep-shell upgrades and speculative high-grade filtration are pay-later. The commonly-cut items that cost the most later are: perimeter heating/zoning for winter comfort (IE-09), acoustic absorption finishes (IE-16), and operable-façade cost (IE-08) — each cut as "value engineering" and each converting into a comfort or intelligibility complaint.
- Evidence: The ranking is a synthesis of the effect and threshold evidence in this file, not a single citation: radon carries a hard per-unit risk (IE-03) and is structural; ventilation has a real-if-small cognitive benefit (IE-18) and effectiveness is nearly free at design time (IE-10); classroom RT/STI (IE-16) is geometry-fixed-at-build; daylighting/thermal comfort claims are correlational (IE-19), so they earn plan-protection but not a premium spec. The "commonly cut at cost" list follows from what the corpus already warns is hard to retrofit (EN-21, CN-26, HB-16).
- Source: synthesis of IE-03/IE-08/IE-09/IE-10/IE-16/IE-18/IE-19 above; cross-ref `decision-making.md` (veto-able in-repo choices) — in-corpus.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE / HEURISTIC (the specific order is judgement)
- Scope: value-engineering phase of any IEQ-relevant project
- Confidence: Medium (the ranking is defensible; the exact order shifts by building type and geology)
- Grid translation: Emit `ieq_investment_priority[room]` = an ordinal derived from the flags set in IE-03/IE-08/IE-10/IE-12/IE-16 (e.g. ground-contact + radon-prone → rank 1; speech-critical classroom → RT absorb; interior deep zone → perimeter/thermal). Publish a "cut list" with the retro-fitting cost class (cheap/easy vs costly/impossible) beside each candidate.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Cutting acoustic absorption and filtration as "invisible", then buying them back as remedial panels and retrofits; or over-spending on a circadian lighting spec (IE-13, recommendation-stage) while leaving the kitchen source unseparated (IE-12, hard PM outcome).

### IE-23 The IEQ outcome pass — five steps, run per floor after the façade and services zones are set
- Rule: Sequence the pass so it adds outcomes without re-touching quantities owned upstream. - Source: `envelope-daylight-quantification.md` §Climate pass procedure; `environmental-design.md` EN-18/EN-26 — in-corpus.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE (procedure)
- Scope: any inhabited floor
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Emit an `ieq_pass` record per floor: `{targets, air_basis_by_room, levers, computable:[…], flagged:[…], priority_cut:[…]}`. ## Weak or contested

- **"CO₂ ≤ 1000 ppm as a health/comfort limit" is the widely-believed number this file could not substantiate.** No WHO or ASHRAE document opened this pass sets 1000 ppm as a health threshold: WHO 2021 sets *no* CO₂ value at all, ASHRAE 62.1 frames IAQ around odour-satisfaction and ventilation rates rather than a CO₂ cap, and EN 15251's own restatement says the CO₂ indicator figures are "not consistent with the required ventilation rates." The 1000 ppm (≈ 700 above outdoor) rule is a legacy ASHRAE-era convention carried into client briefs without a current primary source. It is printed here as a *finding*, not as a limit. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the dated edition that first fixed 700/1000 ppm, if it is to be cited as anything.
- **The EN 15251 CO₂ category increments could not be read cleanly.** The REHVA page's Table-2 CO₂-increment values returned garbled in retrieval and are deliberately **not** reproduced; only the qualitative "CO₂ is an indicator, and disagrees with the rate method" finding is carried. Obtain the EN 15251 category table directly before quoting "Cat I/II/III = +350/+550/+800 ppm above outdoor".
- **Melanopic EDI is a consensus recommendation, not yet a regulation.** 250/10/1 lux at the eye are Brown et al. 2022's 18-expert values; the IES/CSA light-transmitted IP that would make melanopic EDI a codeable metric was not opened. Report as a target, not a limit, until the IP's thresholds and clause are read.
- **WELL v2 trails WHO 2021 on three pollutants.** WELL's PM₂.₅ 15 = WHO's *24-h* not annual (a 3× gap on the annual mean); WELL's NO₂ 53 ppb ≫ WHO annual 10; WELL's radon 4 pCi/L ≈ 148 > WHO 100 Bq/m³. A WELL pass is *not* a WHO pass — recorded as a gap column in IE-06, never averaged into a single "compliant".
- **ASHRAE 55's exact operative ranges and the design-band PPD % were not read from the standard.** The verified facts are the −0.5<PMV<+0.5 band and the ≥80% acceptability floor (attributed to ASHRAE 55-2017 via a T4 summary); "PPD ≤10%" and the per-season/clo/met °C tables are carried as specification convention and flagged — do not put them in an executable validator before opening the standard.
- **The ventilation→performance effect is real but noisy.** Kuramochi I²=84% means the pooled studies disagree more than they agree; the "no publication bias" result is reassuring but the demographic and task range is narrow. Directional support for SV-01, not a percentage to sell.
- **Daylighting→learning remains contested and correlational.** HMG's 15–23% faster is regression-adjusted association, not causation; the corpus already logs the methodology debate. The oft-cited "20% math / 26% reading" from the 1999 report was not the figure read here and is flagged.
- **ISO 3382-3 was mis-scoped in the commissioning brief** (it is the open-plan-office measurement part); classroom RT/STI came from BB93/DIN 18041/ANSI S12.60, and those came from a T4 reproduction, not the standards. Clause numbers named, not read — the specific limits need standard-text verification before use in a validator.
- **Cooking magnitudes are one niche study.** The ACS 2026 numbers are air-fryer chamber results; they establish order-of-magnitude and the ~10× dilution effect, not a universal cooking PM₂.₅ mass value. No residential *mass* (µg/m³) cooking peak was retrievable this pass.
- **The satisfaction-variance percentage was deliberately omitted.** A single-digit "% of thermal satisfaction explained by the measured environment" is widely repeated but appeared in no opened primary this pass; it is recorded as a gap (see the effect-size register) rather than repeated — the pattern this file enforces throughout.

- **Hotels (flagship).** The cellular guest floor concentrates the *outcome* failures the grid can actually catch: interior rooms have no operable façade, so IE-08's adaptive claim is invalid and IE-09's interior zone is internal-gain-dominated; the door undercut and shared bathroom plenum are the acoustic weak link (IE-17/HB-24); night light (IE-13) and the radon/ground-contact of the podium (IE-03) and the kitchen→guest-floor PM spill (IE-12) are the high-consequence, low-visibility items. Most *realisable* outcomes here stay `unknown` (no ppm/°C sensor on the host), so the hotel value of this file is the geometry that *protects* the outcome, not a score.
- **Offices.** Ventilation effectiveness and the CO₂-proxy discipline (IE-01/IE-10) and the thermal band/adaptive-applicability (IE-07/IE-08) bind; the whole "productivity" sales pitch leans on IE-18 and must be quoted with its I²=84% caveat.
- **Schools / education.** Acoustic outcome (IE-16, RT≤0.6 / STI≥0.6 / ≤35 dBA) is the strongest standard-backed, geometry-controlled item in the file; daylighting→learning (IE-19) is the loudest claim and the weakest evidence — keep them in separate columns.
- **Hospitals.** Source control, filtration, night-light (IE-13) and acoustic background (IE-17, EN-10) bind hardest; measured ward noise is far above target (in-corpus EN-10), so the outcome gap between criterion and reality is widest here.
- **Residential.** Radon (IE-03), cooking PM (IE-12) and formaldehyde source selection (IE-04) are the three outcomes that most often decide whether the dwelling is healthy — all decided at build, all expensive to retrofit (IE-21 rank 1–3).
- **Where this file is silent by intent.** No computed ppm/µg/m³/Bq/m³, no °C, no PPD %, no melanopic lux, no STI, no measured RT, no satisfaction figure, no monetised IEQ ROI, and no restatement of any SV-* rate, EN-18/EN-19 target, or EQ-13 daylight metric — each is either a plan-computable preliminary (with basis declared) or the exact `outcome_claim: UNVERIFIED-needs-[...]` flag, because a plan engine that can zone by exposure will otherwise be asked to certify outcomes it cannot see.
