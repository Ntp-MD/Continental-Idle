Scope: the **massing calculator** the corpus does not have. `coverage-map.md` §5 lists this as an open gap
("Quantified massing & urban generation: FAR/coverage/setback arithmetic, block typology metrics,
sky-exposure and daylight-recession style tests, street-network generation — `site-context.md` gives the
factors, not the numbers"). This file supplies the arithmetic only, and the boundary is deliberate:

  aspect edges (SC-02, SC-13), factor→consequence reasoning for noise (SC-14), drainage and imperviousness

  (SC-23). It also states, without computing, that FAR / coverage / imperviousness are three independent

  and daylight separation (SC-15), that overshadowing is tested against a protected receiver (SC-16), that
  D/H is one decision with street width (SC-20) and that the street wall is a percentage rule (SC-21).
  **This file does not re-argue any of those.** It supplies the numeric forms, the jurisdiction-stamped
  values and the worked computations that sit underneath SC-03, SC-04, SC-15, SC-16, SC-20, SC-21, SC-23.
- `multi-scale.md` MS-01…MS-24 owns the **stacking ladder and vertical discipline**: the vertical control
  grid (MS-01), stack order (MS-13), core continuity (MS-03/MS-04), core-to-plate ratio as *the* efficiency
  number (MS-19), shaft clustering (MS-05/MS-20), the podium-to-tower transition (MS-22). Where a rule here

  daylight-factor arithmetic** (EQ-05 shadow factor, EQ-07 inter-block gap, EQ-08 the 25°/45° tests,
  EQ-11/EQ-12 depth multipliers). This file cites those IDs for every solar or daylight figure and adds only
  the *bulk-control geometry* layer the code imposes on top of them.
- `structural-sizing.md` ST-* owns load take-down and grid discipline; `vertical-transport.md` VT-* owns
  lift bank and core count. This file names the massing-level input to each and stops.
- `cost-mathematics.md` (Domain AH) owns rates, elemental splits and life-cycle discounting; `economics.md`

  areas* and takes back only the efficiency input it needs to state a yield. No currency figure appears here.

Host grid, unchanged: `1 tile = 0.5 m`; `1 m² = 4 tiles`; `1 ha = 10 000 m² = 40 000 tiles`; `1 acre =
4 047 m² ≈ 16 188 tiles`; one independent 2D grid per floor; tile states `walkable | blocked | door`; no
material, no section — heights are carried as `storeys × floor-to-floor`, and every vertical figure below
states its floor-to-floor datum (the flagship case uses 3.6 m = 7.2 tiles and 4.0 m = 8.0 tiles, declared
per computation). Zoning figures are **jurisdiction-specific and amendable**. Nothing in this file makes a
design "compliant"; it makes a design *consistent with the cited rule*, and the verification step is named
in every rule.

## Rules

### MU-01 Compute the site budget as five independent numbers, then report which one binds
- Rule: For every site, emit all of: (1) permissible gross floor area `GFA_max = FAR × site_area`; (2) maximum footprint `A_max = coverage × site_area`; (3) buildable ground area after setbacks (per-edge mask, SC-03); (4) height-derived floor count `n_h = floor(H_limit ÷ floor_to_floor)`; (5) storey-count cap where the code counts storeys rather than metres. The achievable massing is `min` of these, and the report must name the binding one. Never publish "the scheme is 8 storeys" without the four other numbers.
- Evidence: Three of the five ceilings are independent by definition in every instrument opened this pass: Singapore defines plot ratio as "the ratio of the GFA of a building to its site or plot area" and GFA as "the total area of covered floor space … but excluding voids" (URA, T1); Germany fixes ground coverage and floor area as two separate numbers per zone (`GRZ`/`GFZ`, BauNVO §17(3)); New York sets residential lot coverage by district and section (80 % interior/through lots, 100 % corner lots in R6-R12, §23-362) *and* height/storeys by a different mechanism. A plan can satisfy FAR and break coverage (SC-04's point), or satisfy both and break the height-derived floor count — the arithmetic in **Site budget calculator** shows all three failure modes on one plot.
- Source: Urban Redevelopment Authority (Singapore), *Development Control Handbook — Gross Floor Area* — https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/ — T1 (page rendered 2026, handbook edition as shown on page); *Baunutzungsverordnung (BauNVO) §17 (3)* — https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/baunvo/__17.html — T1 (Germany, current consolidated text); NYC Zoning Resolution §23-362 — https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-362 — T1 (as amended, rendered 2026). Corpus: `site-context.md` SC-04 (the three-ceiling principle), SC-23 (quantity discipline).
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (the ceilings) / FACT (the `min` logic)
- Scope: every site, every type; the entry point for all other MU rules
- Confidence: High for the method and for each cited figure *as published*; Low for transferring any figure to another jurisdiction
- Grid translation: `site_tiles = W × H` at 0.5 m; `GFA_max_tiles = FAR × site_tiles`; one floor-plate area is `plate_tiles ÷ 4` m². Emit `binding_constraint ∈ {far, coverage, envelope, height, storeys}` in the record, and when the binding constraint is not `far` publish `stranded_far_tiles = GFA_max_tiles − gfa_delivered_tiles` — that is the number a design review will ask about.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Instruments that regulate by *dwelling units per hectare* or by form (US form-based codes, English site-allocation policies) have no FAR at all; then run the same five-slot report with `GFA_max` replaced by `units_max = density × site_area × (site ÷ gross)`. Failure symptom: a 5-storey scheme "permitted" because the FAR was met, in a district whose height cap allows 4.

### MU-02 Quote every GFA figure with its inclusion rule attached, because the same room is counted in one city and not in the next
- Rule: Never write a bare floor area. State the measurement basis and, for each of these six classes, whether the cited instrument counts it: circulation corridors, stair/lift cores and lobbies, basements and cellars, balconies/terraces, mechanical and equipment floors, and double-height voids. Where the instrument has a partial rule, publish the fraction.
- Evidence: Opened texts disagree in exactly the places that matter. Singapore: GFA is "the total area of covered floor space … including the half thickness of external walls … but excluding voids", and "all covered floor area, uncovered commercial spaces, and strata areas are included as GFA, unless exempted" — an air-conditioning ledge inside a strata boundary "will be computed as GFA", a common carpark is "not computed as GFA", and circulation is apportioned to the use it serves (URA GFA handbook + *GFA Related Matters*, T1). New York exempts by formula instead: "Fifty percent of the floor space of a corridor may be exempted from the definition of floor area" (§23-232), "three square feet per dwelling unit" of furnished space (§23-233), and "up to 100 square feet of such entryways" (§23-234) — T1. Germany counts floors, not walls: `GFZ` is a ratio of full-storey floor areas and its orientation values run 0.4-3.0 by zone (BauNVO §17(3), T1; the same 0.2-3 range is independently reported for European plot ratios at T4).
- Source: URA GFA handbook and *GFA Related Matters* — https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/ , https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/gfa-related-matters/ — T1 (Singapore); NYC ZR §§23-232/233, 23-234 — https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3 — T1 (New York City, text as rendered); BauNVO §17(3) — https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/baunvo/__17.html — T1 (Germany); Wikipedia, *Floor area ratio* — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floor_area_ratio — T4 (definition; "an FAR of 1.5 is translated as an FSI of 150 %"). Corpus: `economics.md` EC-01/EC-02 (measurement conventions GIA/net/rentable).
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (jurisdiction-stamped) / FACT (the arithmetic consequence of choosing one)
- Scope: all types; decisive for hotels and hospitals, where plant, service corridors and voids are a large share of the envelope
- Confidence: High for each quoted rule as published; Medium for completeness (only the leaf sections opened here, not the whole exemption article)
- Grid translation: Keep two ledgers per floor and never mix them: `tiles_gross` (all blocked+walkable tiles inside the envelope, i.e. GIA-like) and `tiles_counted` (after the jurisdiction's exemptions). For a NYC residential corridor the conversion is 50 %: a 3-tile-wide, 40-tile-long corridor = 120 tiles gross → 60 counted. For Singapore, void tiles are removed *before* the ratio, so a staircase opening costs nothing and an enclosed lift lobby costs full area. Publish `gfa_counting_basis` as a string.
- Exceptions / failure mode: The classic error is applying an exempt-area list from one district to another, or double-counting: a scheme that exempts a corridor *and* reports net-to-gross on the exempted figure (EC-02's warning). Second error: assuming a basement is free — Germany's `GFZ` counts a full storey wherever it is, Singapore counts covered basement floor area as GFA unless specifically exempted, and New York's §23-23/§12-10 cellar rules are use-dependent.

### MU-03 Convert a height limit into a floor count by dividing, and state the datum
- Rule: `n = floor((H_limit − parapet_or_roof_allowance) ÷ floor_to_floor)`. Floor-to-floor, not clear height, is the divisor; the quotient must be floored, never rounded. Publish which of the three common datums the limit uses (highest occupied floor, roof parapet, or top of mechanical screening), because 4 m of plant screening is one storey of yield.
- Evidence: Arithmetic, not opinion — a 24 m cap gives `24 ÷ 3.6 = 6.67 → 6` storeys, `24 ÷ 4.0 = 6.0 → 6` storeys and `24 ÷ 3.2 = 7.5 → 7` storeys, so the section choice alone moves the stack by one storey; on the worked plot one storey at the envelope plate is 1 690 m², which is 20.9 % of that site's entire permissible GFA. Instruments also regulate on different datums: Singapore's controls are GFA/plot-ratio based with a separate height limit (URA, T1), Germany's are storey- and roof-form based in the Bebauungsplan (BauNVO §16/§17 family, T1), and the Japanese regime uses absolute-height and diagonal-line caps measured from the street boundary (T4 practitioner summary; the statutory text was not reached this pass).
- Source: arithmetic derived here (FACT) over the corpus's declared section data (`multi-scale.md` MS-01/MS-02, 3.6 m = 7.2 tiles, 4.0 m = 8.0 tiles); jurisdiction datum evidence: URA — https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/ — T1; Touwa Corp., *What Japanese building regulations should you know?* — https://touwa-corp.co.jp/en/knowledge/regulations/ — T4 (practitioner summary, height limits quoted there as "10 m or 12 m" bands; NEEDS VERIFICATION).
- Class: FACT (the division) / STANDARD (the reporting of the datum)
- Scope: universal; the single most common source of "the model has more floors than allowed"
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Storeys are integers, so the grid is exact: `z_top(floor n) = n × floor_to_floor`, and with 0.5 m tiles `3.6 m = 7.2 tiles` → a floor is 7 full tiles plus 0.2 of drift, which must be absorbed at the top of each block of 5 storeys (MS-14's zero-drift rule). Report `storeys`, `height_m`, `floor_to_floor_m`, `height_datum` as a four-tuple; `round()` on the quotient is a defect.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Double-height lobbies, mezzanines and "deemed" storeys (some codes count a 6 m retail floor as two) break the division — handle by counting *deemed* storeys, not physical ones, and say which the instrument uses. Failure symptom: a "compliant-looking" 6-storey stack that is actually 7 because the ground floor was counted as one and the code counted it as two.

### MU-04 Run the setback envelope as a mask and the coverage cap as a second, tighter number
- Rule: Build `envelope = site_mask ⊖ per-edge setbacks` (round inward, SC-03), then compare the resulting ground area against `coverage × site_area`. Report `coverage_headroom = coverage_area − envelope_area`: when it is positive, the setbacks are the binding control and the coverage number is decoration; when negative, the footprint must shrink inside the envelope.
- Evidence: Setback distances of this order are published and are asymmetric by edge, which is exactly why the headroom term matters: Singapore requires a road buffer of "15m (5m)" on major roads and "7.5m (3m)" / "5m (3m)" on lesser ones, a "3m (including 2m peripheral planting verge)" boundary separation, and ancillary structures at "5m from Category 1 road / 3m from Category 2-5 roads / 2m" (URA *Building Setback from Boundary*, commercial handbook, T1, page updated 9 June 2026). New York's residential coverage caps (80 % interior lots, 100 % corner, and 65 %/50 % reductions on larger zoning lots under §23-434) show the same control moving with site size (§23-362, T1). Germany's `GRZ` orientation values (0.4 WA/WR; 0.6 WB/MI; 0.8 MU; 1.0 MK) cap the ground coverage of *all* structures and hard surfaces together (BauNVO §17(3), T1).
- Source: URA Development Control Handbook, *Building Setback from Boundary* (non-residential/commercial) — https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/non-residential/commercial/building-setback/ — T1 (Singapore); NYC ZR §23-362 — https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-362 — T1; BauNVO §17(3) — https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/baunvo/__17.html — T1. Corpus: SC-03 (mask method), SC-04, SC-23.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (jurisdiction-stamped figures) / FACT (the mask/headroom arithmetic)
- Scope: universal; the figures are Singapore's / New York's / Germany's, the method is not jurisdiction-specific
- Confidence: High for the method; High for each quoted figure *as published*; Low for transfer
- Grid translation: `setback_tiles = floor(setback_m ÷ 0.5)` rounded inward per edge; a 3 m side setback = 6 tiles, a 7.5 m buffer = 15 tiles, a 15 m buffer = 30 tiles. On a 40 × 60 m site (80 × 120 tiles) with build-to lines on the two street frontages, a 3 m west setback and a 6 m rear, the envelope is `40 × 54 = 2 160 m²` against a 70 % coverage cap of 1 680 m² — the cap binds, and 480 m² (22 %) of the envelope is legally unusable ground. Where the geometry is tighter (3 m on both flanks: `34 × 54 = 1 836 m²`) the cap still binds, by 156 m². Report `envelope_tiles`, `coverage_area_tiles`, `binding` separately, and re-run the pair for every edge combination the code treats differently (corner vs interior lot, §23-362's split — T1).
- Exceptions / failure mode: Zero-setback street-wall districts (NYC's "building line" districts, most US main-street form codes) make the envelope equal the site and put the entire burden on coverage and height; and basement slabs, canopies and open staircases are often *deemed* to occupy the yard (NYC §23-311/23-312 permits obstructions in yards — T1, sub-clauses not opened). Failure symptom: a design whose only violation is a 1-tile balcony inside a required rear yard.

### MU-05 Height-to-street-width is a formula: compute the cap the street imposes, do not inherit it
- Rule: Where a code ties building height to the width of the street it faces, compute `H_cap = c × W_street (+ base)` with the jurisdiction's coefficient `c`, then intersect that with the absolute height limit. If no coefficient is published for the site, record the ratio you assumed and mark it an input, not a rule.
- Evidence: The mechanism exists in the opened material in two forms. Japan applies a "road diagonal line" from the far street boundary, described by the practitioner source as a slope of "1:1.25 in residential districts" (T4 — NEEDS VERIFICATION of the coefficient per zone against Building Standards Act Art. 56). Singapore regulates the same relation the other way, by requiring a wider *road buffer* (15 m / 7.5 m / 5 m) as road category rises (URA setback handbook, T1). The qualitative counterpart already in the corpus is the enclosure-ratio bands (SC-20, Manual for Streets: no fixed rules, bands traded against daylight and wind), and the street-wall percentage is SC-21.
- Source: Touwa Corp. — https://touwa-corp.co.jp/en/knowledge/regulations/ — T4 (Japan practitioner summary); URA setback handbook — https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/non-residential/commercial/building-setback/ — T1; corpus: SC-20 (D/H bands), SC-21 (street-wall share), EQ-08 (obstruction angle).
- Class: HEURISTIC (the coefficient, absent a statute) / CODE REQUIREMENT (where a coefficient is published)
- Scope: urban street frontages, podium scales, and any design that is forming a street rather than facing one
- Confidence: Medium for the mechanism; Low for any particular coefficient
- Grid translation: A 20 m street = 40 tiles; `c = 1.25` gives `H_cap = 25 m` = 50 tiles of stack, i.e. 6 storeys at 3.6 m or 5 at 4.8 m. Implement as a per-edge height budget: `z_max(edge) = c × w_street(edge)`, so a corner site has two budgets and the lower one shapes the envelope.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Applying the ratio to the *tall* part of a podium-and-tower section is the usual misuse — the ratio governs the street wall, and the tower behind it is governed by separation and plane controls (MU-08, MU-09). Failure symptom: a 6-storey wall on an 8 m lane that reads as a canyon (SC-20's trade-off with wind, EQ-08's no-sky-line region).

### MU-06 Derive tower separation from the daylight/overlooking figure that binds, and take the larger
- Rule: Separation between two buildings is `max(privacy_min, daylight_min, fire_min)` for the facing pair, where `daylight_min = (H_obstruction − h_receiver) × cot α` for the accepted sun altitude (EQ-07) and `privacy_min` is the jurisdiction's number. Publish which of the three governed each pair.
- Evidence: The corpus already establishes the two inputs and the "take the greater" method (SC-15: published separation minima for privacy/outlook and daylight, "the governing value is whichever is greater once the obstruction's size and storey count are taken into account", citing Barnsley MBC SPD and BRE 3rd ed. 2022 — T1/T2, 21 m / 25 m family of figures recorded there). The geometry is EQ-07's, worked there: at 40°N on 21 Dec a 30 m block needs `117 m` of gap for 09:00 sun and `58 m` at noon. Independent published separations are smaller and surface-scale: Singapore requires "3m (including 2m peripheral planting verge)" between buildings at a shared boundary (URA, T1), i.e. a boundary-separation rule, not an amenity rule — a warning against importing one number for all three purposes.
- Source: URA setback handbook — https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/non-residential/commercial/building-setback/ — T1 (Singapore); corpus: `site-context.md` SC-15 (privacy/daylight minima and their sources), `envelope-daylight-quantification.md` EQ-07/EQ-08 (sun and angle arithmetic), `fire-safety-quantification.md` FQ-* and `building-codes.md` CODE-* (apparatus access, cross-ref SC-09).
- Class: STANDARD (method) / CODE REQUIREMENT (each jurisdiction's figure, separately stamped)
- Scope: multi-building sites, courtyards, tower clusters, campuses, resorts
- Confidence: High for the method; Medium for the UK figures as recorded in SC-15; Low for their transfer
- Grid translation: 21 m = 42 tiles, 25 m = 50 tiles; the daylight figure is not a constant, so compute it per pair per season and store `separation_m`, `governed_by`. On this grid the two-block case is trivially checkable: `min(pair_distance) over facing tiles ≥ required`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Narrow courtyards satisfy the *gap* formula and fail daylight inside both blocks (EN-08's and EQ-07's stated exception). Failure symptom: a "1.0 × height" separation with every living room on the courtyard side.

### MU-07 Compute the sky-exposure/angular-plane envelope as an intersection of per-edge planes
- Rule: The buildable volume is the intersection of (a) the setback prism, (b) the absolute height prism, and (c) one plane per controlling edge, each plane rising from a stated base height at a stated slope measured from the boundary line. For floor `n`, the mask is `envelope ∩ {tiles whose distance from each controlling line ≥ (z_n − base_i) ÷ slope_i}`. Emit the resulting shape class (flat plate / stepped / tower-in-yard) as a metric, not as a preference.
- Evidence: This is what the published instruments describe. New York's residential bulk system runs on planes and height factors: §23-73 sets out special provisions for "Sky Exposure Plane Buildings" and §23-732 pairs a maximum floor area ratio with a *minimum required open space ratio* that increases with the height factor (the rendered table gives increments of "+0.5" per additional height factor for R6/R7, "+0.3" for R8, "+0.4" for R9 — T1; the district-by-district FAR values in the same table were NOT cleanly rendered: **NEEDS VERIFICATION** of the exact R6-R9 FAR/OSR pairs from the official section text). Japan's regime is plane-based by name (road and north-side diagonal lines, T4 practitioner summary; statutory coefficients NOT VERIFIED). The sun-plane consequences are already computed in EQ-05/EQ-07 and the 25°/45° screening tests in EQ-08.
- Source: NYC ZR §23-73 and §23-732 — https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-73 , https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-732 — T1 (New York City, text as rendered); §23-36/23-362 — https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-362 — T1; Touwa Corp. — https://touwa-corp.co.jp/en/knowledge/regulations/ — T4; corpus EQ-05/EQ-07/EQ-08, SC-16.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (where planes are statutory) / FACT (the intersection algorithm)
- Scope: dense urban sites, towers, any site with a plane control; the shape generator for MU-13…MU-18
- Confidence: High for the algorithm; Medium for the NYC mechanism (section structure confirmed, several table values unverified); Low for the Japanese coefficients
- Grid translation: A plane with slope `s` (horizontal per unit vertical) and base `b` metres cuts the grid at `d_n = (z_n − b) × s`; store it as one integer per edge per floor, so the whole envelope is a stack of rectangles and the massing is `mask(n) = envelope ∩ ⋂_edges rect(edge, d_n)`. Worked: with `b = 30 m`, `s = 2.5`, the 40 m line gives `d = 25 m` = 50 tiles and the 60 m line `d = 75 m`, off-site — i.e. one plane is active and one is not, and that is the moment the scheme becomes a tower.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Reading the plane as a *shadow* rule or vice versa (they answer different questions: one is a legal envelope, one is solar geometry — SC-16/EQ-07). Second: intersecting planes in the wrong datum (from the building face vs from the street line). Failure symptom: a stepped massing that "meets the envelope" but whose steps are on the wrong elevation.

### MU-08 Publish the open-space / permeable-area budget as a residual, and check it against the coverage cap
- Rule: After footprint and paving, the remainder is the open-space budget. Emit `open_share = 1 − (footprint + paved) ÷ site_area` and compare against the jurisdiction's requirement (open space ratio, permeable/landscaped area, or a green-area figure). Where the requirement is expressed as a *ratio of site area* (NYC's OSR), the tower-in-the-yard option is a trade of FAR for open space and must be reported as such.
- Evidence: The instruments opened do use exactly these forms: NYC §23-732 pairs a *maximum* FAR with a *minimum* open space ratio per height factor (T1, increments quoted in MU-07); Germany caps the sum of building and hard surfaces through `GRZ` with a stated `GFZ` above it (BauNVO §17(3), T1); Singapore's planting verge (2 m of the 3 m boundary separation) is a landscape requirement embedded in a setback (URA, T1). The imperviousness consequences are SC-04/SC-18 (NEMO research values, 10-15 % stress threshold) and the per-capita green-space benchmark from the opened public-health literature is WHO's "minimum of 9 m2 of green space per individual with an ideal UGS value of 50 m2 per capita" (Russo & Cirella 2018 reporting WHO 2012 — T2, second-hand).
- Source: NYC ZR §23-732 — https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-732 — T1; BauNVO §17(3) — https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/baunvo/__17.html — T1; URA setback handbook — T1; Russo, A.M. & Cirella, G.S., "Modern Compact Cities: How Much Greenery Do We Need?", *IJERPH* 15(10):2190, 2018 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6209905/ — T2 (quotes WHO 2012; the WHO original was not opened → NEEDS VERIFICATION for the primary wording). Corpus: SC-04, SC-18, and `architecture-theory.md` TH-24 for the published room minima this budget has to sit beside.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (ratios) / STANDARD (residual reporting)
- Scope: all sites; the figure most often left implicit until a reviewer asks
- Confidence: High for the method and for the cited ratios as published; Medium for the WHO per-capita figures (reached through one opened secondary)
- Grid translation: Classify every non-building tile `soft | paved | water | deck` and report the shares; `1 ha = 40 000 tiles`, so at 9 m²/person (36 tiles) a 2400 m² site with 300 m² of soft ground supports 33 persons' worth of the minimum. A podium deck is *not* open space unless the instrument says so; carry a boolean `deck_counts_as_open` and name the rule.
- Exceptions / failure mode: The classic inversion — meeting the OSR by making the site a plaza. Second: double-counting a green roof as both landscaped area and building footprint relief without a rule that allows it. Failure symptom: an amenity-space figure that only exists as a deck above a car park with 0.3 m of substrate.

### MU-09 Derive the storey ladder from the floor-to-floor budget before the massing shape, and check both section and envelope agree
- Rule: Choose `floor_to_floor` per use from the programmatic requirement (structure + services + clear), then compute the storeys a height cap permits, then compute the GFA those storeys deliver at the permitted plate, then compare with the FAR cap. The smallest of the two GFA figures is the real yield; publish both and the gap between them.
- Evidence: This is the arithmetic that decides the flagship plots. On the 60 m × 45 m plot at `FAR 3.0` and an envelope-limited footprint of 1 690 m², the FAR permits 8 100 m² — worth 4.79 storeys. Under an **18 m** cap the section choice then changes the answer outright: `floor(18 ÷ 3.6) = 5` storeys delivers 8 450 m² of physical capacity, so the FAR binds and all 8 100 m² are built (a stepped fifth floor at 79 % plate); at 4.0 m `floor(18 ÷ 4.0) = 4` storeys delivers only 6 760 m², stranding **1 340 m² = 16.5 %** of the permissible GFA. Under a **24 m** cap both sections reach six storeys, the FAR binds in both cases, and the extra storey is worth nothing — the same plot with the opposite conclusion. Full table in **Site budget calculator**. Section data come from the corpus (MS-01/MS-02 stack, MS-19 core ratio, EC-06 plate curve).
- Source: computed here from the declared inputs (FACT); inputs cross-referenced to `multi-scale.md` MS-01/MS-02/MS-19, `economics.md` EC-06, `grid-translation.md` GT-24; jurisdictional ceilings as cited in MU-01.
- Class: FACT (arithmetic) / STANDARD (report both caps)
- Scope: every multi-storey massing decision; hotels and hospitals first, because their floor-to-floor is set by services (SV-*, VT-*)
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Storey height in tiles = `floor_to_floor ÷ 0.5` (3.6 m = 7.2 tiles; 4.0 m = 8.0 tiles) — keep the fractional tiles and carry the drift to the roof, do not silently round (MS-14). `gfa_height = n × plate_tiles`, and `stranded_far_tiles = GFA_max_tiles − gfa_height_tiles` is the number to report when the shape is wrong.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Mixed-section buildings (4.8 m retail over 3.6 m hotel) make a single divisor wrong — compute the ladder per band. Failure symptom: a massing that "fits the FAR" only because the model assumed one floor-to-floor everywhere.

### MU-10 Convert permissible GFA into a unit or key count, then check the count against the mix rules
- Rule: `units_max = GFA_counted ÷ (average unit area ÷ net-to-gross)`. Then test the count against whatever the instrument says about *size* rather than area, because density rules usually arrive as a pair: a minimum unit size and a required size mix. Report `units_max`, the implied dwellings/ha, and the mix feasibility together; a yield that satisfies the area and breaks the mix is not a yield.
- Evidence: Singapore is the cleanest opened example of the pair: "all self-contained DUs island-wide shall be at least 35sqm nett in internal area"; outside the Central Area a scheme must provide "A minimum of 20% of DUs with nett internal area of at least 100 sqm" and may provide "A maximum of 20% of DUs with nett internal area of 50 sqm or less"; in the Central Area the floor is "A minimum of 20% of DUs with nett internal area of at least 70 sqm" (URA, *Guidelines on Dwelling Units in Non-Landed Residential Developments*, handbook page updated 7 June 2026 — T1; the operative formula itself is published on that page as images, so the *arithmetic form* is **NEEDS VERIFICATION**). The hotel analogue is in-corpus and already stamped: 68-72 m² of GFA per key for the flagship case (`hotel-operating-standards.md` HO-04) and the key-area band (HO-08).
- Source: URA, *Development Control Handbook — Guidelines on Dwelling Units* — https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/residential/flats-condominiums/maximum-number-du/ — T1 (Singapore); in-corpus: HO-04/HO-08, EC-06, EC-19.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (the size floors and mix shares) / FACT (the division)
- Scope: residential and hospitality massing; the bridge between the site budget and the programme
- Confidence: High for the quoted percentages and the 35 m² floor; Medium for their transfer (they are Singapore's, island-wide with a Central Area carve-out); Low for the exact averaging rule (image-only on the page read)
- Grid translation: Work in m² then convert (`1 m² = 4 tiles`). A 14,400 m² permissible GFA at 70 m²/key → 206 keys; at 35 m² nett + 25 % core/loss allowance (efficiency 0.75) → `14,400 × 0.75 ÷ 35 = 308` minimum-size units. Report `units_per_ha = units ÷ (site_m² ÷ 10 000)` with `net_or_gross` declared — the same site reads 1283 units/ha gross and far less net once roads and open space are removed (cross-ref MU-19, SA-21).
- Exceptions / failure mode: Studio-heavy and co-living schemes break the mix rule rather than the area rule (the 20 %-of-50 m² cap is the binding number, not FAR). Second: quoting dph without saying net or gross makes every comparison meaningless (SC-23's discipline applied to density). Third: the same conversion for *jobs* rather than dwellings uses an area-per-worker factor the corpus already stamps to a code (FQ-02 / CODE-01: business occupant load ≈ 14 m² gross per person, i.e. 150 ft²) — at `FAR 7.5` one hectare therefore carries about 5 300 jobs gross, which is the figure a jobs-housing balance would consume; no planning instrument defining that index was reachable this pass, so treat any jobs-housing target as `HEURISTIC` until one is cited. Failure symptom: a "300-unit" scheme that cannot place 20 % of them above 100 m².

### MU-11 Perimeter block: the depth is set by daylight reach plus the corridor, and the courtyard by the sun
- Rule: Gross block depth = `2 × room_band_depth + corridor_width + 2 × wall`. Take `room_band_depth` from the daylight arithmetic (EQ-11/EQ-12), not from habit, and treat any excess as an electrically lit band that must be justified. Minimum courtyard dimension comes from the sun/angle tests (EQ-07, EQ-08), not from the plan grid.
- Evidence: The reach numbers are already sourced in the corpus: with head height 2.1 m, the published multipliers give 2.1 m (1.0 × H), 3.15 m (1.5 × H) and 3.0 m (2.5 × glazing height) — EQ-11, and EQ-12 computes the consequence for plates. Room bands of 4.2 m and corridors of 2.4 m are the flagship hotel and residential section data (`multi-scale.md` MS-01 stack, `floor-plans.md` FL-*, `human-scale.md` HS-03/HS-21 for aisle and effective corridor widths). Germany's `GFZ` orientation values show what the typology must deliver to be useful: 1.2 in WA/WR, 1.6 in WB, 3.0 in MU/MK (BauNVO §17(3) — T1), i.e. a 4-6 storey perimeter block on `GRZ 0.6` reaches `GFZ ≈ 2.4-3.0`.
- Source: BauNVO §17(3) — https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/baunvo/__17.html — T1 (Germany); corpus: EQ-11, EQ-12, EN-03 (plate depth caps), EN-08/EQ-07 (courtyard self-shading), SC-23.
- Class: ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT (the daylight bound) / DESIGN PRINCIPLE (the response)
- Scope: urban residential and mixed blocks, 3-8 storeys; not applicable where the plate is set by a process (retail box, hospital ward block)
- Confidence: High for the arithmetic; Medium for the multipliers it inherits (EQ-11 shows three non-interchangeable published values); NOT ESTABLISHED for a published minimum courtyard size (see **Weak or contested**)
- Grid translation: `depth = 2 × 8 tiles + 5 tiles + 1 tile = 22 tiles = 11 m` for a two-aspect residential block; a 16 m (32-tile) depth puts 2 tiles on each side beyond the 3.15 m reach — report `unlit_core_depth_m`. Courtyard check: `courtyard_tiles ≥ required_gap(pair, season)` from EQ-07's formula, and the self-shading test `sun_hours(courtyard face) > 0` or the aspect is decoration (EN-08's warning).
- Exceptions / failure mode: A courtyard that passes the *gap* test and fails the *inside* test (EQ-07's stated exception, EN-08's "opposed aspects looking onto a self-shaded void"). Deep perimeter blocks with a light-well section read as double-aspect in a metric and single-aspect in a room. Failure symptom: `double_aspect_share` high and `daylit_share` low on the same plan.

### MU-12 Point tower: the plate cap, the core ratio and the apartment count per core are one equation
- Rule: Fix the plate cap from the code or the design policy, then solve `units_per_floor = (plate × net_to_gross − core_area) ÷ average_unit_area` and check the result against the travel-distance and lift-capacity bounds. If the count needs a second core, the plate is too big — split the plate, do not pad the core.
- Evidence: The plate cap is a published control where high-rise policy exists: the corpus records Toronto's minimum separation and floor-plate minima adopted in its Tall Building Design Guidelines through SC-23, and `multi-scale.md` MS-04/MS-19 fix the single-core rule and the core-to-plate ratio as the efficiency number. The daylight ceiling on plate depth is EQ-12's arithmetic (double-sided reach `2 × 3.15 m` = 6.3 m of guaranteed reach; an 18 m plate is 65 % shadow band). The lift-side bound is VT-*'s handling-capacity arithmetic and MS-11's stop-coverage rule; ADA/IBC's elevator exemption threshold in MU-18 shows how jurisdictionally unstable the core question is.
- Source: corpus: `site-context.md` SC-23 (Toronto floor-plate/separation minima, with its own T1 citation and its limits), `multi-scale.md` MS-04/MS-19, `envelope-daylight-quantification.md` EQ-12, `vertical-transport.md` VT-*. External numeric cap: **NEEDS VERIFICATION** for any specific m² plate limit (the Toronto guideline PDF was not opened this pass; SC-23's recorded value governs until re-read).
- Class: ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT (core/travel/daylight bounds) / HEURISTIC (the plate cap itself)
- Scope: towers 6+ storeys on constrained urban sites; hotels and residential
- Confidence: High for the equation; Medium for the inputs, which are project- or jurisdiction-set
- Grid translation: A 700 m² plate = 2800 tiles, e.g. `40 × 70 tiles (20 m × 35 m)`. At efficiency 0.75 → 525 m² lettable → 7 units at 70 m² or 15 at 35 m². Core band: measure `core_tiles ÷ plate_tiles` and report against MS-19; the grid makes the check exact because the core is a tile set repeated by MS-03.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Slab towers (deep plate, single loaded corridor) hide the failure: the count per core is satisfied while two units are single-aspect onto a court. Failure symptom: `units_per_floor` rising while `double_aspect_share` falls — report both.

### MU-13 Slab / line block: length is the risk, depth is the constraint, and the network pays for the length
- Rule: A line block longer than the inherited grain's block length is a connectivity defect regardless of its plan quality; cap length at the value that keeps the route grid continuous with the surrounding blocks (SC-11) and measure the consequence with SA-21's indices; cap depth at MU-11's daylight number.
- Evidence: The corpus's rule is qualitative and jurisdiction-neutral: `site-context.md` SC-11 makes the inherited grain a constraint — "Read the existing block dimensions, junction frequency and network type around the site before proposing a site layout, and keep the new routes continuous with that grain. A large parcel is a strong argument for subdividing it with new streets, not for a single superblock with one access", with Jacobs' "CONDITION 2: Most blocks must be short" behind it (T3 there). `spatial-analytics.md` SA-21 owns the indices that measure the consequence (node density, link density, intersection density, `α/β/γ`, ringness), and SA-13 the detour penalty. **No numeric block-length band is asserted here:** the FHWA/CNU recommended-practice page that would carry one returned 403 this pass, and the earlier 90 m / 300 m pair that circulated in this corpus was withdrawn when `human-scale.md` was narrowed to ergometrics. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** a published maximum block length or junction-spacing standard, with its authority. Daylight depth is EQ-11/EQ-12's arithmetic again.
- Source: corpus: SC-11 (street grain and superblock critique, with its own citations), SA-21 (permeability indices and their NEEDS-VERIFICATION bands), SA-13 (trip statistics), SC-14 (edge-as-screen mechanism), EQ-12. External: none — see the NEEDS VERIFICATION note above and **Sources** item 17.
- Class: DESIGN PRINCIPLE (the length argument) / FACT (the metric consequences)
- Scope: residential bars, campus and hospital ward blocks, peripheral and edge developments
- Confidence: High for the metric and for the grain-as-constraint reading; Low for any specific length limit, which is not printed
- Grid translation: `block_length_tiles = façades + routes`; run SA-21 on the site layer and refuse any single massing that raises `detour_index` on the through-route (SA-13) by more than the declared tolerance. On a 60 m site a 110-tile (55 m) bar with one penetration reads as permeable in the drawing and `no_circuit` in the metric — the penetration must be `walkable`, not gated.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Sound barriers, rail edges and river edges justify a long block *as* a screen (SC-14's mechanism); then the design owes a compensating crossing at the stated spacing, and the record must say so. Failure symptom: a "permeable" site plan where the only gate is the one the fire authority locks.

### MU-14 Terraced / stepped massing: the steps are the plane intersection, and each step buys headroom you can count
- Rule: Where a plane or a height limit forces a step, enumerate the step positions and compute the GFA recovered by widening the plate below the plane versus the GFA lost by shortening the stack above it. Choose the step line by the arithmetic; report `gfa_at_step_n` for each candidate, not the shape you like.
- Evidence: This is the direct use of MU-07's plane stack, with the published NYC pairing showing what steps are traded against (a maximum FAR set against a minimum open space ratio, incremented per height factor — §23-732, T1, as flagged in MU-07) and Japan's north-side/road diagonals being the reason stepped roofs are ubiquitous on small Japanese urban sites (T4 practitioner summary; coefficient unverified). The section logic that keeps a stepped massing buildable is MS-14 (module repetition, zero drift) and ST-*'s grid continuity.
- Source: NYC ZR §23-732 — https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-732 — T1; Touwa Corp. — https://touwa-corp.co.jp/en/knowledge/regulations/ — T4; corpus: MS-14, MS-22, `structural-sizing.md` ST-*.
- Class: FACT (the enumeration) / CODE REQUIREMENT (the plane that forces it)
- Scope: plane-constrained sites, sloping sites, schools and hospitals with low-rise wings
- Confidence: High for the method; Medium for the specific statutory triggers read this pass
- Grid translation: Each step is a change in the per-floor mask; implement as `mask(n) = envelope ∩ ⋂ planes(n)` (MU-07) and the "step" is where a mask first shrinks. GFA recovered by stepping at floor `n` = `Σ (plate(n') − plate_tower)` — one integer sum. Keep `wall_step` on a tile line so the setback is measurable (SC-21's street-wall check).
- Exceptions / failure mode: Stepping that breaks the structural grid or the core (MS-03, MS-07, ST-*) is a false economy: one transfer floor costs more than the GFA gained. Failure symptom: three steps, two core offsets, one transfer slab.

### MU-15 Podium + tower: the podium takes the coverage cap, the tower takes the plate cap, and the two are checked separately
- Rule: Size the podium at the permitted footprint (MU-04) and the height of the street wall (MU-05), then set the tower footprint at the plate cap (MU-12) and check three things the composite hides: the tower's shadow on its own podium terraces (EQ-07), the core continuity through the podium (MS-22), and the servicing that the podium must absorb (MU-21).
- Evidence: Toronto's tall-building regime is the corpus's recorded source for the podium-to-tower discipline (SC-22 phasing, SC-23 plate and separation minima) and MS-22 owns the transition itself. The plane math that produces the podium height is MU-05/MU-07; the self-shading arithmetic is EQ-05/EQ-07, which computed at 40°N noon in December that a 60-tile (30 m) block lays ~120 tiles (60 m) of shadow — a 12-storey tower beside a 4-storey podium puts the podium's north terrace in that shadow for the whole winter day.
- Source: corpus: SC-22/SC-23 (with their T1 citations and limits), MS-22, EQ-05/EQ-07; URA setback handbook — https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/non-residential/commercial/building-setback/ — T1 (buffers the podium must respect).
- Class: STANDARD (the two-cap split) / FACT (the shadow consequence)
- Scope: urban hotels, mixed-use, any tower on a site with a street wall
- Confidence: High for the method; Medium for the transferred plate/separation figures, which stay stamped to their own cities in SC-23
- Grid translation: Model the podium and tower as separate masks with separate caps: `podium_tiles ≤ coverage_area_tiles`, `tower_tiles ≤ plate_cap_tiles`; the podium roof is a *floor* for terrace checks, so run the daylight metric on it. Report `podium_share_of_gfa` and `tower_footprint ÷ coverage_area`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Treating the podium roof as free space — it is a structure, a drainage surface and usually a GFA-relevant element (MU-02, MU-08's `deck_counts_as_open` flag). Failure symptom: a scheme at 96 % of the coverage cap whose tower plate is 40 % of it.

### MU-16 Choose the typology from two ratios — rights against flat-plate capacity, programme against rights — not from taste
- Rule: Compute two ratios and publish both. `flat_capacity = plate_max × n_storeys` (the largest flat-plate stack the envelope, coverage and height cap allow), then `yield_index = GFA_max ÷ flat_capacity`; and `programme_index = GFA_programme ÷ GFA_max`. Below 1 the area rights run out before the envelope does, so the plate should shrink upward (steps, tower, or deliberately unused FAR); above 1 the envelope and height cap are binding and only more storeys, a wider plate or relief closes the gap. A `programme_index` above 1 is not a massing problem — it is a brief problem, and it must be escalated as such.
- Evidence: The two indices separate the two failures the flagship cases show. The 60 × 45 m plot at an 18 m cap: `8 100 ÷ (1 690 × 5) = 0.96` — rights and envelope are matched, so a flat plate at five storeys is the answer and a tower would buy nothing. The 40 × 60 m hotel plot: `plate_max = 1 680` m² at the coverage cap and `n = 18` storeys inside the 70 m limit (2 podium storeys at 4.8 m plus 16 tower storeys at 3.6 m = 67.2 m), so `flat_capacity = 30 240 m²` and `yield_index = 18 000 ÷ 30 240 = 0.60` — the FAR is the first limiter, which is the arithmetic reason the scheme steps back to a tower rather than filling the site; and `programme_index = 20 860 ÷ 18 000 = 1.16` says the 250-key brief does not fit the *rights* at all, before MU-12's plate cap reduces the delivered figure again (both computed in **Worked site-yield example**). Germany's zone pairs legislate the same relation directly: `GRZ 0.4 / GFZ 1.2` (WA) allows about three storeys' worth at full coverage while `GRZ 1.0 / GFZ 3.0` (MK) allows three storeys on every square metre of the plot (BauNVO §17(3) — T1).
- Source: computed here (FACT); BauNVO §17(3) — https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/baunvo/__17.html — T1; typology properties cross-referenced to MU-11…MU-15 and to `architecture-theory.md` TH-01, `economics.md` EC-06.
- Class: HEURISTIC (the index is this file's construction) / FACT (the arithmetic in it)
- Scope: massing optioneering on any site with at least two of the three controls active
- Confidence: Medium (useful discriminator, no external validation of the index itself)
- Grid translation: Two divisions per option; store `yield_index` and `programme_index` beside `binding_constraint`. Options to enumerate: flat plate, stepped, perimeter block, tower-in-yard, podium+tower — see MU-26's recipe.
- Exceptions / failure mode: The index is blind to the *shape* penalties (a 1.0 index can be an over-deep plate that fails MU-11) and to code types that cap units rather than area (MU-10). Never let it choose alone — it ranks, it does not decide. Failure symptom: an option ranked first on yield and rejected on daylight.

### MU-17 Plate geometry is a conversion asset: keep the depth near the residential-compatible band
- Rule: Where long-life value or a future change of use matters, hold plate depth close to the double-aspect daylight bound (MU-11) and check it against the in-corpus conversion figure rather than against the maximum the envelope allows.
- Evidence: The in-corpus figure is `EC-17`: office→residential conversion works at plates of about 18 m (recorded there as a T3 source: Neumann Monson, *Design Thinking: Floor Plates and Office Adaptive Reuse*, 2023, and the Advanced Buildings daylight pattern guide), against EQ-12's computation that an 18 m (36-tile) office plate is 65 % shadow band under the 1.5 × H rule. The two statements are consistent and they bound each other: 18 m is the practical conversion ceiling and the daylight arithmetic explains why it is a ceiling.
- Source: corpus: `economics.md` EC-17 (with its source list), EQ-11/EQ-12; `failure-patterns.md` FL-01 (circulation share) and `grid-translation.md` GT-24 (efficiency after quantisation).
- Class: HEURISTIC (the band) / FACT (the daylight arithmetic behind it)
- Scope: offices, hybrid and speculative urban plots, and any hotel/office stack where floors may convert
- Confidence: Medium (single secondary source for the 18 m value, in-corpus and labelled T3 there)
- Grid translation: `plate_depth_tiles` and `double_aspect_share` per option; a 36-tile (18 m) depth is the reported threshold, so the metric key is a count, not a prose claim. Rotation and notching to fake double aspect is detectable: count *distinct* aspects per unit, not per floor.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Deep plates are correct when the use needs depth (labs, hospital diagnostics, retail, data halls) — then say the design is opting out of conversion, and report `daylit_share` honestly (EQ-12/EQ-13). Failure symptom: a "flexible" 26 m plate that converts into nothing.

### MU-18 State the vertical-access threshold as the pair of limits it is written as, and read the conjunction carefully
- Rule: Access-lift mandates are typically written with two numeric limbs — storeys and per-storey area — and the word joining them decides everything. Record `threshold_storeys`, `threshold_area`, `joiner ∈ {and, or}` and the derived `lift_required` for every scheme, including the rejected options. Where the limbs are joined by **or**, a tall building with small floors can be exempt and a low building with large floors cannot: the massing choice, not the height alone, decides.
- Evidence: The opened US comparison states its exception in the disjunctive form: "Elevators are not required in facilities that are less than three stories or that have less than 3000 square feet per story" (ADA 4.1.3(5) / 206.2.3 comparison, US Access Board — T1), with the mezzanine rule set by "aggregate area of not more than 3,000 square feet (278.7 m2)" and "occupant load of five or fewer persons" (IBC 1104.4, same source — T1). Nothing in these provisions makes a 700 m², 16-storey residential tower optional — it fails both limbs — but the same GFA spread over four 250 m² floors would sit under the area limb, which is exactly the kind of massing decision that is a code decision. The corollary runs the other way too: the *massing* choice changes the core count, and the core count changes the yield through MS-19's ratio.
- Source: US Access Board, *ADA Standards for Accessible Design — ADA/IBC comparison, Chapter 2* — https://www.access-board.gov/ada/ada-ibc-comparison/chapter-2/ — T1 (United States, 2010 ADA Standards / IBC comparison). Corpus: `multi-scale.md` MS-10/MS-11, `vertical-transport.md` VT-*, `building-codes.md` CODE-* (egress), `operations-maintenance.md` OM-16.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (the threshold pair, US-stamped) / FACT (the conversion)
- Scope: every multi-storey massing; the thresholds and their joiners are jurisdiction-specific and must be re-read per site
- Confidence: High for the quoted exception as published; Low for its transfer (England, Australia and Germany set their own, different thresholds and typically mandatory residential lifts well below these limits — **NEEDS VERIFICATION** per site)
- Grid translation: `3000 ft² = 278.7 m² = 1 115 tiles`. Evaluate both limbs per option and store the joiner, because `lift_required` is not a height test: a 4-storey, 1 500 m² plate and a 12-storey, 500 m² plate can return different answers under the same text. The core that satisfies `lift_required = true` is MS-04's single core until its travel/queue bounds fail (MU-19).
- Exceptions / failure mode: A code exemption is not a design decision: an exempt 3-storey plate still needs an accessible route (MS-16's continuity rule, reached through SC-16's site-level version) and an egress path (MS-21), and residential accessibility duties often survive an elevator exemption. Failure symptom: "no lift required" reported as "no vertical access designed".

### MU-19 Bound the apartment count per core by travel distance and handling capacity, not by a folklore band
- Rule: `units_per_floor ≤ f(egress travel distance, common path, lift handling capacity, lobby queue)`. Compute the bound from the two governing arithmetic sets (egress: CODE-*/FQ-*; vertical transport: VT-*) and publish the resulting maximum, the binding term, and the units actually placed. Do not carry a "12 flats per core" style number unless a jurisdiction's own document is cited with its edition.
- Evidence: The corpus owns both operands and neither is folklore: MS-04 fixes the single-core start condition ("floor-plate depth from core face to façade exceeds the reach bound, or portal queue demand exceeds what one lobby can absorb") and records its own core-count thresholds as `UNCITED — heuristic`; VT-* builds handling capacity, interval and QoS from declared inputs and publishes the flagship 250-key hotel case; `fire-safety-quantification.md` and `building-codes.md` carry the prescriptive travel and common-path limits with their clause addresses. What is *not* in the corpus, and was not reached externally this pass, is any official dwellings-per-core band.
- Source: corpus: MS-04, MS-11, MS-19, VT-* (traffic analysis), FQ-*/CODE-* (travel and common-path limits). External: **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** obtain the adopted edition of the local egress code's per-storey exit provisions and any published unit-count cap per core (e.g. a national multifamily guidance) before printing a band.
- Class: FACT (the bound is arithmetic) / HEURISTIC (any unsourced units-per-core band)
- Scope: residential and hotel towers; the rule that turns a plate decision into a core decision
- Confidence: High for the method; Low for any numeric band not re-sourced
- Grid translation: Per floor: `max_travel_tiles` from the core tiles to the farthest unit door (octile distance, MS-11/MS-16 route rules), and `lift_bank_tiles` from VT-*'s group sizing; then `units_per_floor` is capped by whichever binds first, and the count check is exact because doors and cores are tile sets. Report `core_count`, `units_per_core_per_floor`, `binding_term`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Two cores on one plate can satisfy travel distance and still fail MS-19's efficiency test and MS-03's continuity rule — the correct answer is then a smaller plate, MU-12's equation restated. Failure symptom: units per floor rising while every unit's travel to the lift rises with them.

### MU-20 Size the movement network the massing implies, then check the massing against it
- Rule: A massing option implies a pedestrian demand at each of its edges. Compute `demand = population × trip rate` at the site's crossings and internal routes, compare against the capacity of the crossing and route widths actually provided (HB-02's flow-per-width figure and HB-01's density bands, applied through SA-14's Little's law), and treat a failing crossing as a massing constraint — not as a signal operation.
- Evidence: The measurement chain is owned by `spatial-analytics.md` (SA-12 flow/betweenness on the skeleton, SA-13 trip and desire-line deviation, SA-14 density via `N = Q × T`, SA-20 isochrone catchments) and the behavioural bands by `human-behavior.md` HB-01/HB-02; the site-level instruction to lay routes on desire lines and judge the site by walk-time to transit is SC-10. What this file adds is only the massing-side quantity: population comes from MU-10 (`units`/`keys` × occupancy; VT-* uses 1.6 occupants + 0.32 staff per key for the flagship case) and the crossing demand follows from it, so an option's `population` and its `movement_capacity_shortfall_tiles` must be published together.
- Source: corpus: SA-12/SA-13/SA-14/SA-20, HB-01/HB-02, SC-10, VT-* (occupancy per key). External numeric guidance for crossing capacity (a signal-cycle or HCM-style capacity table) was NOT opened this pass → **NEEDS VERIFICATION** before quoting a persons-per-cycle figure as standard.
- Class: FACT (the demand arithmetic) / STANDARD (co-reporting requirement)
- Scope: urban sites, transit-adjacent schemes, hotels with conference demand, any massing that concentrates arrivals
- Confidence: High for the method; Medium for the occupancy inputs (declared in VT-* as project assumptions); Low for cycle capacities
- Grid translation: `1 tile of queue ≈ 1 person` (VT-*'s convention); a crossing failure appears as a sustained `flow > capacity` edge in SA-12's strain map, and batch arrivals from MU-18's lifts appear as the same spike at lobby scale (MS-23's queue model). Emit `population`, `peak_arrival_per_min`, `crossing_strain`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: The demand is a *pulse*, not a rate: an average hourly figure hides the 07:30 breakfast and 18:00 dismissal peaks that HO-* and VT-* size for, and a crossing sized on the average fails twice a day. Second: a scheme whose only viable crossing is onto a road the authority classifies as vehicular — that is an MU-26 question, not a design detail. Failure symptom: a footway that is comfortable all day and impassable at two fixed hours.

### MU-21 Hold the ground floor to a measured active-frontage share, and the street wall to a measured percentage
- Rule: Two separate percentages, both measured on tiles: `active_frontage_share = frontage tiles with an active use and an opening ÷ total street-frontage tiles`, and `street_wall_share = frontage tiles within the permitted distance of the line ÷ total`. Set each target from the jurisdiction's own text where one exists, and from a declared project threshold where it does not; report both, never one.
- Evidence: Two different controls, routinely confused. The *street wall* is legal in some places: New York enforces it as a share of façade length within a defined distance of the street line with capped recesses (§23-431, recorded with its T1 citation in SC-21), and Canby's design-review scorecard scores "frontage contact" as façade within 5 tiles of the build-to line (SC-05's grid reading of that scorecard, T1). The *active* share is usually guidance rather than code: SC-12's rule is that a long blank perimeter is a liability on both sides of it, and the opened sources for it (National Design Guide, London Plan, Jacobs) set the requirement without a percentage. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** a published transparency or active-frontage percentage — the US form-based-code pages that would supply one (Dexter MI, Los Angeles) both returned 403 this pass — so no glass-ratio number is printed here, and the design guidance formerly carried in this corpus at 75-90 % was withdrawn as unverified.
- Source: NYC ZR §23-431 and the Canby scorecard as cited in `site-context.md` SC-21 and SC-05 — T1 there; corpus: SC-12 (blank frontage), HB-18 (supervision needs sightlines, not glass), SA-22 (aperture angle). No percentage is asserted by this file.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (the street-wall share, where a code states it) / STANDARD (the measurement) / NEEDS VERIFICATION (any transparency percentage)
- Scope: urban frontages, hotels on streets, mixed-use podiums
- Confidence: High for the two-metric split and for the §23-431 mechanism as recorded in SC-21; Low for any specific active-frontage target
- Grid translation: Frontage tiles are counted along each edge (SC-02's four-edge record); an opening is a `door` tile state, so `active` is testable only if the ground-floor use is tagged — emit `frontage_tiles`, `open_tiles_on_frontage`, `active_tiles_on_frontage`. A 60 m frontage (120 tiles) against a *declared* 75 % target requires 90 active tiles: put the target and its source in the option record.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Glass is not activity — a 24-tile glazed lobby behind a security desk reads active and supervises nothing (HB-18's visible-share test, SA-22's aperture angle). Failure symptom: `active_frontage_share = 1.0` with `open_tiles_on_frontage = 2`.

### MU-22 Give servicing its dimensions first, because it is the control that cannot be negotiated later
- Rule: Refuse storage, loading, and fire-appliance access occupy ground area inside the buildable envelope and are dimensionally fixed. Reserve them before allocating the podium, and report `servicing_area` against the coverage budget. Where a jurisdiction fixes buffers or clear widths, use its number and name it.
- Evidence: Published dimensional controls exist at exactly this scale: Singapore's handbook sets ancillary-structure setbacks of "5m from Category 1 road / 3m from Category 2-5 roads / 2m" and requires multi-storey car park perimeter walls at "6m (minimum)" with "4.5m (minimum)" on other elevations (URA setback handbook — T1, page updated 9 June 2026), i.e. the utilitarian elements are regulated separately from the building. The corpus already owns the vehicle-side arithmetic: SC-08 (stacking and berth counts as published by Toronto and Canby), SC-09 (IFC Appendix D apparatus access: reach distance, width with parking accounted for, radii, gradient), and `operations-maintenance.md` OM-* for the refuse and servicing logic.
- Source: URA Development Control Handbook, *Building Setback from Boundary* — https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/non-residential/commercial/building-setback/ — T1 (Singapore). Corpus: SC-07, SC-08, SC-09 (each with its own jurisdiction-stamped citations), OM-*.
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (each published figure, stamped) / FACT (the reservation arithmetic)
- Scope: all types; hotels and hospitals hardest, because their refuse, laundry and delivery loads scale with keys and beds (HO-*, SV-*)
- Confidence: High for the quoted Singapore figures as published; Low for transfer; High for the "reserve first" ordering
- Grid translation: Reserve whole tile rectangles per element (`loading_bay`, `refuse_store`, `apparatus_route`) with the route width taken from SC-09's figure and the turning swept path measured on the grid; then the coverage check becomes `building_footprint + servicing_pads ≤ coverage_area_tiles`. A fire lane is a `walkable`-excluded, `blocked`-for-building tile set with a width test — encode it as a hard mask, not a note.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Basements move the problem rather than removing it: the ramp, the headroom and the extract for a basement car park all consume surface area and volume (ST-*, SV-*), and a lift-out refuse system is a maintenance commitment (OM-*). Failure symptom: a 70 % coverage podium with 15 % of it unbuildable because the truck court was never subtracted.

### MU-23 Provide private amenable space as an area per dwelling with a solar test, and say which denominator you used
- Rule: Amenity space is `A_min(m²) per dwelling` (plus any per-resident increment), sited and sized, then tested for solar access against the same sun arithmetic as everything else (EQ-05/EQ-07). Publish it as three fields — total area, minimum per unit, and the share that passes the sun test — because an area-only figure hides shaded balconies.
- Evidence: The *internal* minimum is verified for one jurisdiction and is the number that bounds the yield: Singapore requires that "all self-contained DUs island-wide shall be at least 35sqm nett in internal area" (URA dwelling-unit guidelines — T1, page updated 7 June 2026). The *outdoor* minima for other systems were not reachable this pass: the English private-amenity-space and London-plan open-space figures, and the Australian `ADG` amenity-space minima (PISP/balcony areas and the "≥ 50 % of habitable rooms get 2 hours of sun between 09:00 and 15:00 on 21 June" family of controls), exist as published rules but the NSW document returned only undecodable PDF streams. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** obtain the operative text for each before any figure is printed. The solar geometry needed to run the test is fully in-corpus (EQ-05 shadow factor, EQ-07 gap formula, EQ-09 sun-hours per orientation).
- Source: URA dwelling-unit guidelines — https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/residential/flats-condominiums/maximum-number-du/ — T1 (Singapore); corpus: EQ-05/EQ-07/EQ-09, SC-16, `architecture-theory.md` TH-24 (published minimum room dimensions per room type, England's nationally described space standard, stamped there). Blocked this pass: NSW *Apartment Design Guide* PDFs (`https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-3-siting-the-development.pdf` — FlateDecode streams, no text extracted).
- Class: CODE REQUIREMENT (the 35 m² floor, Singapore) / NEEDS VERIFICATION (outdoor minima in other jurisdictions)
- Scope: residential and hospitality massing; any scheme claiming amenity space
- Confidence: High for the quoted internal minimum; Low for unsourced outdoor areas — none is printed
- Grid translation: Balconies and terraces are `walkable` tiles on their floor with an `amenity` tag; the sun test is `sun_hours(face) > 0` at the declared date via EQ-09 plus the obstruction angle test via EQ-08 against the neighbouring receiver. Report `amenity_tiles_per_dwelling` and `amenity_sunlit_share`.
- Exceptions / failure mode: A podium deck is only amenity if the instrument counts it (MU-08's `deck_counts_as_open`) and if it passes wind and overlook tests (SC-19, HB-18). Failure symptom: 1.0 amenity space per dwelling on paper, all of it shaded by the tower's own 12th floor.

### MU-24 Budget public open space per capita from the population the massing creates
- Rule: Take the population from MU-10/MU-20, multiply by the applicable per-capita standard, compare against existing provision within the jurisdiction's distance threshold, and carry the deficit as a site requirement (land, deck, or contribution). Publish the distance threshold as an input, never as an assumption.
- Evidence: The opened public-health literature states the WHO line as "a minimum of 9 m2 of green space per individual with an ideal UGS value of 50 m2 per capita" (WHO 2012, quoted in Russo & Cirella, *IJERPH* 15(10), 2018 — T2, secondary; the WHO original was not opened → **NEEDS VERIFICATION** for the wording and for the distance/time limb that is commonly cited alongside it and that this file therefore does not print). The corpus's own qualitative counterparts are SC-18 (soft-surface budget), SC-12 (the edge that overlooks public space) and HB-18 (supervision measured, not asserted).
- Source: Russo, A.M. & Cirella, G.S., "Modern Compact Cities: How Much Greenery Do We Need?", *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health* 15(10):2190, 2018 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6209905/ — T2 (open access, full text opened; reports WHO 2012). Corpus: SC-04, SC-18, SA-20 (isochrone recipe for the distance test).
- Class: STANDARD (per-capita method) / NEEDS VERIFICATION (the primary source's exact wording)
- Scope: masterplans and any scheme adding population within a district with fixed provision
- Confidence: Medium (single opened secondary, uncontested figure)
- Grid translation: `9 m² = 36 tiles` per person, `50 m² = 200 tiles` per person; for 300 residents the minimum is 2,700 m² = 10 800 tiles — on the 60 × 45 m worked plot that is exactly the whole site, which is the arithmetic reason per-capita standards are met off-site or through a district pool. Use SA-20's isochrone to find the receiving pool before claiming a deficit.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Per-capita arithmetic double-counts when a city's total is compared with a site's own population without a catchment definition; and "green" that is a road verge is not amenity (SC-18's material classes). Failure symptom: a "surplus" that no resident can reach in the stated time.

### MU-25 Read slope and levels as a cut/fill balance and a deck-cost signal, before the massing is fixed
- Rule: Compute `V_cut − V_fill` from the site grid and the proposed finished levels, keep the balance near zero, and price the residual as import/export hauls. Then treat every podium and suspended deck as an area that is simultaneously buildable, unloadable and expensive — flag it for the structural and cost files rather than absorbing it silently.
- Evidence: The slope-class method and the accessible-route check are already owned by SC-17 (with Saratoga Springs' hillside bands and the ADA slope limits, T1, jurisdiction-flagged), so only the earthworks arithmetic is new here: `V = Σ (d_cut − d_fill) × cell_area`, with cell area `1 tile = 0.25 m²` at the model resolution and a swell/shrink factor applied to the *excavated* volume, which is the term most often omitted. Structural consequences are ST-*'s (grid continuity, transfer, loading over a deck) and cost consequences are EC-*/CN-*'s; this rule only requires that the massing decision record which decks it created.
- Source: corpus: SC-17 (slope bands and accessible-route budget, with its T1 citations), `structural-sizing.md` ST-*, `construction.md` CN-*, `economics.md` EC-*. External: swell/haul factors — **HEURISTIC**, no opened source; obtain a published earthworks estimating table (a road-authority or agency manual) before quoting a percentage.
- Class: FACT (the balance equation) / HEURISTIC (the cost signal) / CODE REQUIREMENT (the slope bands, as cited in SC-17)
- Scope: sloping sites, podium decks over basements, split-level massing
- Confidence: High for the arithmetic; Medium for SC-17's transferred bands (recorded there); Low for any numeric cost delta
- Grid translation: Give each tile a level `z_tile` (site) and `z_finish`; `cut_fill_m3 = Σ (z_finish − z_tile) × 0.25`. Report `balance_m3`, `max_cut_m`, `max_fill_m`, `deck_area_tiles`. A deck over a basement is `blocked-for-building` on the ground layer and a normal plate above — two records, not one.
- Exceptions / failure mode: Balancing in place ignores the structural and drainage consequences of a 3 m cut at one boundary (retained earth, SC-18's drainage budget, SC-19's wind at the changed levels). Failure symptom: a "balanced" scheme with a 4 m retaining wall nobody costed and a courtyard that now drains uphill.

### MU-26 Enumerate massing options on the grid by intersecting envelopes, and log which question belongs to the authority
- Rule: The generator is a bounded search, not a guess: (1) build the per-edge setback mask; (2) build the plane set; (3) for each floor `n` compute `mask(n) = envelope ∩ ⋂ planes(n)`; (4) choose a plate from MU-12's cap and MU-11's depth bound; (5) place cores by MS-04's single-core start and MU-19's bound; (6) compute the five MU-01 ceilings plus MU-16's index; (7) rank options on the published vector, never on one metric (SA-28's rule at plan scale). Anything the site data cannot answer is emitted as a *question with the specific clause named*, not as a default value.
- Evidence: The corpus already fixes the neighbouring machinery this composes — SA-28 (never rank on one metric), MS-04/MS-19 (core placement and ratio), GT-24 (efficiency after quantisation), `layout-algorithms.md` LA-* (solvers, unsat diagnosis, selection-by-input-contract), and VA-*/`uncertainty.md` UN-* (three-valued verdicts, what to refuse to assert). What is specific here is the *envelope algebra*: a plane is a per-floor inequality, so the whole legal envelope is computable from four integers per edge, and the answer is either an optimum or a provably empty set.
- Source: corpus: SA-28, MS-04, MS-19, GT-24, LA-*, VA-*, UN-*; statutory structure of the plane/FAR pairing as cited in MU-07 (NYC §23-73, §23-732 — T1).
- Class: STANDARD (procedure) / FACT (the intersection algebra)
- Scope: all site generation on this host; the file's own integration rule
- Confidence: High
- Grid translation: Metric keys, one record per option: `site_tiles`, `far`, `gfa_max_tiles`, `coverage_max_tiles`, `envelope_tiles`, `height_m`, `floor_to_floor_m`, `storeys`, `plate_tiles`, `flat_capacity_tiles`, `core_tiles`, `net_to_gross`, `gfa_delivered_tiles`, `stranded_far_tiles`, `binding_constraint`, `yield_index`, `programme_index`, `units_or_keys`, `separation_min_m`, `open_share`, `active_frontage_share`, `servicing_area_tiles`, `sun_hours_site`, `daylit_share`, `population`, `movement_strain`. Complexity: plane masks are `O(floors × edges)` and plate search is a rectangle scan, so the full enumeration is trivial at flagship scale (a 40 × 60 m site, 25 floors, 4 edges = 2 500 tile-tests).
- Exceptions / failure mode: Two opposite errors: treating an unverified statutory number as a design input (the "1.5 × height" spacing style of folk rule — MU-06's warning), and refusing to compute at all because the instrument is not on file. Compute with the assumption *named and tagged*, then emit the clause to verify. Failure symptom: a ranked option set where every option assumed a different floor-to-floor, a different net-to-gross and a different height datum.

## Worked site-yield example

**Site:** 40 m × 60 m = 2 400 m² (`80 × 120 tiles = 9 600 tiles`), two street frontages (40 m north, 60 m east),
one shared boundary west, one low-rise neighbour south. **Programme:** 250-key hotel plus a retail podium.
**Stated envelope — assumed for the exercise and stamped as such:** `FAR 7.5` → 18 000 m² permissible;
`coverage 0.70` → 1 680 m²; `height 70 m`; setbacks: build-to line on both street frontages, 3 m west, 6 m
south; floor-to-floor 3.6 m for guest rooms (7.2 tiles) and 4.8 m for the podium (9.6 tiles). The magnitudes
are drawn from the instruments cited in this file (Singapore's 80 %-class commercial coverage and
multi-hundred-percent plot ratios, sources 1-3 and 11; New York's 80/100 % coverage and height-factor system,
sources 6-7). None of them is this site's actual rule.

| Test | Computation | Result | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Footprint envelope | `40 × (60 − 6) = 2 160` m² before coverage | 2 160 m² | coverage binds instead, at 1 680 m² |
| Podium plate | `= coverage cap` | 1 680 m² = 6 720 tiles (35 m × 48 m) | ok |
| Height stack | podium `2 × 4.8 = 9.6` m; tower `70 − 9.6 = 60.4` m; `floor(60.4 ÷ 3.6) = 16` | 16 tower storeys, top at 67.2 m, 2.8 m roof allowance | ok |
| Tower plate cap | MU-12, plate ≤ 700 m² (2 800 tiles, 20 m × 35 m) | 700 m² | ok |
| Delivered GFA | `2 × 1 680 + 16 × 700` | 14 560 m² → **FAR 6.07** | 3 440 m² (19.1 %) of the rights unused |
| Keys delivered | `16 × 700 ÷ 70 m² per key` (HO-04's 68-72 band) | **160 keys** | 90 keys short of the programme |
| Programme need | `250 × 70 + 2 × 1 680` | 20 860 m² | 2 860 m² (16 %) over the FAR as well |
| `yield_index` | `18 000 ÷ (1 680 × 18)` | 0.60 | the flat-plate stack has more capacity than the FAR allows: the rights bind first, the plate cap then strands what the height cap does not |
| `programme_index` | `20 860 ÷ 18 000` | 1.16 | the brief exceeds the rights before any geometry is blamed (MU-16) |

Four levers were tested against that failure, and only two survive as design options; the other two are
decisions somebody else owns:

1. **Raise the tower.** 250 keys at a 700 m² plate need `17 500 ÷ 700 = 25` storeys → `9.6 + 90 = 99.6` m
   (+42 % height) *and* 20 860 m² → `FAR 8.69` (+16 %). Both reliefs, or neither: a height increase alone
   strands the FAR, and a FAR increase alone has nowhere to go. This is the option to put to the authority,
   with both numbers named (MU-26's compute-versus-ask split).
2. **Widen the tower plate.** 250 keys in 16 storeys needs `17 500 ÷ 16 = 1 094 m²` — 56 % over the plate cap.
   EQ-12's arithmetic then puts the interior band beyond the 3.15 m daylight reach, and MS-04's travel and
   queue bound forces a second core, which MS-19's ratio and MU-19's units-per-floor cap both reject. Rejected
   on computation, not on taste.
3. **Shrink the key.** `11 200 ÷ 250 = 44.8 m²` of GFA per key, below HO-04's 68-72 m² band — a different
   operating model (HO-04, EC-19), not a different massing. Rejected unless the brief changes.
4. **Build what the envelope permits.** 160 keys over a two-level podium at 67.2 m and FAR 6.07: coverage 70 %
   ok, envelope ok, height ok, FAR ok — with `stranded_far_tiles = 13 760`,
   `binding_constraint = far × plate_cap` (height only becomes binding once the plate cap is lifted) reported as the finding.

Carry-through checks on the chosen option: the 67 m tower lays `67 × cot(26.55°) ≈ 134 m` of shadow at 21 Dec
noon at 40°N (EQ-05/EQ-07) toward the southern neighbour, so MU-06's separation test and SC-16's protected-receiver
question bind before any podium amendment; servicing (MU-22) has to sit inside the 1 680 m² podium, and the
loading, refuse and apparatus pads are subtracted from the lettable podium plate, not from the tower; and
MU-19's core bound with VT-*'s handling-capacity arithmetic must be re-run for 160 keys rather than the 250-key
case VT-* works elsewhere in this corpus.

## Weak or contested

- **Say "consistent with the cited rule", never "compliant".** Every number here is jurisdiction- and edition-stamped, and zoning text amends continuously: the New York sections were read as rendered in 2026, the Singapore handbook pages carry their own update dates, and the German text is the consolidated version. A design checked against this file is consistent with what was opened; compliance requires the site's own district schedule (VA-04's verdict discipline, and the standing instruction in `AGENTS.md` that a claim names its source). **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** no instrument here was checked against a real lot's zone and overlays.
- **New York's sky-exposure geometry is named but not quantified.** The section structure (§23-43, §23-73 family) and the FAR/open-space-ratio mechanism are confirmed, but the plane base heights and slope ratios did not render and §23-732's FAR column came through garbled (only the OSR increments are quoted, and they are quoted as increments). **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** §23-431…§23-433 and §23-732…§23-735 in the official print text — base height above the street line, slope ratio per district, and the FAR/OSR pairs. MU-07 is written as an algorithm precisely so that it stays usable and jurisdiction-neutral until then.
- **All Japanese figures in this file are T4.** Zone BCR/FAR ranges, the "1:1.25" road diagonal, the 10/12 m bands and the winter-solstice shadow rule come from one practitioner page; three attempts to reach the Building Standards Act text and an official municipal English page failed (source 18). **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** Articles 55, 56, 56-2 in the official translation plus the zone tables, before any Japanese coefficient is used as a rule. The north-side diagonal coefficient commonly quoted (1.2/1.5) is deliberately absent from this file because it could not be opened.
- **No verified density-band table.** The London Plan's PTAM-based optimal/acceptable ranges, the English 40-dwellings-per-hectare convention and any non-UK equivalent were all unreachable, so this file publishes only *computed* densities from its own worked plots (333 units/ha gross on the 60 × 45 m plot) and no external band. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** London Plan Policy D4 and its density table in the current edition, plus one Australasian and one continental-European instrument, before a dph band is used to prefer a massing.
- **Open-space and amenity minima are thin, and the file says so where they are.** Verified: Singapore's 35 m² internal DU floor and its 20 % size-class shares (T1), the WHO per-capita 9 m² / ideal 50 m² pair (T2, second-hand), Singapore's 2 m planting verge (T1). Unverified and therefore *not printed*: outdoor private-amenity areas per dwelling, NSW's solar-access-to-habitable-rooms and PISP percentages, England's play-and-informal-recreation figures, and WHO's distance/time limb. MU-08, MU-23 and MU-24 are written as methods with that gap named in place.
- **Cores: method, not guidance bands.** MU-19 prints no units-per-core number because no official one was readable (source 15) and because the corpus already flags its own MS-04 thresholds as `UNCITED — heuristic`. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** the single-exit-per-storey provisions of the adopted egress code (the IBC §1006.3 family) and any published units-per-core or stair-count guidance. Until then, treat "N flats per core" figures in circulation as folklore and re-derive from travel distance and handling capacity.
- **Block length has no verified number in this corpus or in this file.** SC-11 states the constraint qualitatively (read the inherited block dimensions and junction frequency; a large parcel argues for new streets, not a superblock) and SA-21 owns the indices that measure the consequence, but SA-21 itself marks its interpretation bands NEEDS VERIFICATION and the external sources that would carry a maximum block length were unreachable (FHWA/CNU, TfL, National Model Design Code — see **Sources** items 12, 13, 17). MU-13 therefore prints a method and no figure. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** a published block-length or junction-spacing standard with its authority, before any length test is used to reject a massing.
- **`yield_index` and `programme_index` (MU-16) are this file's constructions.** They are arithmetic over published controls, but no source validates them as discriminators; they rank and they localise the binding constraint, they do not decide the typology, and they are silent on shape penalties such as over-depth.
- **Efficiency figures are borrowed on purpose.** The 0.78 net-to-gross in both calculators is a *declared input* standing in for EC-06's curve; the corpus already records that the ratio depends on the measurement convention (EC-01/EC-02: GIA vs net vs rentable) and that its own circulation cut-lines are heuristics (FL-01, GT-24). Nothing here changes an efficiency band — the point is what a band costs in stranded GFA.
- **Hotel yield inherits HO-04's band.** 70 m² of GFA per key is the flagship figure from `hotel-operating-standards.md`, stamped there with its own sources; if that band moves, the worked example's 160-key answer moves with it. Recompute, never scale (the same caution `coverage-map.md` §4 applies to cross-file conflicts).
- **Jurisdictions are mixed inside single rules, and each term is labelled.** The buffers in MU-22 are Singapore's, the coverage caps in MU-04 are New York's, the orientation values in MU-01 are Germany's; the per-capita figure in MU-24 comes through a public-health paper. Combining them in one computation is legitimate only as an *illustration of magnitude*, and MU-26's last clause exists to enforce that.

## Type-specificity audit

- **Multi-floor hotel (flagship).** Load-bearing rules: MU-01/MU-09 (which ceiling actually binds the stack), MU-15 (podium-plus-tower is the default urban hotel form), MU-10 (keys from GFA via HO-04's band), MU-12/MU-19 (plate and core bound the storey count), MU-22 (refuse, laundry, loading and apparatus scale with keys and covers), MU-20 (breakfast and banquet pulses, not average rates), MU-18 (no 16-storey stack is lift-exempt). Type-neutral: MU-02, MU-04, MU-06, MU-07, MU-26. The worked example is a hotel because a hotel states its programme in m² per unit and therefore fails an envelope loudly rather than quietly.
- **Office and hybrid plates.** MU-17 (plate depth as a conversion asset), MU-16 (index-ranked options) and MU-02 (mechanical-floor and corridor exemptions decide the lettable yield) dominate; MU-11/MU-13's daylight depth bound is weaker because artificially lit depth is accepted (EQ-12's own exclusion), so the binding numbers migrate to MU-18/MU-19's cores and VT-*'s banks.
- **Residential towers and perimeter blocks.** MU-10's size-mix rules and MU-11's depth and courtyard arithmetic decide feasibility before anything aesthetic; MU-06's separation is the value-preserving rule, MU-23/MU-24 the amenity ones, and MU-10's gross-versus-net density discipline is where most residential comparisons quietly fail.
- **Hospitals, campuses and resorts (multi-building sites).** MU-06 (pairwise separation per facing pair), MU-04 (per-phase envelope headroom, with SC-22's phasing), MU-25 (levels, decks and retaining) and MU-22 (ambulance, service and refuse circuits) bind; MU-12's plate cap is often void because ward plates are process-set — record that as an explicit exclusion rather than importing a tower rule.
- **Retail, industrial and single-storey.** The FAR-to-footprint collapse empties MU-01's storey and height slots: coverage and servicing are the whole game (MU-04, MU-22), and MU-11/MU-17's daylight bounds do not apply (EQ-12's process-depth exclusion). Density in MU-10/MU-24's sense is normally replaced by parking and loading ratios, which this file deliberately does not cover (SC-07/SC-08 own them).
- **Schools, civic and low-rise urban fill.** MU-16's low-index regime: the site often cannot consume its own rights, so `stranded_far_tiles` is the headline number and MU-14's stepping is the design tool; MU-24's per-capita field then dominates the site budget because sports and play areas exceed the surplus.
- **Site and masterplan generation.** MU-26's enumeration with MU-19's network and MU-20's movement; MU-13's block length and MU-08's open-space residual are the site-form rules, and the measurement of all three is handed to SA-20/SA-21 rather than recomputed here.
