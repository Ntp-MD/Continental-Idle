Every great city hides something beneath its surface.

In the shadows, a network operates on gold coins and blood debts. The Continental hotels stand as neutral ground — sanctuaries where assassins can rest, where business is forbidden, where a single rule keeps the underworld from tearing itself apart: no killing on Continental grounds.

Every major city has its Continental. New York. Rome. Casablanca. Bangkok. Each branch answers to the High Table — the twelve seats of power that decide who lives and who dies. Gold coins change hands across borders. Markers are honored in every language. The network binds them all.

Each branch fights to expand its influence, vying for the rare honor of being chosen to join the High Table — a council that rules the underworld from above.

Far from all of it, in southern Thailand, you were raised by a middle-class family since childhood. You had no memories of your mother or father. You didn't know who they were or where they came from. You lived an ordinary life, day after day, until

Then, one day, the sound of an engine that didn't belong.

A caravan of black cars was parked along the narrow dirt road. Windows tinted, paint too clean for the dust they had just driven through. No one on this stretch of coast owned cars like that.

Stepping outside, there they stood — a line of men in tailored suits, already in position, as if they'd rehearsed the arrival. Not threatening. Something quieter than that. Composed. Deliberate. Waiting.

One of them stepped forward.

"Greetings, young master. My name is Natthapon. Most people call me Mod. I am here to inform you of your father's passing."

The truth came plainly, piece by piece — the way someone delivers news they've rehearsed too many times to soften anymore.

The relatives who raised you were never an accident. Your father chose them to protect you — to keep you away from the danger of his world, to ensure you never had to carry the weight of the assassin's life. His enemies never knew you existed. That was why you were safe. He was the owner of {branchTerm}, a global network the underworld itself answered to. Assassins. Fixers. Rival bosses. Blood debts spanning decades. All of it existed under his authority — and none of it was ever allowed to reach you.

But he didn't survive the war. He was assassinated — a casualty of the power struggle that rose to the High Table.

Your father had people watching from a distance since the beginning — always close enough to keep you safe, never close enough to be seen. You never knew they were there. That was the point.

That distance was the whole point. The empty space where two parents should have been wasn't an accident of age. It was a wall your father built on purpose, one that held for your entire childhood.

And now it was gone. Killed in the same war it was built to keep out.

Mod hadn't come to deliver grief alone. He'd come with an inheritance — {branchTerm}, its resources, its alliances, its enemies, and the authority tied to all of it, waiting for someone to claim it.

Your father's people are still loyal. They won't bow to anyone outside his bloodline — not without a fight. If you refuse, they'll fracture, or be swept aside by whoever takes the throne. And the seat won't stay empty for long. In this world, power never leaves a vacuum. Someone will fill it — the rightful heir, or whoever moves fastest to take it by force.

Mod would stay. Guide. Protect. Teach everything the quiet years by the sea never prepared you for.

But the choice — step into a world you never knew was yours, or turn back and shut the door on all of it — had to be made alone.

The suits were still waiting for an answer.

You looked at the line of black cars, then back at the sea. The answer came before you could speak.
